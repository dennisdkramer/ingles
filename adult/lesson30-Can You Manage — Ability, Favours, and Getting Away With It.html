Can You Manage — Ability, Favours, and Getting Away With It

Four words decide most of what you get from people here: can, could, be able to, and manage to. This page puts them in one night — Dana's door, a dead car, a parking sticker, and a shift nobody else will cover.

1 · The favour across the hall

5:41, and Dana is at the door

  1. DanaCould you keep the music down? I'm trying to f---in' sleep.
  2. NicoMy bad. Yeah, no problem.
  3. DanaAnd can you take the trash down when you leave?
  4. NicoI can't tonight. I'm already late for the shift.
  5. DanaCan you manage the whole night on four hours of sleep?
  6. NicoDone it before. I'll be fine.

Two openers, two distances

Could you… is the ask that leaves the other person room to say no. It is not the past here — it is the same favour, said from one step back.

Can you… is the straight one: a neighbour, a roommate, somebody already on their feet.

Dana uses both in twenty seconds, and Nico answers each one the way it was asked.

2 · Four ways to say you can

One ability, four doors

Same idea, four forms — pick the one the tense allows
FormWhat it coversIn a line
canright now, and what you can do in general I can drive. I can't tonight.
couldwhat you could do back then, and the softer ask I could swim at six. Could you take it down?
be able toevery tense can has no form for I'll be able to make it. I haven't been able to sleep.
manage toone occasion that nearly didn't happen I managed to get a spot.
There is no will can, no to can and no have could. Where the sentence already carries a tense word, be able to goes in its place.

Why a fourth one exists

Can carries its own tense and refuses to wear a second: no to, no -ing, no will, no have. Every tense it can't wear gets pushed onto be able to.

Manage to is the honest one. It means the thing happened and something was in the way — the car, the lock, the deadline.

O poder do português cobre quase tudo isso de uma vez. É por isso que a frase parece certa na sua cabeça e sai errada em inglês.
