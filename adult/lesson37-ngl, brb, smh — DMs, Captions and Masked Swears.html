ngl, brb, smh — DMs, Captions and Masked Swears

Half of your English now arrives in five letters. This is how to read it and how to answer it.

The five-letter set

What the letters actually say

Each one is a whole sentence you already know how to build

Text language is not a code you memorize; it is the same English with the vowels pulled out and the politeness removed. Learn what each abbreviation replaces, and you can always expand it back into a full sentence — which is exactly what this lesson trains.

The core set, and the sentence behind it
Text Full English When it is used
idk I don't know. genuinely no answer — and no apology for it
ngl Not gonna lie. before an honest opinion that might sting
tbh To be honest. softens a judgment: "tbh I'd skip it"
brb Be right back. minutes, not hours — you are coming back
omw On my way. you left, or you are about to lie about leaving
wyd What you doing? the opener with no greeting at all
smh Shaking my head. disappointment, no words needed
wtf What the f---? shock, and already a masked swear
lol / lmao laughing out loud / laughing my ass off a tone marker, often with no laugh behind it
"ngl that's rough, lol, smh." — three abbreviations, two emotions, and a full reaction. Nobody writes "I am shaking my head" in a DM.

Three real messages

Read each one in the tone it was sent

  1. "omw — brb, tbh idk yet." — I left, I am back in a minute, and I honestly do not have the answer.
  2. "wyd? wtf happened lmao." — what are you doing, what the hell happened, and I am laughing at it.
  3. "this shi- again? smh, ngl I'm done." — the same problem again, head in hands, and I am telling the truth about how done I am.

Tone lives in the punctuation

The same four letters, five moods

In a text, how you type is what you mean

caps is volume: "WHERE ARE YOU" is a shout, and a friendly one only if you already have that joke together. A period at the end is the cold one: "ok." lands like a door closing, while "ok" is fine and "okayyy" is warm. k alone is not an abbreviation for anything — it is a declaration of war.

No punctuation and all lowercase is chill: "see u there" says we are close enough to skip the grammar. Three dots are passive aggression: "sure..." means it is not sure at all.

Emoji are tone, not decoration: 😂 softens a roast, 💀 marks something as fatal and funny, 🙃 means "I am done". And lol is a tone marker more than a laugh — people write "lol" when they did not laugh, and everyone knows it.

One practical rule: match the other person's temperature. If they write three words, do not answer with a paragraph. If they send a voice note, do not send a form letter.

How a DM actually runs

No greeting, no goodbye, no full sentences

Text between friends is logistics plus noise, and the grammar is optional

A DM between people who know each other has no greeting. It opens with the subject: wyd, u up, u free tonight. The reply is a fragment: nada, chilling, working till 8. Then the plan arrives in pieces: 8? — 9 better — omw.

Reply speed is a message of its own. Answering in two minutes says you are free and interested. Answering six hours later says you were busy, and "sorry, just saw this" is the lie everybody accepts, including when it is obviously a lie. Leaving somebody on read is an answer too: it means the message did not require one, or that you are annoyed.

Double texting is fine once — a second "you there?" after an hour is normal. The third is panic. And if the other side sends a voice note, they have decided that typing is too slow; reply in kind or keep it short, never with an essay.

Two openings you will meet constantly: wyd (what are you doing, friendly, no agenda) and u good? (are you okay, and it usually means somebody heard something). Both expect two or three words, not a report.

Masked swears

One asterisk protects the writer, never the reader

People mask for three reasons: an app's filter, a screenshot that may travel, and a group chat with a coworker in it. The standard masks are f*ck, sh!t, b!tch, f---, and the abbreviations that are already masks of themselves: wtf, stfu, af. Platforms also push their own words — "unalive" for kill, "grape" for rape — because a filter is cheaper to satisfy than an argument.

Where does it belong? With close friends, typing the word is normal and masking can read as cold or ironic. In a work chat, a family group or a public caption, mask it or drop it. A regra prática: a máscara protege quem escreveu, não quem lê — se a mensagem pode ser printada, mascare.

What masking never does is change the meaning. "this shi- again?" is the same sentence as "this shit again?", and both carry the same tiredness. The mask is a social instrument, not a different word.

Type the letters

Say it in the shortest form

Each line gives you the meaning. Type the abbreviation a native would send.

  1. — that's rough, and I'm not gonna lie about it.
  2. I don't know yet, — I'll text you when I do.
  3. ? Are you home or out?
  4. He did it again. I can't even argue anymore.
  5. — I just left the house, ten minutes.
  6. Hold on, — somebody's at the door.
  7. — I'd skip that party, it's gonna be dead.

Notice what the abbreviations replace: a full clause with a subject and a verb. Nothing about the grammar changed — it was compressed, and you can always expand it back.

Read the temperature

One message, one mood. Pick the right reading.

1She answered your long message with: "k"
2He wrote "ok." — with the period, and nothing else.
3She sent "sure..." about the plan you just made.
4You are about to send a swear word into the work group chat.

Send one, expand one

Compress, then decompress

First: turn this paragraph into a text you would actually send a friend. Second: take a real text thread and write out what it means in full sentences.

1. I am on my way. I will be right back, I just need to grab my keys. To be honest I don't know whether we can still get a table at nine.

2. wyd? omw, brb. smh, ngl this shi- again.

Model answers

1. "omw — brb, tbh idk yet." Six words, and every piece of information survives: you left, you are coming back in a second, and you are not sure about the table.

2. "What are you doing? I'm on my way, be right back. Shaking my head — not gonna lie, this shi- again." Same content, the tone of a person who is tired of the same problem.

Both directions matter. Compressing makes you sound like a person; expanding makes you understand one.
