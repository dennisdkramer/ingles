Gotta, Should, Better — Who's Forcing Whom

Four words before the verb, four different amounts of force — and one of them is a threat.

The pressure dial

Where is the push coming from?

One slot, four words, four amounts of force

Lesson 30 put a word in the slot before the verb: can. This lesson puts four more there, and the only question that matters is who is pushing. A boss, a law, your own mouth, or a threat — the word you pick tells the listener which one it is before they finish the sentence.

Portuguese is where this goes wrong. Dever covers both must and should, so a Brazilian reaches for must when they mean should and lands like a cop giving an order at a barbecue. In American speech must is rare in the mouth and loud when it appears — it belongs on a form, a sign, or a boss who wants to sound like a form.

have to / gotta — the push is outside me: my schedule, my boss, the law. I am not giving an order, I am reporting a fact about my life. 🔊 "I have to work Saturday — no choice."

must — the push is the speaker's own authority, or a printed rule. "You must wear a helmet." Nothing softens it, which is why nobody says it to a friend.

should / oughta — advice with no force behind it. The listener can say no and nothing happens. "You should text her."

had better / you'd better — advice with a threat under the floorboards. "You'd better be there" = be there, or deal with what comes after.

The dial in one line: should (I care) → have to (life says so) → must (I say so) → better (or else).

The heavier the form, the smaller the circle of people who are allowed to use it on you.

The negative that flips

Don't have to is not mustn't

The two words look symmetrical and mean opposite things

don't have to — not necessary

"You don't have to come." Fica à vontade, ninguém vai te obrigar.

A door left open. You may stay home and nothing happens to you.

🔊 You don't have to come tonight.

mustn't — forbidden

"You mustn't come." Não venha — é proibido.

A wall. If you come, something happens to you.

🔊 You mustn't come in here. (a sign on a door)

In the street, mustn't is almost always replaced by can't or not allowed to, which carry the same wall in normal words: "You can't park there." Keep mustn't for signs, forms and written rules; say can't out loud. And hear the difference in the negative: don't gotta is common in speech for don't have to ("You don't gotta pay me back"), but mustn't has no slang shape at all.

The excuse family

Supposed to, in charge of, hands are tied

Three phrases that make "not my fault" sound like a fact

This is the vocabulary of the shift, the family and the group chat. Each item does one job: it points at who was expected, who owns it, or why the decision is not mine.

The seven items, in the sentence they live in
ItemWhat it doesSay it like this
supposed towhat was expected or arranged — and often did not happen"I was supposed to close, but nobody told me."
in charge ofthe person who owns the job"You're supposed to be in charge of this shi- — gotcha."
my hands are tiedI cannot change it: the decision is above me"My hands are tied, I've got to; I'm better off saying nothing."
better offthe wiser of two futures, even when it costs something"You're better off telling her now."
have got to — have to, spelled out, a little heavier "I've got to finish this tonight."
no choice — zero options, delivered deadpan, usually with a shrug "I gotta f---in' go, I have to — no choice."
gotchaI understand, or I caught you — tone decides"Gotcha. I'll handle it."
Supposed to looks backward at who was meant to do it; in charge of looks at who owns it now. Mixing them is how a worker starts sounding like a customer.

Better is a warning

Oughta, better, and the threat under the sentence

Advice has a volume knob, and "better" is the top of it

Oughta is ought to said at speed — advice, one notch softer than should, and it keeps the to: "You oughta tell her." You hear it from someone older, or from a friend being gentle with you.

Better is the same slot with the threat switched on. "You'd better tell her" means tell her, or deal with what comes after. A warning you give a friend before a party is usually better; the same warning to your boss is not a sentence you get to finish.

Better off weighs two futures and picks the wiser: "You're better off telling her now" = the other way costs more later. Same word, different job — look at what follows: better off takes -ing or a noun, better takes the bare verb.

Four lines at a kitchen table

Read the pressure in each turn before the note under it

  1. Duda You oughta tell her, you should — you'd f---in' better.
  2. Caio I know, I know. I'm gonna tell her tonight.
  3. Duda You'd better. She's your sister, not a client.
  4. Caio Fine. But if she yells at me, that's on you.

Duda climbs the whole dial inside one line: oughta is care, should is advice, you'd better is the threat. Then she repeats the threat alone in turn three — that is how a warning gets louder without a new word.

Which pressure does the scene demand?

Three people, three situations. One answer each.

1Your buddy is about to text his ex at two in the morning. Nobody is forcing anything; you are giving advice he can ignore.
2The sign on the machine says it, and the company fires people over it.
3Your place, your party. You want the guest to relax about the food.

Your own week

Four pressures, written by you

Write four real lines about your week: one with have to / gotta (no choice), one with a rule (must on a sign or a form), one piece of advice for a friend (should / oughta), and one warning (you'd better). Do not explain them — write them the way you would say them.

Model answer — check the pressure, not the words

1. I gotta work Saturday — no choice, my boss put me on the schedule.

2. The sign says you must show your badge at the door.

3. You oughta text her first; she's been waiting all week.

4. You'd better be there by nine, or the manager locks the door.

If your four lines put the pressures in that order, you own the dial. Lines 1 and 4 cannot swap: line 1 is a fact about your life, line 4 is a threat with a time on it.
