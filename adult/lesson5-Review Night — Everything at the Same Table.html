Noite de revisão. Nada novo entra hoje: as quatro lições anteriores sentam na mesma mesa. Cada seção usa um veículo diferente daquele da lição original — porque reconhecer uma palavra numa tabela e reconhecê-la numa história são habilidades distintas, e só a segunda sobrevive na rua.

1. A história das onze e meia

Leia sem consultar nada. As palavras sublinhadas em negrito já são suas — se alguma travar, o problema é de reconhecimento, não de vocabulário, e é isso que a noite de hoje conserta.

Friday, 11:30 p.m. Bia is waiting outside the restaurant. She’s tired, she’s broke, and she’s hungry — the bad kind of hungry. Her buddy Léo is late again, and he’s not calling. “He’s kidding, right?” she says to nobody.

Inside, the music is important: it’s the only reason this place is full. A guy at the door is pushing people in, and a kid is running between the tables. Bia pretends she isn’t angry. Actually, she is.

Léo arrives with a story about a conference, a library, and the television crew at his college. “It’s different this year, bro. Every nation sends someone.” He’s joking — he’s a waiter on a Tuesday. But he’s also watching the door, and he’s working the room like a politician. Is it possible that he’s right? “Is it natural to lie to your friends?” she asks. “No,” he says. “It’s important not to.”

They split a plate of fries, and the night is chill again. Nobody is drunk, nobody is done — yet. Right now the only problem is the pool of beer on the table.
