It Got Handled — the Passive That Hides the Doer

Who did it? The passive is the grammar of not saying.

The object becomes the boss

From "they fired him" to "he got fired"

The thing that receives the action moves to the front, and the doer leaves the sentence

Take an active sentence: They fired him. Now decide that the interesting thing is him, not they, and move him to the front: He was fired. That is the whole passive. The object becomes the subject, the verb becomes be + past participle, and the person who did it disappears unless you invite them back with by.

O português faz o mesmo — "ele foi demitido", "foi feito". A forma é barata para você; caro é lembrar que o tempo verbal mora no be, e o particípio nunca muda. The tense lives on be: is fired, was fired, has been fired, will be fired. The participle never moves.

Why bother? Three reasons, and you use all of them daily. The doer is unknown: "My bike got stolen." The doer is obvious: "He got arrested" — by the police, obviously. The doer is not the point, or you would rather not say it: "The shi- got handled." That last one is the reason this lesson exists. The passive is the grammar of not saying who.

The tense moves, the participle stays

One verb, five tenses, one participle
Tense Passive When you hear it
present The report is made every Friday. routine, rules
past The report was made yesterday. one finished event
present perfect The report has been made. it exists now, because of then
future The report will be made tomorrow. a promise, a plan
gonna The report is gonna be made tonight. the spoken future
Continuous and modals follow the same road: is being made, must be made. Learn the participle and every tense is available.

Get does the informal half

Get + participle is the street passive

It marks a change — usually a bad one, always a story

Get replaces be in speech, and it adds a twist: the event happened to someone, as a change in their life. He was fired is the fact. He got fired is the story you tell about it. The get-passive leans toward bad news and surprise: got fired, got dumped, got caught, got arrested, got robbed. Good news uses it too, but rarely: got paid, got promoted.

Three places get is wrong and be is required: I was born (never "I got born"), I was told (information arrives, it does not "get told"), and any formal writing — a report does not say the budget "got approved".

And by? You add it only when the doer is the news: "The song was written by her brother." When the doer is obvious, unknown, or the point is to hide them, the sentence ends at the participle: "It got handled." Say by too often and your English sounds like a translated form.

Three lines to keep, because they cover the whole lesson: "She got dumped, he got fired, they got caught — no shi-." "He got f---ed over by his own brother." "I was born here, and I was told to leave." The first two run on get; the third uses be twice, because a birth and an order are facts about you, not events that jumped on you.

The seven you will actually hear

Each one is a passive you should be able to say without thinking

Active and passive, the way people say them
Passive Active version What it hides
be made — It's made in Brazil. They make it in Brazil. the factory
get fired — He got fired on Friday. The company fired him. the company, the reason
get dumped — She got dumped over text. He dumped her. the person, mostly
get caught — They got caught on camera. The camera caught them. who was watching
be told — I was told to leave. Somebody told me to leave. who gave the order
be born — I was born here. My mother had me here. the mother, always
get arrested — He got arrested outside the bar. The cops arrested him. the cops, unless you name them
The middle column is what the passive is protecting. Say the active version out loud once, then decide whether you actually wanted to say that.

The neighborhood brief

Nobody says who

Six things happened on the same street this week. Every gap is a passive: choose be for a fact, get for a story, and keep the participle unchanged.

  1. Marcos on Monday, and nobody told him why.
  2. His car the same week — bad luck comes in pairs.
  3. The thief two days later, downtown, on camera.
  4. Aline in the same building she lives in now.
  5. The new bar next month, they say.
  6. Who paid for the party? Nobody knows. It quietly.

Gap 4 is the trap: birth is always was born, never "got born". Gap 6 is the reason the passive exists — the sentence is complete and the doer is still a secret.

Turn the gossip around

Five active lines, made passive

Here are five things people say. Rewrite each one as a passive so the doer disappears. Use be for facts and get for the bad-news ones.

1. Somebody stole my bike. 2. The company fired her on Friday. 3. His brother screwed him over. 4. The cops arrested two guys outside. 5. They're gonna close the bar next month.

Model answer

1. My bike got stolen.

2. She got fired on Friday.

3. He got screwed over by his own brother.

4. Two guys got arrested outside.

5. The bar is gonna be closed next month.

Four of the five take get, because all four are bad news with a story attached. Line 5 takes be, because a closure is an announcement, not a personal disaster — and if you wanted the emotion, you would have said "they're closing the bar", not the passive at all.
