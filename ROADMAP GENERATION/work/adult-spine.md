# ADULT — SPINE (work file, PHASE 1)

One line per concept family, in teaching order. The count of these lines goes into `## Decisions`.

- sound foundations (letters vs sounds, vowels, cognates, false friends, cognate stress) — lessons 1–2 — PEDAGOGY.md pins both; transparent wins come first and open the whole course
- sentence skeleton and the [mode] rule — lessons 3, 7 — the empty/filled mode slot is the single rule every tense reuses, so it is stated before any tense
- present simple and the daily routine — lessons 4–5 — facts vs now; the PT "estou"-for-now trap is core and returns later
- questions and negatives — lessons 6–7, 14 — be-inversion then do-support/did-support, the same move at present and past
- spoken future and reductions — lessons 8–9 — gonna/will; reductions introduced early per PEDAGOGY, and gonna as a future mode not a verb
- get family and root-grouped phrasal verbs — lesson 10 — get as the coringa, then a phrasal family on one root (PEDAGOGY groups items by family)
- prepositions by meaning — lessons 11, 18 — TO/FOR/ON/IN/AT taught as meaning early, then recycled when existence/place language arrives
- conversation management — lessons 12, 19 — small talk, tags, fillers, follow-ups; the talk tools that keep a real scene alive
- past narration — lessons 13–15 — regular/irregular past, was/were, then used-to/would for nostalgia
- noun quantity and money — lessons 16–17 — count/mass, quantifiers, numbers and prices; the street every adult meets
- adjective shape and intensity — lessons 20–21 — position, order, intensifiers (incl. taboo boosters), then comparison
- story assembly — lesson 22 — connectors and scene-then-event, the first whole-story lesson
- connected speech — lesson 23 — linking, elision, weak forms, assimilation once there are enough words to glue
- swearing core — lesson 24 — f---/shi-/bullshi-, censoring policy, and the where-not-to-use advice the register demands
- perfect aspect — lessons 26–27, 29 — duration (the "há + years" trap), result-now, then perfect continuous
- past continuous — lesson 28 — scene vs interrupting event
- modals of ability and obligation — lessons 30–31 — can/could/be-able-to then must/have-to/should/had-better, base verb after the mode
- conditionals and hypotheticals — lessons 32–33 — zero/first/second/third and would
- reported speech — lesson 34 — backshift, say/tell, shifted time words, reported questions
- passive — lesson 35 — be + participle, by-agent, why-use
- relative clauses — lesson 36 — who/that/which/where/when and object omission
- digital and internet language — lessons 37–38 — text abbreviations, tone, DMs, internet slang, then music/lyrics decoding
- AAVE — lessons 39, 46 — a real variety (habitual be, aspect markers, negative concord, context/respect); deepened in the tail
- humor and implicature — lessons 40, 47, 49 — sarcasm, hyperbole, roasting, hard disagreement, argument, callbacks and long jokes
- taboo and intimate language — lessons 42–43 — sexual/plain and slang terms, then the hardest taboos and slurs explained (never used), all finished before the last exam
- politeness and decorum — lessons 41, 44 — softened imperatives, then the public/work ceiling and soft disagreement as the required prudent counterweight to the swear-heavy course
