# ADULT — LABELS (work file, PHASE 2)

Every label, its one-sentence gloss, and its assignment `→ L<n>`. The roadmap's `teaches:` lines are copied from this column.
Lowercase, `-` inside a part, `/` between parts, at most 3 parts. One label, one lesson.

## sound

- sound/letter-vs-sound — the letter does not fix the vowel sound, so you learn the sound of each word and let the spelling be only a hint. → L1
- sound/cognate-suffix-code — Portuguese endings map onto English ones (-ção→-tion, -dade→-ty, -mente→-ly), handing you thousands of words for free. → L1
- sound/reduction-preview — fast English squeezes common words into reduced forms (gonna, wanna), which you will learn as they are really said. → L1
- sound/vowel-pairs — some vowels split by length and tension (beat/bit, pool/pull), and the pair is chosen by the word, not the spelling. → L2
- sound/false-friend — a cognate can look safe but carry a different meaning (push≠puxar, parents≠parentes, actually≠atualmente). → L2
- sound/cognate-stress — in -tion, -ic and -ity words the stress lands just before the ending, not where Portuguese puts it. → L2
- sound/linking — a final consonant joins a following vowel, so words run together (an apple sounds like "a napple"). → L23
- sound/elision — weak sounds drop in fast speech (next day becomes "nex'day"). → L23
- sound/weak-forms — function words shrink (to→tuh, for→fer, and→'n') and the strong form sounds unnatural. → L23
- sound/assimilation — a sound changes to match its neighbour (in bed sounds like "im bed"). → L23

## word-order

- word-order/skeleton — English fixes the order subject→verb→object→place→time, and you cannot shuffle it the way Portuguese can. → L3

## verb

- verb/be-identity — am/is/are declare who or what something is (name, nationality, job, mood). → L3
- verb/want-to-need-to — want, need, try and plan take to + base verb. → L10
- verb/like-enjoy-ing — enjoy, hate and like take -ing (and like also takes to with a meaning shift) for preferences. → L10
- verb/go-to-place — go to + a place is movement (going to the office), a different thing from the future mode gonna. → L8

## mode

- mode/empty-vs-filled — the verb carries the tense marker when no helper stands in its slot, and a helper takes the marker when one does. → L3
- mode/marker-moves — the -s marker lives on does/don't, not on the main verb, which returns to base. → L7

## present-simple

- present-simple/facts-not-now — the simple present states facts, habits and general truths, never the right-now action. → L4
- present-simple/third-person-s — he/she/it adds -s to the verb, and everyone else leaves it off. → L4
- present-simple/pt-now-trap — "estou fazendo" is not "I do" but "I'm doing", because the simple present cannot mean now. → L4
- present-simple/vs-continuous-now — choose the continuous for right-now and the simple for in-general. → L5

## adverb

- adverb/frequency-position — always/usually/sometimes/never sit before the main verb (after be). → L4

## present-continuous

- present-continuous/now — be + -ing marks happening-now and temporary situations, the twin of "estou + -ndo". → L5
- present-continuous/temporary — be + -ing also covers temporary states around now (this week I'm staying at my cousin's). → L5

## question

- question/be-inversion — with be, the question swaps subject and verb and uses no do. → L6
- question/short-answer — a yes/no answer echoes the helper (Yes, I am / No, I'm not), not a bare yes. → L6
- question/wh-be — what/where/who/how + be asks about identity, place or state. → L6
- question/do-support — do/does carries present questions for every verb except be, and the main verb returns to base. → L7
- question/wh-do — what/where/when + do + subject + base verb asks for detail on an action. → L7
- question/tag-check — a short tag (right?, you know?) turns a statement into a check on the listener. → L12
- question/did-support — did carries past questions and the verb returns to base. → L14

## negation

- negation/dont-doesnt — don't/doesn't + base verb forms the present negative. → L7

## future

- future/gonna-mode — [be] gonna + base verb is the natural spoken future (vou), a mode rather than a verb. → L8
- future/gonna-vs-going-to — gonna is the spoken form and going to its written and formal twin, both marking the future. → L8
- future/will — will + base verb makes promises, offers and on-the-spot decisions. → L9
- future/wont — won't (will not) makes the negative future and refusals. → L9
- future/gonna-vs-will — gonna is for a plan already in mind, will for a decision now or an offer. → L9

## get

## preposition

- preposition/in-inside-at-point — IN means inside a space or time span, and AT means a point in space or time. → L11
- preposition/verb-plus-prep — verbs travel glued to one preposition (depend on, wait for, talk to), so learn them together. → L11

## present-perfect

- present-perfect/duration-for-since — have/has + participle for an action running from past to now, with for = duration and since = start point (the "trabalho aqui há 5 anos" trap). → L26

- present-perfect/result-now — the perfect reports a past action whose result is visible now (I've lost my keys). → L27
- present-perfect/been-gone — have been (went and came back, or experience) contrasts with have gone (still away). → L27
- present-perfect/pt-divide — in some places English needs the perfect where Portuguese reaches for the present or the past. → L27
