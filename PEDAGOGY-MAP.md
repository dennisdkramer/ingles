## [PASSES] — capability-map.html §1's three steps, in its order; the first pass decides nothing about form
### [1a] CONTENT — the words of the lesson, before any shape
- **One section, one teaching point, and the heading names it.** The voice decides how the point is said and never blurs what it is. If a section cannot be summarised as "this section teaches …", or two headings could be swapped without the page changing, that section is a tour of the concept's uses rather than a step in a sequence: rewrite it until the one thing it teaches is on its face. Clarity and simplicity of the teaching point outrank the track's flavour every time — slang, scene and joke are cut the moment they make the point harder to find.
- The teaching sequence is written first, and every word the student will read, hear or answer is written with it: the headings, the explanations, the examples, the dialogs, the activities with their instructions and their keys, the word lists, the chart contents, and the lines a recording will carry.
- Nothing about appearance is decided here: no `data-span`, no `data-look`, no visual's markup, no section invented to fill a row, no frame. A block is opened only where a teaching unit ends.
- This is the pass that decides whether the lesson is good. A block whose content is thin cannot be rescued by the shape it is given later, so the [COVERAGE] rules below are met on the words alone.
### [1b] ARRANGEMENT — the shape, once the words exist
- Each block takes the visual that answers its question, chosen from capability-map.html §3 by the concept it teaches and never by the visual's name; its markup is copied from the example where that visual is drawn (§4 or §5), never from memory.
- §3's tests are the gate: a visual that repeats the sentence beside it, or whose removal would cost the student nothing, is not used. A block of prose with no visual is the normal case.
- `data-span` and `data-look` are set now and only here, and **width is earned, never defaulted**: every block is written at the narrowest span its content reads in — 4, or 3 when the whole block is one short line — and is widened only when its own content cannot be read there (a chart's columns, a dialog's long turns, a marked sentence's arrows, an answer field that needs the line) or when it is alone in its row and the rest of the line would be empty. Two short blocks share a row before either goes full width; the spans in a row add to 12; full width is the exception and the page's own content is its reason. One tint and one outline per section at most, and a look that never marks importance, never dresses a teaching block as an activity, and never varies the page for its own sake.
- The tools run here — `qa/pos.mjs` for the marks, `qa/audio.mjs` for a recording, `PronKey.render` for a key — and what they print is pasted, never retyped.
### [1c] WORDING — after the page is built
- Prose may be shortened to remove a wrapped stub at the end of a line, or lengthened to fill one, only where the lesson does not get worse for it; a block shorter than its neighbour in a row may gain an example or a question to even the heights.


## [AUTHORING] - 
- Each lesson is imagined and generated in a unique and varied way, the order and type of its sections and blocks are never a standard - they are conceived independently as the optimal way to deliver the specific lesson and/or the specific elements within it.
- The tone and vocabulary described in the REGISTER is maintained throughout. The ROADMAP teaching concepts, vocabulary, and examples are thoroughly explored, explained, trained, and tested - there is *NO LIMIT* on lesson duration or content quantity - more depth, more thorough explanations (**clarity**, never filler or prose for its own sake), and more training activities are always better than less- all incorporated into an engaging and relevant lesson for the <TRACK>-REGISTER.md [PERSONA].
## [LEVEL] — the lesson number is a curriculum position, and it fixes the page's difficulty
- **The number is both floor and ceiling.** The page may assume every concept, form and word the course taught through the lesson before it, and nothing else; and it must demand the work of a student who has had that many lessons. Lesson 1 assumes no English; by 50 the student has functional fluency.
- **Difficulty is what the student must do — never how rare the words are.** A page is hard when the student must hold a rule across a whole sentence, choose between forms that compete, move several things at once, produce rather than select, and notice where the rule does not apply. It is easy when the student fills one word per line with the answer printed beside it, however advanced the topic's name.
- **The bands, and what each one owes:**
  - **1–10, foundation** — no English assumed. Portuguese, or two-word English, may carry the instruction; one concept at a time; recognition before production; single-word answers; every new word glossed; the page explains from zero.
  - **11–25, building** — short English instructions, Portuguese where the mapping misleads; two concepts side by side; one- and two-word gaps; glosses on the new items; the page still explains from zero.
  - **26–40, consolidating** — English-only instructions; two or three concepts combined in one sentence; phrase- and sentence-length answers; a gloss only where Portuguese misleads; the metalanguage (subject, verb, tense, clause) is used without being defined.
  - **41–50, advanced** — English only, metalanguage included: *reporting verb, backshift, clause, deictic, aspect* are the page's working words, never terms it stops to introduce. The earlier lessons are machinery, not revision: the page does not re-teach the past, the pronouns or the question form — it makes them carry new work. Answers are clauses or whole sentences; a one-word gap is the exception, never the format, and no item's answer is a word the page printed in the line above. Exceptions, the choice between speech and careful writing, and what a reporting verb does to the social meaning are content, because the student is ready for them. The quiz combines at least two concepts per item and includes production, not only selection.
- **A page can be advanced in content and beginner in difficulty, and that is a defect.** Reported speech at 44 is not a table of shifts plus one-word gaps: it is the system (what moves, why, in what order), the switch that turns the shift off, the exceptions, and the student producing paragraphs of relayed speech.
- **The explanation is not the material.** The prose that explains a concept or a vocabulary item may be plain: it should sit at the lesson's band, and it does not have to be advanced. What must be at the band is the English the student handles — every example sentence, quoted line, dialog turn, gap answer, drill item and quiz item. A page that explains reported speech elegantly around "I'm tired." is a beginner page wearing advanced prose, and it fails this section even when every paragraph reads beautifully.
- **The examples are also the register.** Those same sentences carry the track's voice: they are written in the REGISTER's scenes and with its own words from its lists, never in a neutral course voice that merely describes them. A block whose examples could be printed in any track is a register failure even when its topic fits the track.
- **What maps directly** onto Portuguese is cheap; what maps onto it and misleads - or has no counterpart - is expensive. ("descend" is cheaper than "go down" although it is the rarer word. Present perfect is very expensive because the mapping breaks)
- **Sentence complexity** - a clause inside a clause, a form whose Portuguese counterpart does not match, etc.. is expensive
- **Importance can outrank level.** A concept the lesson must teach, when it is expensive, gains increased support around it grows - more teaching and more training. Support adds teaching and training; it never restores an earlier band's scaffolding.
- Optimize concept/element/content grouping/order according to the needs of the student. [Introduce, compare, or make note of] [synonyms, antonyms, homophones, homographs, false cognates, relevant idioms/expressions, etc.] that are relevant to the lesson content.
- Every (new word/form/concept) the student must understand is presented before it is used; if a block wants the student to *infer*, the page says so. 
- Teach the rule, the reasoning (the mental model), and its direct consequences.
- Multiple-choice options are shuffled so the right one is not always in the same slot. No copy-exercises: the student produces from memory or meaning,
- Portuguese is used to explain content, compare/contrast grammar/vocabulary between the languages, or give translations when the necessary information in english would not be well understood by the student at the level of the lesson that contains it or when it is significantly more efficient to explain something with portuguese.
- **[COVERAGE] The roadmap section is a floor, not a summary.** Every concept, construction and example it names is accounted for in the page, and every vocabulary item it lists is carried where the register licenses it. The lesson is not finished because the section has been mentioned: it is finished when each concept has been taught, contrasted, trained and tested.
- **A concept is not taught until the student could produce it unaided.** For each concept the page carries the rule; the reasoning (the mental model) and what follows from it; where Portuguese maps, misleads or has no counterpart; worked examples in the register's own scenes; training in which the student produces it rather than recognizes it; and a check that would fail had the student only read the explanation. A label, a table row or a passing mention is not teaching.
- **Thin is measured by consequence, not by length.** An explanation that leaves the student unable to say the sentence unaided is thin at any length; a short one that hands them the rule and its reason is not. Where a concept is expensive (above), its support grows — more teaching before it and more training after it — because importance outranks level.
- **The page ends by using every concept it taught on material the student has not seen**: mixed, new sentences, in the register's world — never the lesson's own examples again.
- **[TEACH → TRAIN → QUIZ] Every lesson runs the same three obligations, in this order.** **Teach** the elements of its content thoroughly — each concept and vocabulary item the lesson owns, with the rule, its reasoning, its consequences and the Portuguese contrast where the mapping misleads — and present each before the student is asked to use it. **Train** what was taught: the student practices it, producing from memory or meaning, on material that is new to the page rather than a copy of the explanation, and the training follows the teaching it belongs to. **Quiz** at the end: the lesson closes with a quiz (the last section before the notebook) that tests the lesson's content as a whole — every concept the lesson taught appears in it, alone or inside a sentence that combines it with another, on material the student has not seen, with its keys written — so that passing it requires having learned the lesson, not having read it.

## [MUSIC] — a section built on a recording, when the lesson carries one
- **The song is chosen by the lesson's structure, never by taste.** The line must carry the form or the vocabulary the lesson is teaching in a way that can be pointed at on the page. A line that merely paraphrases it is not a candidate, and a well-known song whose line teaches nothing is not one either. The phrase is found by web search — LRCLIB cannot search lyric text — and then proved with `--finds`, which prints the line number; a line number is never passed unproved.
- **Two runs, the second one pinned.** Run `qa/audio.mjs` once without `--out` to see which upload it paired (it checks the length within 2 s), then repeat with `--lrc-id` pinned, because the site's results move between calls and an unpinned run can pair a different edit of the same song.
- **The tool prints, the page pastes.** `qa/audio.mjs` writes `assets/<name>.mp3` itself and prints the figure markup with its `data-t` values, its `data-duration`, and the provenance row. Nothing is retyped and **no `data-t` is ever edited by hand**: a highlight that fires early or late is moved with `--cue-shift`, an excerpt sitting wrong inside the recording is moved with `--early`, and either flag leaves the command that reproduces the page. On a lesson page the source is `../assets/<name>.mp3`, and the printed row goes to `assets/README.md`. The player script is already in `_shell.html`; nothing is added to the page for audio.
- **One per lesson, and it is a section like any other.** It has a teaching point that its heading names, and it runs the lesson's own sequence on that line: the student predicts what they will hear, hears it, the line is marked and read, the rule is stated as it appears **in this line**, and the student then produces the same structure from words that are not the song's. The recording is evidence, never decoration — if the section would teach the same thing with the audio removed, it is not a section and the line was not relevant.

## [CHECK] — before the build, read the page back against its strongest inputs

- Read three example sentences and three drill items at random — the prose around them is not the evidence. - Read the section headings alone, in order, with the body hidden. Each must name the one thing its section teaches, and each may assume only what the headings before it established. (Catches a page that is full of the concept and vague about what it is teaching.)
- Read the page's difficulty against its number: name the band, then name what the page demands — the length of its answers, whether it re-teaches a lesson the student has already had, whether a lesson 5 could read its instructions. (Catches a clip that decorates a section instead of carrying its point.)
