2. The page must run on the site: mechanical skeleton + deliverable state the engines can read;
3. Every factual claim is true and defensible — never pad a section with an unverified assertion.

## [BUILD] SHAPE
1. The build is a **synthesis according to [I/O] Precedence and the model to make outstanding teaching material**
2. Track register, [LEVEL] and model intuition may supercede the ROADMAP's items, but never silently - the purpose for the deviation must be authored below the <h2> of the affected content.

3. Use your creativity to decide what [[type of content] and [in what order]] is optimal for the student to learn from, and enjoy each lesson. **The order is always** Pedagogical shape -> Lesson Content -> THEN vehicles are imagined or selected which best present that content in that shape. **Consider the following**:
  - a) Can the concepts be grouped or categorized to optimize understanding?
  - b) To what extent and in what combination does student success depend on [understanding [a concept] or [translation mapping]], [training and getting accustomed to its use], or [memorization]?
  - c) Is this lesson/section/block a good opportunity to [introduce, compare, or make note of] any [synonyms, antonyms, homophones, homographs, false cognates, relevant idioms/expressions, etc.]?
  - d) Considering (a), (b), and (c), is the given content well suited to a particular display element, vehicle, or game from [I/O] 5 or from an outside source?
  - e) If an item from among [I/O] #5 `###` headings is chosen, read only that heading's line range for implementation details. Each heading contains a high-level description, and 'clarity of purpose' and 'fun' scores. Any shape and vehicle you imagine is equally valid and may be used for any part of any lesson - examples are mere possibilities.

## [PORTUGUESE] Quantity decreases as student english level increases **HIGH PRIORITY**
- For any generated [[**Lesson introduction, explanatory text, tip, headline, or column header**], it is authored in Portuguese [only if [LESSON #] < ([CONTENT LEVEL] of the explanation)] ELSE [it is authored in English, and [CONTENT LEVEL] is authored to be as close as possible to [STUDENT LEVEL]]]
    - **The Portuguese share is a target range in figures, not a judgement call.** Take the elements this rule names - lesson introduction, explanatory text, tip, headline, column header - and the share of them authored in Portuguese must sit inside:
       - **ceiling = 75 − (L − 5) × 1.8 %** - no more Portuguese than this, at any lesson number;
       - **floor = 35 − (L − 5) × 1.8 %**, clamped at 0 - no less, because a lesson that stopped glossing has abandoned its reader.
       - With L the lesson number, lesson 1 targets **42–82%**, lesson 5 **35–75%**, lesson 20 **8–48%**, lesson 30 **0–30%**, lesson 40 **0–12%**, and from lesson 47 the ceiling itself reaches 0 and is clamped there, so nothing above that level is glossed.
- For any generated content which is NOT [[**Lesson introduction, explanatory text, tip, headline, or column header**]], [it is synthesized with [[derived socio-linguistic context] and [its TRACK's [register details and persona priorities]]], [CONTENT LEVEL] is as close as possible to [STUDENT LEVEL], and [it is Portuguese ONLY when *defensibly optimal for teaching purposes*] ELSE it is authored in English].

## [LEVEL]
- "STUDENT LEVEL" = [Current Lesson #].
- "STUDENT LEVEL APPROPRIATE" = at or above [STUDENT LEVEL]
- **The difficulty is measured by the CONCEPTS and the required  TRAINING to master them, never in the prose or the options.** An explanation may sit at whatever level clarity needs — it is an auxiliary tool, and density there is reading load, not difficulty. What is graded is what the student must infer, choose and produce: a page whose hardest part is a sentence to read and whose answers are single words is too easy however dense its explanations.
- Lesson generation includes the vocabulary: the roadmap's `- Vocabulary:` items are the minimum, and any other vocabulary relevant to the lesson or the student's goals is taught as well — as a list, inside phrases, or as short notes beside related content. Drill and recycle the most useful items.
- **Every block MARKS its own band.** `data-level="10|20|30|40|50"` on the block element, the band of that block's hardest demand, assessed while authoring and stated as one of the five. The assessment is the author's — no page is ever read back to make it — and the check is arithmetic: **a block's number is at or above the lesson number, and a block marked 50 never carries a keyed answer**, because 50 means the page's own answer is not one key (49). A mark that cannot name which of the four demands set it is not a mark.
- **A block's load is its content level times the activity's multiplier, and the two are read together.** `load = L × M`: **L** is the CONTENT LEVEL of the block, 1–50, read off the four demands above — the hardest thing the page asks a student to think about; **M** is the multiplier the activity declares in its`###` heading ([BUILD] 3(e)), never a survey of other entries. For the work the block is given, `M = qty × pace × risk` — **qty**: how much the student holds at once (an item's options or fields, or the block's item count); **pace**: a clock, or a rule that allows one shot at each item; **risk**: lives, or `data-retry="0"`. `M = 1` is the identity, so a block that adds none of the three computes to exactly its own L and nothing already built changes meaning. **Only L is capped, at 50, because 50 is the top of the content scale. The product is not capped at all:** 40-level content at ×2 computes to 80, and that is a legitimate instruction to make a block hard, not an error to round off. The formula exists to produce a number to compare against the student's level, not a number to bring into range.
- **M is declared by the activity, never estimated by the build.** The entry states the count at which the activity is ×1, its multiplier at that activity's own ceiling, and what its clock or its one-chance rule adds. Only the activity knows whether its eighth option is twice the work of its fourth (`whackMole`: it is) or whether a fourth is already the end of the range (`catchBasket`: four pots and no fifth). **The line is a curve, not a ceiling:** the baseline is where M = 1, above it the entry gives the value at its own ceiling, and *below* it the multiplier falls with the work — an activity whose baseline is four items sits under ×1 at two, because two is half the searching. Serving a lower-level student with fewer items of the same activity is exactly what that direction is for; it is not a way past the bar, because the total still has to clear the student's own level. A count beyond the entry's `opts` range is not a larger multiplier — it is an unauthorable block.
- **The multiplier moves the LOAD, in either direction; it never changes the KIND of demand the content makes.** `L` is the level of thinking the content asks for; `M` is how much of that thinking the block carries out. So `L=20, M=2` and `L=40, M=1` both land on a load of 40 and remain different blocks, and neither is a defect — the first is 20-level thinking asked twice over (more to hold, more to search against, more pace), the second 40-level thinking asked once (inference, accuracy). Landing on one load while choosing between them is how a build decides which axis its difficulty sits on. What M cannot do is turn one kind of question into another: ×2 on 10-level content is *the same stated fact twice*, which reads as volume and never as inference, so a teaching point that needs the student to infer cannot be served by counting more of something easier. The content level is chosen for the thinking; the multiplier is chosen for the amount.
- **There is no load ceiling — a block over about 70 has a LENGTH problem, not a difficulty one.** The cap of 50 bounds the *content level*, never the load: a build asked for a super-challenging block builds a high load and is finished, which is exactly the freedom this file grants when it refuses to defend an upper bound — no page has come back too hard and many too easy. The only practical limit is how much a student can hold in one sitting, so past roughly 70 (about 1.4× the top of the content scale) the items belong in **two blocks** — two blocks of 35–40 instead of one wall of 70 — and the split is for length and attention, not because the work was illegal. 70 is the owner's number and lives only here; a block at 60 is one block.

## [AUTHORING]
- The register governs every string the student reads — headline, explanation, stem, option, note, table cell — not only dialogue and vocabulary. Each is compared against `<TRACK>-REGISTER.md` before it ships: would this persona write this, in this situation, in this country? An explanation in textbook English beside slang options teaches the student to answer in the wrong voice.
- That comparison is the author's, applied string by string; no harness can make it. A checker holds the floor (no uncontracted auxiliary in speech, no formal connective, no British word in an American scene) but cannot certify the ceiling. A section is finished only when every register-carrying string has been read back against the register file and rewritten until it passes.
- Explanatory layer may live anywhere: lead, chart cell, an extra explanation column, a `tr.note` row. A chart is a teaching device, not a display device.
- Every (new word/form/concept) the student must understand is presented before it is used, else removed; if a block wants the student to *infer*, the page says so. An item never requests a judgement on an axis the page didn't define prior to the item, and no 'terminology' is used to categorize or describe linguistic concepts or classes if it is not verifiably in real-world use.
- Never show the answer in a prompt, and show Portuguese hints/explainers only where NECESSARY — the English that would carry the point sits above this lesson's level — or OPTIMAL — the point is a contrast between the two languages that English cannot make. Test, in order: can the student act correctly without it? cut it. Could the same point be made in English at this level? write English. Otherwise the smallest Portuguese that unblocks it: the word, the clause — never a translation of English already on the page.
- No copy-exercises: the student produces from memory or meaning, never transcribes the prompt.
- Teach in context, in phrases, inside a scenario, dialogue or story — not isolated glosses.
- Whenever student level appropriate, teach the rule, the reasoning (the mental model), and its direct consequences (integrated with the lesson content or in its own section as appropriate); say plainly when [there is no clear or consistent] [rule and/or reasoning].
- Each item carries the condition that decides it (a gloss that could go either way is not an item).
- False cognates (eg. 'pretend' -X- 'pretender' -> 'intend') and words which [in one language cleanly map onto] [more than one word in the other] (eg. 'live' -> 'morar' / 'viver') are presented together with these characteristics [shown/explained/demonstrated as appropriate].
- Practice trains the lesson's concepts, inline following what they train and/or in a penultimate section. The last section is a quiz that tests all of it — it may echo the training, never copy it.  **Every new item and/or concept is thoroughly taught and/or demonstrated, confirmed with training, subsequently TESTED - each by its own question.** A vocabulary item or a concept is tested only when it is the correct answer to a question that is about it: one question per new item, at least one question per concept (difficult concepts like present perfect tense require MANY questions).
- All trained or tested items in an activity must be the answer rather than a distractor, a word in the stem, or a word in the surrounding prose (an item that only ever appears as a wrong option was ignored, not tested).  **A concept is declared, not implied:** each pid on this lesson's own roadmap `teaches:` line is written verbatim into `data-teaches="<pid>"` on every block that trains it and on the quiz question that tests it.
- Multiple-choice options are shuffled so the right one is not always in the same slot.
- **An item's option count is set by what it has to discriminate, never by habit.** 2 is right only when the pair is genuinely binary, and 5–6 belong to an item whose content supplies that many plausible candidates (a family of ten look-phrases, six tense forms). If a vehicle has a maximum option count, it is stated in its CONTENT_EXAMPLES entry, and options must be authored in that range or the vehicle replaced. Difficulty is the other limit carried by a game's **`load` multiplier** (else it is 1) — what the count, the clock and the one-chance rule each add — which a build multiplies by the block's content level to read the block's total load (`[LEVEL], the load rules`). The activity states the number; a build never estimates one.
- **Every training block declares its move**, `data-move` on the block element, one value: `spot` (find the right one among decoys), `choose` (pick the form that fits a stated situation), `produce` (write the language yourself), `correct` (find and fix what is wrong), `contrast` (decide between two forms that are confused), `recall` (retrieve something taught in an earlier lesson, marked `data-recalls="<pid>"`). A `produce` block carries an `input.gap` inside a sentence or a `textarea` other than `#feed` — a radio-only block cannot be `produce`. A block that trains nothing is a **teaching** block (a chart of reference, a dialogue, a scenario) and declares no move.
- For any section and/or block, a concise text below the header names what the student must do with the block, or what the block contains. It repeats no header, option, gloss or sentence already on the page.
- Activity answer options match the lesson in concept and the [LEVEL] in length and linguistic complexity — a phrase answer gets phrase distractors, never one-word ones.
- Never compress, summarise or omit an item to fit a page. There is no cap on sections, tables, examples, exercises, dictation lines, recordings or quiz items, and no page-length limit.

## [PAGE] MECHANICS — the order a page is decided
- The generation order is always content-first: the lesson's shape, then the student-facing strings, then content and concept-based organization — parallel elements sit beside each other in matching block sizes (three things to compare are three thirds in one row, a pair is two halves) so the comparison reads as one set. Then the vehicle ([BUILD] 3) is chosen and applied. A block's size follows the content it has been chosen for, never anticipates it. The block takes the narrowest slot the content fills — its width steps up one slot only when a structural element stops fitting — prose wraps, so prose never widens a block. → (style.css `/* ## BLOCK WIDTH SLOTS`)
- **The build's last pass is a cleanup pass:** two adjacent blocks with content of unequal height are re-read as they appear in their containers and relevant content is authored to extend the shorter one — never a stretched option or random prose.

## [PROOF] - IGNORE, DISABLED
- **The build ends by measuring the page it just wrote.** When the page and its sidecar exist, and before the lesson is reported as done, run the static check on that one page and on nothing wider:
- `node qa/lang.mjs --track=<track> <N>` - the page's own voice against the target range in [BUILD]
- **Scope is that page.** A sweep (`--all`, or a bare `npm run qa`) is not a self-check: it puts other lessons' pages, bands and fingerprints in front of a build that may not read them ([GENERATION] 1 and 3).
- **A verdict on finished work, never a dial.** A share met by adding Portuguese because the floor is near, or a concept met by marking a block that does not train it, satisfies the instrument instead of the student - the same defect as a copied shape, reached from the other side. A red is answered in the page.
- **The record leads the page.** If a check sends the build back to reshape the page, the fingerprint no longer matches and a new record is appended: the record never catches up to the page it describes.
- `qa/content.mjs`, `qa/overflow.mjs` and `qa/live.mjs` are not part of this step - each opens a server or a real browser, so they belong at the end of a batch, not at the end of every page (`VERIFICATION.md`).
