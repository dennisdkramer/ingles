# PEDAGOGY-MAP — Complete Lesson Plan for BUSINESS Lessons 1-50

*Field key:
   · `Content:` what the lesson teaches 
   · `Examples:` / `Key vocabulary:` / `Vocabulary:` the language it uses 
   · `Focus:` high-level taught-concept/purpose of lesson
   · `Advanced element:` / `Professional relevance:` / `Natural register:`  details
   · `Add:` draft-plan material (to be folded in)
   · `Generation:` build instruction or hold note for that page

## Generation rules (hard)

1. **Depth follows the lesson.** The ramp is smooth: Lesson 1 A0 → Lesson 10 A1 → Lesson 20 A2 → Lesson 30 B1 → Lesson 40 B2 → Lesson 50 C1. Any concept or vocabulary item a lesson includes must be taught at the depth and difficulty of that lesson, and must not be redundant with prior lessons: a topic returns only by going deeper, wider or more natural/register-appropriate — never by repeating an earlier explanation or its drills.
2. **No length or quantity budget.** There is no cap on sections, tables, examples, exercises, ditado lines, recordings or quiz items, and no page-length limit. Everything this roadmap entry references must be robustly included, thoroughly explained and fully trained inside its lesson — explanation → examples → recognition practice → production practice → no-consultation quiz. Never compress, summarise or omit a roadmap item to fit a page.
3. **Vocabulary is a word bag.** `Vocabulary:` / `Key vocabulary:` lists are raw material, not copy: group them by function, teach them inside phrases, then drill and recycle them. A lesson whose entry lists no vocabulary must still introduce new, topic-relevant vocabulary of its own — every lesson leaves the learner with new words.
4. **Language of instruction — match each text to the student's level.** Explanatory text goes in English when the student at that lesson's level can understand it, and in **concise Portuguese** when they cannot: compare the student's level with the level *that particular text* requires, sentence by sentence. Lesson 1: every explanation is in Portuguese (an A0 learner understands nothing yet in English). Lesson 20: the student reads many explanations in English, with concise Portuguese kept for the harder ones. Lesson 40: Portuguese explanations are almost never needed — only for a genuinely new or awkward concept. When Portuguese is used it is always tight: the shortest phrasing that unblocks the learner, never a full parallel translation of the page.
5. **Site contract.** `<lesson>.edit.html` is an identical copy of the published `<lesson>.html`, never a different version; navigation is purely sequential (`← Last` / `Next →`) with no lesson count anywhere.

1. **Lesson 1: Introduction to Sounds & the Sentence Skeleton**
   * Content: the subject is always named (I am from Brazil, never "Sou do Brasil"); be am/is/are; a/an decided by sound (a university, an hour); Sound Lab — vowels have no fixed sound (A: cat/cake/car/about · O: go/good/who); the joker it for time, weather, day and distance (It's 8pm / raining / Monday / far); Treino A-G with listening and recording; Quiz.
   * Add (plan 1): /ɪ/ vs /æ/ as the first contrast (sit·hit·fit vs cat·hat·mat); the six stops p/t/k and b/d/g as voiceless/voiced pairs; aspiration of p/t/k at the start of a syllable; the final consonant "cut off" (cat, dog, car, tree — no extra vowel); everyday objects as the carriers of a/an; it's + weather and feelings (It's sunny / raining / hot / cold).
   * Focus: first talk + the first phonetic law (sound is not letter).

2. **Lesson 2: The Cognate Fast Lane**
   * Content: the suffix code (-ção → -tion, -dade → -ty, -mente → -ly, -ado → -ed, -ivo → -ive, -mento → -ment, -ista → -ist); the cognate is not always the word people use (photograph → photo, telephone → phone, television → TV, automobile → car, profession → job); false friends (actually, pretend, push, assist, lunch, college); Treino; Quiz.
   * Add (plan 2): word stress inside the cognates (-TION, -SION, -ITY, -IC always stress the syllable before the suffix: PHO·to·graph → pho·TO·gra·phy → pho·to·GRAPH·ic, and the stress often lands elsewhere than in the Portuguese word); the short-vowel drill /p/ pat-pet-pit, /t/, /k/ cat-cut-cute; "one strong syllable, the rest relaxed" as the first taste of the stress work in lesson 10.
   * Focus: instant vocabulary + the stress rule it brings for free.

3. **Lesson 3: Question Words & Polite Questions**
   * Content: wh- words with be (what, where, when, who, how, why); the embedded (polite) question with statement order (Can you tell me where the office is?); repair phrases (Sorry, could you say that again? / I didn't catch that); Treino; Quiz.
   * Add (plan 3): the pronunciation of the set — /w/ as the "uai" glide (what, where, when, why), who with plain /h/ and no /w/, how; what vs that and where vs there as the listening pair; the six words kept as the highest-frequency words of the language.
   * Focus: asking, and the /w/ sound.

4. **Lesson 4: Simple Present — usage & the -s**
   * Content: what the simple present does (habits, facts, routines) and does not do (past, "now"); the -s of he/she/it (+s, +es, -ies, have → has); affirmative sentences; Treino; Quiz.
   * Add (plan 4): the present continuous (am/is/are + -ing) as the "now" counterpart, so I work vs I'm working are born together; the coffee-shop scenario and its set (coffee, latte, espresso, barista, order, menu, sugar, milk, cream, to go, here, now); -ing/NG as a nasal with no hard "g"; the frequency words previewed (always, usually, often, sometimes, never) for lesson 8.
   * Note: the shipped lesson 11 file still says the present continuous came in "a lição 5" — the content lives here, so redirect that reference when lesson 11 is revised.
   * Focus: the simple present / present continuous pair.

5. **Lesson 5: Do-Support & the Word Order**
   * Content: the fixed order and the [mode] (auxiliary) slot; do/does in questions; short answers (Yes, I do / No, she doesn't); don't/doesn't and where the -s goes; do as a main verb (What do you do?); Treino; Quiz.
   * Add (plan 6): the plan's "core vocabulary + basic sentence structures" objective lands here as the explicit skeleton subject + [mode] + verb + rest, and as the promise that every later operator (be, have, will, would, can, should) reuses the same slot — the frame is named once so lessons 11-20 only swap the operator.
   * Focus: the skeleton that carries every other tense.

6. **Lesson 6: Workhorse Verbs — Movement & Transfer (with their simple past)**
   * Content: go vs come; "levar" → take / bring / take (time); "trazer" and "buscar" → bring / pick up / catch; the sair, chegar, voltar, entrar sets; the simple past of exactly these verbs taught next to their present forms (go → went, come → came, take → took, get → got, leave → left, meet → met, arrive → arrived, go out → went out, come back → came back); the +ed spelling rules (+d, consoante + y → -ied, double consonant) and the three sounds of -ed (/t/ in stopped, /d/ in arrived, /ɪd/ in started) plus the American flap T (called); the past time words (yesterday, last night/week, two days ago, in 2020); Treino; Quiz.
   * Add (plans 5 and 11): participles and the present perfect only as a primer — a participle column in the table (gone, come, taken, got, left, met) and one contrast block (I go / I went / I've gone) so the shape of the system is visible, while every drill stays on the simple past; the frequency argument behind this merge: went, came, took and got are as frequent as go, come, take and get, so the pairs are learned together.
   * Focus: movement verbs in present and past, learnt as one unit.

7. **Lesson 7: Workhorse Verbs — Action & Communication (with their simple past)**
   * Content: say / tell / talk / speak; wait / expect / hope; think / find; pay / cover / charge / spend / waste; miss / be missing / be short of; their past forms in the same breath (say → said, tell → told, speak → spoke, think → thought, find → found, pay → paid, spend → spent, wait → waited, hope → hoped); the /t/ /d/ /ɪd/ contrast revised on these verbs (asked, called, waited); Treino; Quiz.
   * Add (plans 7, 9 and 11): cooking verbs through the imperative — cook, stir, bake, mix, beat, boil, fry, grill, roast, slice, dice, season, serve, taste (Boil the water. / Mix the eggs. / Don't overcook it.), which gives the imperative its first home; the grocery/money field (grocery, cart, aisle, product, price, cashier, checkout, receipt, coupon, discount, sale) as the carrier for pay / charge / spend / cover; the participle primer completed (said, told, spoken, thought, found, paid, spent) with one recognition contrast (I paid / I've paid), drills still on the simple past.
   * Focus: the context decides the verb; the past arrives with the verb.

8. **Lesson 8: Routine, Frequency & Time**
   * Content: frequency adverbs and their place (before the verb, after be); telling the time (o'clock, half past, quarter past/to, noon/midnight); at/on/in for time; ditado; Quiz.
   * Add (plans 5, 7 and 10): the school-day field (morning, alarm, breakfast, shower, backpack, classroom, desk, homework, recess, exam, test, assignment, project, due, finished) and the at-home routine (get up, get dressed, have breakfast, leave, get home, go to bed) — the routines the adverbs need; a whole-day narration that recycles the simple past of lessons 6-7.
   * Focus: saying when.

9. **Lesson 9: There is / There are & Place**
   * Content: there is/are with agreement, questions and negatives; in/on/at for place; and/but/because/so; ditado; Quiz.
   * Add (plans 6 and 7): the rest of the place set — under, above, below, over, behind, in front of, next to, between; the kitchen/home field (kitchen, fridge, oven, stove, sink, counter, cabinet, drawer, shelf, cupboard, knife, spoon, fork, plate, bowl, glass, mug, bottle) used with there is/are + place (There's a fridge in the kitchen. / The cups are in the cabinet.).
   * Focus: existence + location + connection.

10. **Lesson 10: The Sounds — Vowels, Consonants & the Key (double lesson)**
   * Content: the vowel map — name, "big" sound, "small" sound and schwa, plus the C and G rules and every big/small sound with the spellings that produce it; the consonant map — TH voiced vs voiceless (tongue between the teeth), R (American), H, NG (nasal -ing, no hard g), dark L (will, milk, table, full), /w/ vs /v/ (west vs vest) and the flap T (bet·ter, wa·ter); the pronunciation key (· syllable, bold phoneme, gray letters, ə), counting beats, the sleeping vowel, the function-word reductions (a, the, of, to, and, for), the two levels Nível 1 vs Nível 2 and a whole sentence at real speed; Treino; Quiz. (Old lessons 12 and 13 merged into one double lesson.)
   * Add (plans 14, 1, 2 and 13): final consonants cut off, now reinforced by the map; /ɪ/ sit·hit·fit and /æ/ cat·hat·mat kept as the fixed reference pairs; stress as the engine of the key (one strong syllable per word, the weak ones collapse to ə); the cognate stress patterns of lesson 2 (-TION, -ITY, -IC) read through the key; noun vs verb stress pairs (REcord vs reCORD, PREsent vs preSENT); the dark L of will applied when the modals arrive in lesson 15.
   * Focus: the full sound inventory plus the key that turns spelling into sound — one skill, two halves.

11. **Lesson 11: Past Continuous vs Past Simple**
   * Content: was/were + -ing; negative and question forms; when/while; complete action vs action in progress (now set against the past simple of lessons 6-7); work-story sentences; the recorded dialogue; Treino; Quiz.
   * Add (plans 12, 19 and 5): the movie-production field (filming, scene, director, camera, actor, actress, script, take) as the storytelling carrier; reported speech on top of the same forms — say/tell/mention/reply/ask + that-clause with backshift (She said she was working when the client called), reported questions with statement order (The client asked if we had finished), reporting verbs taken from lesson 7; the park/leisure and weather field (picnic, swing, slide, sunny, rainy) for the interruption pattern (We were having a picnic when it started to rain). Limit: past perfect belongs to the advanced block, so backshift stays present → past.
   * Focus: narrating, and retelling what someone said.

12. **Lesson 12: Numbers, Prices & Business Data (new)**
   * Content: cardinals to a million; prices and money ($1,250 = "twelve fifty" or "one thousand two hundred fifty"; R$, €, £); percentages, decimals and fractions (25% = twenty-five percent, 2.5 = two point five, a half, a third, three quarters); dates and years (on July 5th, in 2020, the 1990s); phone numbers, e-mails and URLs read aloud (double letters, at, dot, underscore, dash); ranges and approximations (around 20, between 15 and 20, up to 30).
   * Add: the verb pair numbers need in order to become statements — rise/fall by 10% to 250, up from, down to — so a figure is reported, not just pronounced.
   * Focus: producing and hearing numbers fast enough to work with them.
   * Vocabulary: number, price, cost, budget, discount, revenue, sales, target, increase, decrease, percent, average, total, invoice, receipt, deadline.

13. **Lesson 13: Comparing — Comparatives & Superlatives (new)**
   * Content: short adjectives -er/-est (cheap → cheaper → the cheapest, big → bigger, easy → easier); long adjectives more/most (more expensive, the most efficient); irregulars (good → better → best, bad → worse → worst, far → further); than, as…as, not as…as, less/least; the + superlative and one of the + superlative; intensifiers (much cheaper, a little more expensive, twice as fast).
   * Add: decision language built on the numbers of lesson 12 (25% cheaper, three times faster, the cheapest of the three) and the recommendation frames the business track needs (I'd go with…, it's the best option we have, it's slightly more expensive but faster).
   * Focus: choosing between options out loud.
   * Vocabulary: better, best, worse, worst, cheaper, faster, more efficient, the most, slightly, much, twice, option, than, as…as.

14. **Lesson 14: Words That Shrink**
   * Content: connected speech; have → 've → 'a; and → 'n; going to → gonna, want to → wanna, have to → hafta, got to → gotta, don't know → dunno, kind of → kinda, sort of → sorta, a lot of → lotta, let me → lemme, give me → gimme; woulda/coulda/shoulda; one sentence carrying every phenomenon of the sound lesson (10) plus this one; regional accents (non-rhotic Boston, Texas/South drawl and y'all, non-rhotic British with the long bath vowel, Australian vowels with rising endings); Treino; Quiz.
   * Add (plan 15): the contracted be and the contracted negatives — I'm, he's, she's, it's, we're, they're; don't, doesn't, didn't, can't, won't, haven't; 'cause and outta; the 'd ambiguity (would vs had); the coffee-shop order as the practice script (I'd like a latte but I don't want sugar. / Can you make it extra hot? / To go, please.).
   * Note: the shipped file still says the integration sentence carries "as lições 12, 13 e 14" (old numbering) — it now carries 10 + 14; fix the wording on the next pass over that file.
   * Focus: contractions and reductions in real speech.

15. **Lesson 15: Can, Have to & Should (plus the future)**
   * Content: can/can't; have to/has to, must and gotta; should/shouldn't; Treino; Quiz.
   * Add (plans 16 and 13): the rest of the modal grid — could/may for permission, might/may/could for possibility, ought to, shall, will/would; the single rule (modal + base verb, no to, no -s, one negative per modal); the future forms — will vs be going to vs present continuous for arrangements (I'll call you. / I'm going to call you. / I'm meeting the client at three.); the travel-planning script as carrier (Can we leave early? / Should we visit the museum Saturday? / We must book tickets in advance.); pronunciation: can vs can't in the reduced forms of lesson 14 and the dark L of will from lesson 10.
   * Focus: ability, obligation, advice — and the future.

16. **Lesson 16: Countable, Uncountable & Quantifiers**
   * Content: countable vs uncountable; some/any; much/many/a lot of/a few/a little; Treino; Quiz.
   * Add (plans 6 and 7): the container and partitive phrases the kitchen and grocery fields bring — a cup of coffee, a glass of water, a bottle of water, a can of soda, a jar of coffee, a box of paper, a bag of sugar, a piece of advice/information/news; how much vs how many in shopping questions, tying back to the prices of lesson 12; money as uncountable against coins and notes as countable.
   * Focus: how much of a thing there is.

17. **Lesson 17: Time & Movement Prepositions**
   * Content: at/on/in for time; to/from/into/out of for movement; the fixed blocks (in the morning, at noon, at night, on the weekend); Treino; Quiz.
   * Add (plan 6): the rest of the time set — before, after, by (deadline), until, since, for (duration), during, between, from…to, ago; the calendar/schedule field (calendar, schedule, appointment, deadline, meeting, holiday, weekend, weekday, today, tomorrow, yesterday, next, last, this) as the carrier (by Friday, until noon, since Monday, for two hours); the at/on/in place trio of lesson 9 recapped as the same point/surface/box logic.
   * Focus: the prepositions that decide the meaning.

18. **Lesson 18: Phrasal Verbs & "Get" (plus the passive)**
   * Content: what a phrasal verb is and its formal equivalent; the get family (get back to, get up, get along with, get over); work phrasal verbs (call off, set up, give up, put off, work out, fill in, point out); Treino; Quiz.
   * Add (plan 18): the passive voice built on the participles previewed in lessons 6-7 — be + past participle (The report was finished), get + past participle as the informal passive (He got promoted. / I got paid.), by + agent, purpose with be + participle + to + infinitive (The form was sent to be signed), and the passive of a phrasal verb keeping its particle (The meeting was called off. / The form has to be filled in.); the lab-report script as carrier (The experiment was conducted by the team. / The data was analyzed using software.); separable vs inseparable verbs previewed.
   * Focus: the spoken verb, plus who did what to whom.

19. **Lesson 19: Conditionals 1 & 2 + Would (plus 0 and 3)**
   * Content: 1st conditional (if + present, will); 2nd conditional (if + past, would; if I were); would for polite requests and offers (I would like…, Would you mind…?); Treino; Quiz.
   * Add (plan 17): the zero conditional (if + present, present: If you press this button, the machine stops) and the 3rd conditional with regret (if + had + participle, would have + participle: If I had known, I would have called, reusing woulda/coulda/shoulda from lesson 14); wouldn't/shouldn't/didn't for negative outcomes; would rather / rather than; unless; problem-solving as the carrier (If I lose my wallet, I'll call the bank immediately); the note that would/should/could here are the operators used to report speech in lesson 11.
   * Focus: the four conditionals.

   * Content: have/has + been + -ing for actions that started in the past and continue (or have just stopped); How long have you been…?; for (duration) vs since (point); continuous (in progress) vs simple (finished) — I've been reading the report vs I've read the report; Treino; Quiz.
