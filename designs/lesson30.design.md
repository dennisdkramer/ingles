# Lesson 30 — design (Pass A)

`sequence:` reported speech, slice 2 of 2 — the full system (slice 1: lesson 11 owns backshift of the two past forms inside narration; that frame is not re-taught here).

`recycles:` lesson 11 (backshift of the two past forms inside narration — that frame is used, not re-taught),
lesson 19 (would/should/could as operators), lesson 20 (the perfect), lesson 29 (reporting passives — the same
"report without claiming" instinct), lesson 26 (hedging a claim).
`must not re-teach:` the narrative frame of lesson 11 (past simple + past continuous backshift); the operator grid
(15); the conditionals (19/25); the passive (29).

## Core (what the student can do afterwards)
1. **Choose the verb**: *say* (+ clause) against *tell* (+ person + clause).
2. **Backshift the whole table**: present → past, past → past perfect, will → would, can → could, must → had to —
   and **skip** the shift when the fact is still true.
3. **Move time and place**: now → then, today → that day, tomorrow → the next day, yesterday → the day before,
   here → there.
4. **Report questions and orders**: *asked if / whether* + statement order; *told us to wait* / *asked us to wait*.
5. **Carry attitude with the verb**: admit, warn, offer, promise, refuse, claim — each with its pattern.

## Teaching beats and dependencies
- **Reporting is a camera move in time**, the twin of lesson 29's camera move in agency: the same words, seen from
  later and from outside.
- **The table is mechanical**: one column in, one column out. The only judgement is the **exception** — if the fact
  is still true, the shift is optional (*she said she is working on it*).
- **Time and place words travel with the report**, because *tomorrow* said on Monday is *the next day* said on
  Tuesday. This is the detail that makes a report sound professional.
- **Questions lose their inversion**: *He asked if we had finished* — statement order, no auxiliary fronting.
- **Orders become infinitives**: *told/asked + person + to + verb*; negatives *told us not to wait*.
- **Attitude verbs are the compression tool**: *warn* carries the whole sentence *this is dangerous*.
- **Dependencies**: the narrative backshift from 11, the perfect from 20, the operators from 15.

## Situation
A negotiation the student did not attend: a colleague reports it back in the corridor, then the student writes the
minutes. Same facts, two distances — and one fact that is still true, which must not be backshifted.

## Candidates (three channels)
- **Read — the corridor report.** Six lines of second-hand speech; the student marks what was actually said and what
  changed.
- **Done — the table drill.** Twelve direct sentences → reported, with the exception included.
- **Heard — the voice note.** Four lines with attitude verbs (*she warned us…, he refused…*); the student ticks the
  attitude and the fact.

## Chosen
**Read → Done → Heard.** The corridor first because reported speech is hearsay before it is grammar; then the table,
which is mechanical and needs volume; then the voice note, where the attitude verb carries the message.

**First 30 seconds:** *"I'll call you tomorrow."* → *She said she would call me the next day.* — the student clicks
what changed: the verb, the time word, or both. Nothing is defined.

## What the student produces (evidence)
Six say/tell choices, twelve backshift conversions, four time/place shifts, four reported questions/orders, four
attitude verbs, four listening items, spoken minutes of five sentences, then a no-consultation quiz with no reused
item.

## Width note (for Pass B)
The backshift table and the attitude-verb chart are full width (five fields per row). The time/place group and the
questions/orders group sit as halves in one row — two four-item groups that would each leave a full slot more than
half empty.
