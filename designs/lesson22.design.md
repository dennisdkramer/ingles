# Lesson 22 — design (Pass A)

`recycles:` lesson 18 (the machinery: particle meanings, separability, pronoun placement; *get along with* and
*look after* are already owned — used here, never re-taught), lesson 15 (requests and offers inside the meeting),
lesson 19 (would for the polite move), lesson 21 (talk to / talk about — the preposition pair this lesson extends).
`must not re-teach:` what a phrasal verb is, the particle pictures, or the pronoun rule (all lesson 18's);
the work/planning/problems set — *set up, wrap up, carry out, look into, figure out* (lesson 23); the passive
(lesson 29).

## Core (what the student can do afterwards)
1. **Run a conversation with the talk set**: bring up (raise a topic), go over (review), talk through (explain step
   by step), point out (indicate), catch up with (get the status), come up with (invent).
2. **Describe people arriving and relating**: show up / turn up (arrive), hang out (spend time), fall out with
   (quarrel), make up (reconcile) — and use *get along with* / *look after* from lesson 18 correctly.
3. **Hear *turn* change with its particle**: turn up (arrive), turn down (reject / lower), turn into (become),
   turn out (end up being).
4. **Switch register**: every phrasal verb here has a formal twin for the minutes (raise, review, devise,
   indicate, attend, reconcile).
5. Hold a five-line briefing and report one relationship problem.

## Teaching beats and dependencies
- **These are conversation verbs.** The lesson is organised by what happens in a room: someone raises a topic,
  someone reviews the numbers, someone points out a problem, someone comes up with a fix, someone arrives late.
- **The formal twin is the register switch**, not a synonym to admire: *bring up / raise*, *go over / review*,
  *come up with / devise*, *show up / attend*. The student chooses by audience.
- **`turn` is the demonstration of the particle law**: four particles, four meanings, same verb. This is lesson
  18's machinery being *used*, which is why no definition is repeated.
- **Relationships have a small closed set**: get along with (recycled), fall out with, make up, hang out. The pair
  *fall out / make up* is one story told in two directions.
- **Separability is only tested where it matters**: *point it out* (inside), *talk it through* (inside), *bring it
  up* (inside) — the same rule as 18, exercised with new verbs.
- **Dependencies**: the meeting's requests use 15/19; *talk to / talk about* comes from 21 and extends into
  *talk through*.

## Situation
A weekly team briefing that goes wrong in a small way: a topic gets raised, a number gets reviewed, someone points
out the real problem, someone comes up with a fix, and one colleague turns up late and has to make up with another
after a disagreement. Everything is said in the register people actually use in a meeting.

## Candidates (three channels)
- **Read — the briefing.** Five turns, each built on one phrasal verb; the student matches each turn to its formal
  twin from the minutes.
- **Done — the particle switch.** *turn up / down / into / out* completed in context, then the pronoun drill
  (*point it out*, *bring it up*, *talk it through*).
- **Heard — the corridor.** A 20-second conversation between two colleagues about a third; the student writes what
  happened, verb by verb.

## Chosen
**Read → Done → Heard.** The briefing first, because this set is learned as meeting behaviour; then the particle
switch, which is the one place where the student can *predict* meaning; then the corridor, because relationship
verbs are almost never read.

**First 30 seconds:** *She brought up the budget again.* — the student clicks what she did: raised the topic,
reviewed the numbers, or rejected the plan. Nothing is defined.

## What the student produces (evidence)
Six formal twins, four particle choices for *turn*, four pronoun placements, four relationship completions, four
listening items, a spoken briefing contribution, then a no-consultation quiz with no reused item.

## Width note (for Pass B)
The talk-set chart and the formal-twin chart are full width (five fields per row). The *turn* particle group and
the pronoun group sit as halves in one row — two four-item groups that would each leave a full slot more than half
empty, and both are mechanical decisions.
