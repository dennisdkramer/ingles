# Lesson 20 — design (Pass A)

`recycles:` lesson 6 (present perfect primer — *have + participle* as a chunk), lesson 7 (say/tell, already/never),
lesson 4/5 (the -s and do/does it now contrasts with), lesson 11 (past simple for the finished event),
lesson 15 (the operator grid), lesson 17 (*for* duration; *since* was deliberately deferred to here),
lesson 18 (*get through* — the work that is still going on).
`must not re-teach:` the past perfect (lesson 19 used it inside the 3rd conditional; it returns as narrative in
lesson 36); the passive (lesson 29); the future perfect (not in the map); reported speech (lesson 11/30).

## Core (what the student can do afterwards)
1. **Ask and answer the question that gets a career**: *How long have you been working here?* — *For five years /
   since 2021*.
2. **Build the form**: have/has + been + -ing, for an action that started before now and is still true (or has just
   stopped and left a mark).
3. **Use the present perfect complete**: have/has + participle with *already, just, yet, ever, never* and with
   *for* + length / *since* + point in time.
4. **Choose between the three past-looking forms**: past simple (finished, time stated), present perfect (finished,
   time not stated, result now), present perfect continuous (in progress or recently stopped).
5. **Hold the whole block**: lessons 1-19 as one system — the sentence skeleton, the tenses, the operators, the
   conditions, the phrasal verbs, the prepositions.

## Teaching beats and dependencies
- **The present perfect is a bridge, not a past.** The action starts before now and the sentence ends *at* now.
  That is why it cannot take *yesterday*: *I've sent it* has no date; *I sent it yesterday* has one.
- **Continuous answers "how long / since when"; simple answers "how many / already or not yet".** *I've been
  writing the report* (still at it) against *I've written the report* (done, here it is).
- **since + point, for + length** — the pair deferred from lesson 17, now legal because the form that needs it is
  here. *since 2021, since Monday, since I joined*; *for five years, for two hours*.
- **already / just / yet** are the perfect's adverbs of position in time: *already* (sooner than expected), *just*
  (a moment ago), *yet* (in negatives and questions, still not).
- **The three-way contrast is decided by the time expression, not by the verb.** A stated past time forces the past
  simple; an unstated past with a present result takes the perfect; *how long* takes the continuous.
- **The block closes here.** The last section gathers lessons 1-19 into one page of one-line rules, so the student
  can see the system they now own before the B1 stretch begins.
- **Dependencies**: the participle forms come from lessons 6-7; *for* from 17; the questions from 5.

## Situation
A five-year review and a handover in the same week: a new colleague asks how long the student has been doing each
part of the job, what has already been done, what has not been done yet, and what is still in progress. Every
answer is a perfect form, and one wrong tense moves the event to the wrong week.

## Candidates (three channels)
- **Done — the timeline sort.** Twelve sentences against a timeline: which one is still going, which one just
  finished, which one is a closed past event. The student places, then names the form.
- **Read — the handover.** A note from the person leaving: what has been done, what has not been done yet, what has
  been happening since Monday. The student repairs the tense of four lines.
- **Heard — the review.** Four questions at speed (*How long have you been…? Have you ever…?*), each answered with a
  different form; the student ticks which answer fits.

## Chosen
**Done → Read → Heard.** The timeline first because this lesson's whole difficulty is *where the action sits*, not
how to conjugate; then the handover, where the forms appear in the register the student will actually write; then
the review, because *how long have you been* is heard far more often than it is read.

**First 30 seconds:** two sentences — *I work here since 2021.* / *I've been working here since 2021.* — and the
student clicks the one that is English. Nothing is defined.

## What the student produces (evidence)
Twelve timeline placements, six perfect completions, four *for/since* choices, four three-way choices, four
listening items, a spoken five-year review, then a no-consultation quiz with no reused item, and a written
one-paragraph handover.

## Width note (for Pass B)
The form chart, the timeline contrast and the block-summary chart are full width (five fields per row). The
*for/since* and *already/yet* groups sit as halves in one row: both are four-item groups that would each leave a
full slot more than half empty, and they are the two mechanical decisions of the lesson.
