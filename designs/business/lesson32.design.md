# Lesson 32 — design (Pass A)

`recycles:` lesson 27 (relative clauses — the other cohesion tool), lesson 24 (the report register), lesson 23 (the
meeting), lesson 21 (e-mail prepositions), lesson 26 (hedges that also guide the reader).
`must not re-teach:` relative clauses (27); the passive (29); conditionals (19/25); the e-mail register itself (21/24)
— here only the *links* between sentences.

## Core (what the student can do afterwards)
1. **Link written ideas by function**: contrast (however, on the other hand), consequence (therefore, consequently),
   addition (moreover, furthermore, in addition), reformulation (in fact, that is), closing (to sum up).
2. **Run a spoken meeting with markers**: well, so, right, anyway, I mean, you know, to be honest.
3. **Choose the right weight**: *but* in a chat, *however* in a report; *so* in speech, *therefore* in writing.
4. **Avoid the two failures**: overusing *moreover / furthermore* in speech, and never using any marker at all.
5. **Achieve cohesion by reference**: this, that, the former, the latter — instead of repeating the noun.

## Teaching beats and dependencies
- **A marker is a claim about the argument**, not decoration: *however* says "the next sentence contradicts the
  expectation I just built"; *therefore* says "what follows is a consequence of what I proved".
- **The written and spoken sets are different registers of the same moves**: *so / therefore*, *but / however*,
  *anyway / to sum up*. The lesson pairs them column by column.
- **Overuse is a register error**: *moreover* in a corridor sounds like a legal document; *and then* in a report
  sounds like a transcript.
- **Cohesion by reference is the professional habit**: *this* for the whole previous idea, *the former / the latter*
  for a pair — and the warning that a vague *this* is worse than a repetition.
- **Dependencies**: relative clauses (27) as the other compression tool; the report register (24).

## Situation
A written report and the meeting that produced it: the notes are a list of facts, the report needs links. The
student turns the notes into prose, then chairs the follow-up with spoken markers.

## Candidates (three channels)
- **Read — the report with no links.** Six sentences that sit side by side with no relation; the student inserts the
  marker that states the real relation.
- **Done — the function sort.** Twelve markers sorted by what they claim: contrast, consequence, addition,
  reformulation, closing.
- **Heard — the meeting opening.** Four spoken markers in context (*Right, so, anyway, I mean*); the student ticks
  what each one is doing.

## Chosen
**Read → Done → Heard.** The report first because cohesion is visible when it is missing; then the sort, which
forces the function decision; then the meeting, because the spoken markers are pure sound and timing.

**First 30 seconds:** two sentences on screen — *The figures improved. The timeline slipped.* — the student clicks
the link that shows the contrast. Nothing is defined.

## What the student produces (evidence)
Six marker insertions, twelve function sorts, four register choices, four reference choices, four listening items, a
spoken meeting opening of five sentences, a written paragraph with three links, then a no-consultation quiz with no
reused item.
