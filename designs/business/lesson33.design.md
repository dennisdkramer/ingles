# Lesson 33 — design (Pass A)

`recycles:` lesson 15 (modal proposals), lesson 32 (spoken markers — *right, so, anyway* — as turn-taking tools),
lesson 30 (reporting decisions), lesson 17 (deadlines with *by*), lesson 22 (bringing a topic up).
`must not re-teach:` discourse markers as a system (32); phrasal verbs (18-23); the passive in minutes (29);
reported speech (30) — here only the *decision* is recorded.

## Core (what the student can do afterwards)
1. **Open and chair**: *Let's get started. The aim today is… Can we come back to that?*
2. **Take turns and hold the floor**: *Sorry to interrupt — just on that point… Go ahead. Over to you.*
3. **Propose, second, object, disagree politely**: *I'd suggest… I second that. I see your point, but… I'm not sure that works because…*
4. **Summarise and assign**: *To summarise, we're going with B. Ana owns the deadline; Sam takes the report.*
5. **Use the process vocabulary**: agenda, minutes, action item, owner, consensus, stakeholder, in favour.

## Teaching beats and dependencies
- **A meeting is a turn-taking machine**, and the language is what signals whose turn it is. Everything else is
  content.
- **Proposal → second → objection → decision** is a visible sequence, and each step has a formula. The lesson walks
  it once, then asks the student to run it.
- **Disagreeing is a two-part move**: acknowledge + counter. *I see your point, but the cost…* — the acknowledge is
  not politeness decoration; it is what keeps the meeting alive.
- **The close is the part people skip and the only part that survives**: action + owner + deadline. Taught as a
  three-slot sentence, drilled until automatic.
- **The vocabulary is the register**: *agenda, minutes, action item, owner, consensus, stakeholder* — words that mark
  the student as someone who has run a meeting.
- **Dependencies**: markers (32) for the openings; modals (15) for proposals; *by* (17) for deadlines.

## Situation
A project meeting with a real disagreement: two options on the table, a budget constraint, and four people who all
want to leave with a decision. The student chairs it — opening, taking the proposal, handling the objection,
closing with owners and deadlines — and then writes the three-line summary e-mail.

## Candidates (three channels)
- **Read — the transcript.** Eight turns of a meeting with the chair's lines removed; the student restores the chair.
- **Done — the close.** Six decision facts turned into action items with owner + deadline.
- **Heard — the objection.** Four polite disagreements; the student ticks what is being objected to.

## Chosen
**Read → Done → Heard.** The transcript first because chairing is learned as moves, not words; then the close,
because the three-slot action item is the transferable skill; then the objection, because the polite disagreement is
the hardest thing to hear at speed.

**First 30 seconds:** *We're going with option B.* — the student clicks the missing piece of a usable close: the
owner, the deadline, or the option. Nothing is defined.

## What the student produces (evidence)
Six chair lines restored, four proposal/second formulas, four polite objections, four action items (owner +
deadline), four listening items, a chaired meeting of six sentences, a three-line summary e-mail, then a
no-consultation quiz with no reused item.

## Width note (for Pass B)

process vocabulary sit as halves in one row — two four-item groups that would each leave a full slot more than half
