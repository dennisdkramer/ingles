# Lesson 34 — design (Pass A)

`recycles:` lesson 21 (verb + preposition inside requests), lesson 24 (articles — a missing *the* reads as
carelessness), lesson 29 (passive for bad news without a culprit), lesson 32 (written linkers), lesson 33 (minutes
and action items as e-mail content).
`must not re-teach:` prepositions (21); articles (24); the passive (29); markers (32) — here they are the raw
material of tone.

## Core (what the student can do afterwards)
1. **Climb the ladder of formality**: *Dear Mr Smith / Dear Sam / Hi Sam / Hey guys*; *I am writing to… / Just a
   quick note…*; *I would appreciate it if… / Could you…? / Can you…?*
2. **Read the reader**: internal peer, external client, senior manager — and change exactly what that reader
   requires, not everything.
3. **Write the four hard e-mails**: the request, the follow-up, the apology, the bad news.
4. **Write a subject line that does work**: *Invoice 4471 — payment date*.
5. **Know when formality is the wrong choice** — over-formal to a close colleague reads as distance or irony.

## Teaching beats and dependencies
- **Tone is a set of switches, not a mood**: greeting, contraction, directness, imperative, sentence length,
  sign-off. The lesson treats each as a switch with three positions.
- **The reader decides, not the writer's personality**: the same content is polite to a client and cold to a
  colleague. Reading the reader is the skill.
- **The imperative is the trap**: *Send the file* is neutral inside a team and rude to a client — *Could you send
  the file?* fixes it with one word.
- **Bad news has a shape**: acknowledge → the news → what you can do. Never the news alone.
- **Follow-ups are short and dated**: *Just following up on the invoice I sent on Monday.* No apology for existing.
- **Dependencies**: the request formulas from 15/19/21; passive distance from 29; linkers from 32.

## Situation
One invoice problem, three readers: the colleague who raised it, the client who owes it, and the director who
wants it closed. The student writes the same message three times and then chooses which one to send first.

## Candidates (three channels)
- **Read — the three e-mails.** Same facts, three tones; the student matches each to its reader and finds the one
  switch that is wrong in each.
- **Done — the switch drill.** Six sentences moved up or down the ladder without changing the facts.
- **Heard — the phone follow-up.** Four spoken follow-ups; the student writes the e-mail version.

## Chosen
**Read → Done → Heard.** The three e-mails first because tone is only visible in comparison; then the switch drill,
which isolates each variable; then the phone, because the spoken version is what the student will actually have to
turn into writing.

**First 30 seconds:** *Send me the invoice.* — the student clicks who can receive this sentence as it stands.
Nothing is defined.

## What the student produces (evidence)
Six switch conversions, four reader decisions, four e-mail formulas (request, follow-up, apology, bad news), four
subject lines, four listening-to-writing items, three short e-mails to three readers, then a no-consultation quiz
with no reused item.

## Width note (for Pass B)
The three-level ladder chart and the four-formula chart are full width (five fields per row). The subject-line group
and the reader group sit as halves in one row — two four-item groups that would each leave a full slot more than
half empty.
