# Lesson 11 — design (Pass A)

`sequence:` reported speech, slice 1 of 2 — backshift of the two past forms only, inside the narration frame (slice 2: lesson 30, which owns the full system and must not re-teach this frame).
`must not re-teach:` there is no past perfect yet, so backshift stays present → past here; the past perfect form arrives with the third conditional in lesson 19.

## Core (what the student can do afterwards)
1. **Set a scene and interrupt it**: *We were having lunch when the client called. · I was writing the report at nine.* (past continuous × past simple in one sentence).
2. **Ask what was happening**: *What were you doing at nine? — I was driving.* (question + negative: *I wasn't listening*).
3. **Report what someone said**, in the same narration frame: *She said she was working when the client called. · The client asked if we had finished.*
4. Use the two fields the storytelling needs: the **film set** (*filming, scene, director, camera, actor, script, take*) and the **park/leisure + weather** set (*picnic, swing, slide, sunny, rainy*).

## Teaching beats (per core item) and dependencies
- **Two pasts do two jobs**: the long one (*was/were + -ing*) is the **background**, the short one (simple past) is the **event that cuts it**. The pair is the tense — teaching either alone would leave the student unable to tell a story.
- **Agreement comes from the subject, not the scene**: *I/he/she/it was · you/we/they were* — the same owner rule as lessons 4-5.
- **The question keeps the same skeleton** (lesson 5): *were* moves in front, the *-ing* verb stays: *Were you working? — Yes, I was.*
- **Reported speech is a frame, not a new tense**: *She said (that) …* + the tense moves back one step: present → past, continuous → past continuous. Reported questions keep **statement order** (*asked if we had finished*) — the same law as lesson 3's polite question.
- **Dependencies**: the narration verbs (*call, drive, have lunch, write, finish*) come from lessons 4-7; the field words are glossed before use. **No past perfect** — backshift here is present → past only (the design note in the roadmap).

## Situation
The morning after a project crisis: the student tells a colleague what they were doing when the client called, quotes what the client said, and reports the question the client asked — one continuous story, three tenses' worth of work.

## Candidates (three channels)
- **Heard — the voicemail chain.** Three short messages, each saying what someone was doing when something happened; the student ticks who was doing what.
- **Done — the story strip.** Four pictures of one incident (a meeting, a call, a decision, a late reply); the student writes one sentence per picture, alternating background and event.
- **Read — the incident report.** A short report written in the past; the student rewrites it as speech (*she said…*) and labels background vs event.

## Chosen
**Done (the story strip), entered through Heard.** The pair only makes sense inside a story, and a strip forces the student to choose which half is background on every single line. The report is the best long reading but arrives too late in the level to serve as the entrance.

**First 30 seconds:** one voicemail plays (*I was having lunch when the client called*) and two pictures appear — a lunch table and a phone ringing; the student clicks the one that was already happening. No rule yet.

## What the student produces (evidence)
Six background/event sentence pairs, four past-continuous questions with short answers, four reported statements and two reported questions, a four-sentence story written from the strip and recorded, then a quiz on all four behaviours with no reused item.


## Conflicts, and how I resolved them
- **Reported speech is listed as arriving here *and* in lesson 30.** Resolution: this lesson owns **only the frame inside the narration** — backshift of present → past and past → past continuous, plus *asked if* with statement order. The full system (reporting verbs, time shifts, all tenses) is lesson 30's and is not anticipated.
- **No past perfect exists yet.** Resolution: backshift stops at the two pasts; *the client asked if we had finished* is a single recognition item that shows the next step without teaching it (glossed as lesson 19's form).
- **Two fields in one lesson (film set + park/leisure).** Resolution: the film set carries the **narration** (*we were filming a scene when the director stopped the take*), the park set carries the **weather/background** (*it was raining when we had the picnic*). They are two registers of the same tense, not two topics.

## Shape of the session (what is met, in what order)
1. **O que já estava acontecendo** — the Heard entrance (two pictures, one voicemail), then *was/were + -ing* beside the simple past.
2. **A frase que interrompe** — the *when* pattern, background + event in one sentence.
3. **Perguntar e negar no passado contínuo** — *Were you…? · I wasn't…*.
4. **Contar o que alguém disse** — the reported frame and the reported question.
5. **O set de filmagem e o parque** — the two fields inside a real story.
6. **Lab do som** — *was/were* reduced (*wəz / wər*) and *-ing* without the g, at speed.
7. **Treino** — pairs, questions, reports, story, recording.
8. **Quiz — sem consulta.**

## Notes for the build pass
Portuguese for the explanation; every story sentence carries both tenses (never the background alone); the reported-speech section is a *frame* the student fills with sentences they already own; no vocabulary beyond the two fields.
