# Lesson 18 — design (Pass A)

`sequence:` phrasal verbs, slice 1 of 3 — the machinery and the get family (slice 2: lesson 22, people and talk; slice 3: lesson 23, work, planning and problems; lesson 42 consolidates the strand without new items).

`recycles:` lesson 6 (go out, come back — movement), lesson 7 (say/tell/talk + pick up, put on, take off),
lesson 15 (the operator grid — *have to get through* reuses modals), lesson 14 (gonna/gotta as sound),
lesson 17 (by/until — the deadline the office script runs against).
`must not re-teach:` the passive (lesson 29 owns it — participles are only used); the full phrasal-verb system of
people and talk (lesson 22) and of work/planning/problems (lesson 23) — this lesson installs the machinery and the
*get* family only; reported speech (lesson 11); lessons 6-7 preview the participles and this lesson only uses them.

## Core (what the student can do afterwards)
1. **Recognise the machine**: verb + particle = one new word with a meaning you cannot get from the parts — and
   name the single-word equivalent it usually has (*find out / discover*, *turn down / reject*).
2. **Read the particle**: *up* = completion, *out* = exit or exclusion, *on* = continuation, *off* = separation.
3. **Place the object**: separable verbs take the pronoun inside (*turn it off*, never *turn off it*); inseparable
   ones never split (*get on with it*).
4. **Use the *get* family**: get up, get back to, get along with, get over, get on with, get out of, get through.
5. Say the office day out loud: the printer, the client, the report, the call you have to return.

## Teaching beats and dependencies
- **A phrasal verb is a word, not a phrase.** The test is the meaning: *look after* has nothing to do with *look*.
  The lesson pairs every phrasal verb with its formal synonym so the student can see the choice of register.
- **The particle is the semantic half.** *up* completes (*finish up, set up*), *out* exits or excludes (*get out,
  find out*), *on* continues (*carry on, go on*), *off* separates (*turn off, take off*). Taught as four pictures,
  not as a list.
- **Separability is decided by the object.** A noun object can go inside or after; a **pronoun must go inside**.
  That single line is the whole grammar of separability.
- **The *get* family is seven different verbs** and the lesson keeps them apart by situation, not by translation:
  get up (leave the bed), get back to (reply later), get along with (have a good relationship), get over (recover),
  get on with (continue / also: have a good relationship with work), get out of (avoid), get through (finish /
  reach by phone).
- **The transparent verbs return as one system**: go out, come back, pick up, put on, take off, sit down — the
  student has used them since lessons 6-7; naming them now makes the rule visible.
- **Dependencies**: the office script needs the modals of 15 (*you have to get through to them*) and the deadline
  language of 17 (*by Friday*).

## Situation
A bad office morning: the printer is broken, a client needs an answer, the report is not finished, a colleague is
hard to reach. Everything that goes wrong in that morning is described with a phrasal verb, which is exactly why the
student cannot avoid them.

## Candidates (three channels)
- **Read — the office script.** Four short exchanges where the phrasal verb is the only natural choice; the student
  matches each to its formal equivalent.
- **Done — the pronoun position drill.** The same eight verbs twice: once with a noun object, once with a pronoun,
  so the student feels the difference with their own hands.
- **Heard — the voice note.** A colleague's 30-second voice note using five *get* verbs; the student writes what
  happened, verb by verb.

## Chosen
**Read → Done → Heard.** The script first, because a phrasal verb is learned as a chunk inside a situation, never
as a dictionary entry; then the pronoun drill, which is mechanical and needs the student's hands; then the voice
note, which is the real-world test (nobody slows down for *get through*).

**First 30 seconds:** two sentences on screen — *Please turn off it.* / *Please turn it off.* — and the student
clicks the one that is English. Nothing is defined.

## What the student produces (evidence)
Eight verb↔formal pairs, four particle meanings, six pronoun placements, seven *get* completions, four listening
items, a spoken retelling of the office morning, then a no-consultation quiz with no reused item.

## Width note (for Pass B)
The phrasal-verb chart and the *get* family chart are full width (verb · particle meaning · formal equivalent ·
gloss · audio). The particle pictures and the pronoun drill sit as halves in one row — two four-item groups that
would each leave a full slot more than half empty, and the contrast is the teaching.
