# Lesson 3 (adult) — design (Pass A)

## Fundamental idea taken from `designs/lesson03.design.md`
The wh- words plus `be` are the highest-frequency words in the language and the only tool a beginner needs to
keep a conversation alive; the embedded question (*Can you tell me where the bathroom is?*) is the polite form
that never inverts the order; and the repair chunks (*Sorry, could you say that again?*) are what keeps a
conversation going when the noise wins. The /w/ sound is the price of admission (*what, where, when, why*).

Re-contexted for this track: the questions are the ones an adult actually asks on a Saturday night — *Who's
that guy? What's this song? Where's the bathroom? How much is a beer? What time does it close?* — plus the two
registers of the same question (blunt, polite) and the one place where swearing inside a question is normal
between friends (*Who the hell is that?*) and unacceptable at a bar counter with a stranger.

## Core (what the student can do afterwards)
1. Ask and answer with the wh- set + `be`: *Who's that? Where are you from? What's your name? How's it going?
   Why?* — and recognise that `who` has no /w/ and `how` has no /h/.
2. Ask the **polite** version of anything with statement order: *Can you tell me where the bathroom is?* (never
   "where is the bathroom" inside it), *Do you know what time it closes?*
3. Repair a conversation instead of freezing: *Sorry, could you say that again? / I didn't catch that. / Slower,
   please. / What does "wasted" mean?*
4. Keep apart the two pairs Brazilians merge: **what / that** and **where / there**.
5. Use a question as a register tool: *Who's that?* (neutral) → *Who's that guy?* (curious) → *Who the hell is
   that?* (friends) → *Who the fuck is that?* (friends only, never at a stranger, never at work).

## Teaching beats and dependencies
- **Six words carry the conversation.** what · where · when · who · how · why. Taught as a set with `be`,
  because `be` is the only verb the student owns (lesson 1). **Dependency:** `be` and `it` from lesson 1
  (*What time is it?*).
- **`who` is /huː/, `how` is /haʊ/.** The <w> in *who* is silent and the /h/ in *how* is real; *where/what/when/
  why* start with the /w/ glide (the "uai" shape). Two lines, then six listen-and-tick items.
- **The embedded question keeps statement order.** *Where is the bathroom?* → *Could you tell me where the
  bathroom is?* One model, one chart (blunt | polite), four transformations.
- **Repair is a skill, not a phrase list.** Four chunks, then the student *uses* one on a fast audio line they
  cannot possibly catch.
- **Register inside the question.** *Who the hell is that?* is normal between friends and would end a
  conversation with a stranger; *what the fuck* is stronger than *what the hell*, and both are about who is
  listening. Lesson 1's ladder is reused, not re-taught.
- **what/that, where/there.** Minimal pairs inside sentences: *What's that?* / *That's what I said.* /
  *Where's the bar?* / *There's the bar.*

## Situation
The same night, later: the student arrives at a bar with two friends, loses them, needs the bathroom, wants to
know what a song is, and a guy at the counter says something too fast. Every question on the page is one of
those.

## Three candidate experiences
- **Heard — the bar at 1 a.m.**: five short lines at real speed; one question per line (who/what/where) and a
  repair chunk on the one that is too fast.
- **Done — the polite machine**: turn eight blunt questions into polite ones and back, then choose which version
  to say to a bouncer, a friend and a stranger.
- **Read — a text thread** where two people make plans with wh- questions; the student fills the questions in.

## Chosen
**Heard, entered at the bar.** The persona's stated goal is understanding real speech and self-expression; the
heard entrance is the only one where *not catching it* is part of the exercise, which is exactly what makes the
repair chunks worth learning.

**First 30 seconds:** one line plays fast — *"Hey, are you with the band?"* — and the page offers three
responses: answer it, ask *what?*, or ask *who's that?* All three are correct, and the page says so. Then the
wh- set arrives as the thing the student just needed.

## What the student produces (evidence)
Six question completions with `be`, six listen-and-tick lines, four embedded-question transformations, four
repair responses (including one real fast line), four what/that–where/there choices, one recording of two
questions (one blunt, one polite), and a quiz that re-tests everything on material the training never used.

## Conflicts, and how I resolved them
- **The polite form is a late structure; this is lesson 3.** Resolution: only *Can you tell me…? / Do you
  know…?* with `be` — no other embedded tense — and the page says plainly that the order never changes, which is
  the whole content of the rule.
- **Swearing in a question can read as aggression.** Resolution: every example carries who may hear it, and the
  advice is explicit (*never at a stranger, never at work*). The track asks for the register, not for a licence.
- **/w/ needs a mouth, not a description.** Resolution: two model words plus a mirror instruction (*round the
  lips before the vowel*), then the discrimination is done by ear.

## Shape of the session
1. **Bar, 1 da manhã** — the heard entrance and the three answers.
2. **As seis palavras** — wh- + `be`, the table, six completions.
3. **O /w/ e o /h/** — who/how, the glide, six listen-and-tick.
4. **A pergunta educada** — statement order inside the question, four transformations.
5. **Consertar a conversa** — four repair chunks, one real fast line.
6. **what/that, where/there** — four choices.
7. **Perguntar com força** — the register ladder inside a question.
8. **Treino** — mixed, plus one recording.
9. **Quiz — sem consulta.**
