# Lesson 21 — design (Pass A)

`recycles:` lesson 9 (at/on/in place), lesson 17 (time prepositions, by/until/for/during), lesson 15 (modals in
requests — the e-mail's register), lesson 18 (get back to / sort out — the verbs the e-mail is made of),
lesson 20 (the perfect after *since*).
`must not re-teach:` at/on/in as a place system (lesson 9) or a time system (lesson 17) — used, never re-explained;
phrasal verbs (18/22/23 own them); articles (24); *despite / although* as a full contrast system (only the
preposition + noun/-ing use is taught here).

## Core (what the student can do afterwards)
1. **Say which preposition the verb fixes**: depend on, arrive at, wait for, work on, listen to, deal with,
   apply for, pay for, talk about, look at — the verb owns the preposition, not the translation.
2. **Stop translating the Portuguese preposition**: *depender de* → **depend on**; *esperar por* → **wait for**;
   *casar com* → **marry** (no preposition); *chegar em* → **arrive at** (a place) / **arrive in** (a city).
3. **Use preposition + -ing**: good at, interested in, before/after + -ing, instead of, without.
4. **Hear the family difference**: *agree with* a person, *agree to* a plan; *talk to* someone, *talk about*
   something; *think of* (an idea) against *think about* (a topic); *hear from* (news) against *hear of*
   (existence).
5. Write a coordination e-mail that is correct at every preposition.

## Teaching beats and dependencies
- **The unit is the verb + preposition, memorised as one word.** The lesson never asks "which preposition?" — it
  asks "which verb?", because the verb already answered.
- **The Portuguese preposition is a false friend at high frequency.** The six that cost the most are drilled first:
  depend on, wait for, arrive at/in, listen to, pay for, marry (zero).
- **A preposition before a verb takes -ing.** *Good at*, *interested in*, *before/after*, *instead of*, *without* —
  one rule, five frequent uses.
- **The particle changes the relationship, not the verb**: *agree with* (person) / *agree to* (plan); *talk to*
  (interlocutor) / *talk about* (topic). Taught as pairs, never as lists.
- **Cover prepositions** (*despite, regarding, between/among, toward, throughout*) belong to the e-mail register:
  they replace a whole clause and make the writing shorter.
- **Dependencies**: the e-mail's requests use the modals of 15; the deadline language uses 17.

## Situation
One meeting to organise by e-mail: the room, the date, the draft, the client's answer that everything depends on.
The student writes four lines of the thread and repairs four prepositions in a colleague's draft.

## Candidates (three channels)
- **Read — the e-mail thread.** Four messages, each carrying a verb + preposition unit and one deadline; the
  student finds the wrong preposition in the colleague's draft.
- **Done — the unit builder.** Twenty verb + preposition pairs completed from the verb, not from the Portuguese.
- **Heard — the call.** Four short lines where the preposition decides the meaning (*agree with* / *agree to*,
  *hear from* / *hear of*); the student ticks what was meant.

## Chosen
**Read → Done → Heard.** The thread first because the register is the point (this is the lesson about e-mail);
then the unit builder, which is mechanical and needs repetition; then the call, where the pairs that change meaning
are audible.

**First 30 seconds:** *It depends of the client.* — the student fixes the sentence by clicking the correct
preposition. Nothing is defined.

## What the student produces (evidence)
Six units completed from the verb, four Portuguese-interference repairs, four *-ing* completions, four family
choices (agree with/to, talk to/about), four listening items, a written e-mail of four lines, then a
no-consultation quiz with no reused item.

## Width note (for Pass B)
The unit chart and the cover-preposition chart are full width (five fields per row). The *-ing* group and the
family pairs sit as halves in one row — two four-item groups that would each leave a full slot more than half
empty, and the contrast between the two families is the teaching.
