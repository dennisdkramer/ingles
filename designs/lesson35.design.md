# Lesson 35 — design (Pass A)

`recycles:` lesson 15 (modals of offer/permission), lesson 19 (the hypothetical as a softener), lesson 26 (hedges),
lesson 29 (passive for bad news without a culprit), lesson 34 (bad-news e-mail shape: acknowledge → news →
alternative).
`must not re-teach:` the e-mail formulas (34); conditionals (19/25); the passive (29); *should have* (26).

## Core (what the student can do afterwards)
1. **Run the service script**: greet → identify the problem → own it → fix it → close — in that order, every time.
2. **De-escalate**: *I understand your concern. Let me see what I can do.* Empathy before information.
3. **Say no without losing the customer**: the partial yes — *What I can do today is…*
4. **Escalate properly**: *I'll escalate this to senior management and come back to you by Friday* — with a name, a
   path and a date.
5. **Close the loop**: the follow-up that turns a complaint into retention.

## Teaching beats and dependencies
- **Empathy is a sentence, not a feeling**: the first move is always to name the other person's experience, before
  any fact. Skipping it is what makes a correct answer sound cold.
- **Own it without confessing**: *I'm sorry this happened* — responsibility for the experience, not necessarily for
  the fault. That distinction protects the company and satisfies the customer.
- **The partial yes is the professional no**: never *no* alone; always *no, but here is what I can do today*.
- **Escalation is a promise with a date**: a promise without a date is a second complaint.
- **The script is a sequence, and the sequence is the lesson**: each stage has one job, and skipping a stage is
  audible even when the words are right.
- **Dependencies**: the modal offers from 15; the hypothetical from 19; the bad-news shape from 34.

## Situation
A delayed order and an angry client on the phone: the student takes the call, de-escalates, offers the partial
solution, escalates what cannot be solved, and writes the follow-up e-mail.

## Candidates (three channels)
- **Read — the call transcript.** Eight turns with the agent's empathy lines removed; the student restores the
  stage.
- **Done — the partial yes.** Six impossible requests turned into *what I can do today*.
- **Heard — the escalation.** Four lines where the agent promises something; the student ticks whether the promise
  has a date.

## Chosen
**Read → Done → Heard.** The transcript first because the script is a sequence; then the partial yes, which is the
one skill that survives every call; then the escalation, because a promise without a date is the most common
failure.

**First 30 seconds:** *That's not our problem.* — the student clicks what is wrong with it and what the first
sentence should do instead. Nothing is defined.

## What the student produces (evidence)
Eight stage labels from the transcript, four empathy lines, four partial-yes conversions, four escalation items,
four listening items, a spoken call of six turns, a follow-up e-mail, then a no-consultation quiz with no reused
item.
