# Lesson 35 — design (Pass A)

## Core (what the student can do afterwards)
1. Acknowledge an angry customer without admitting fault: *I can see why that's frustrating, and I want to fix it.*
2. Buy time honestly instead of inventing an answer: *Let me check that and come back to you within the hour.*
3. Offer two real choices rather than one ultimatum.
4. Escalate cleanly — say what happens next, to whom, and when.

## Situation
A client's delivery is late. The customer is on the phone, already annoyed, and has been told "it's on the way" twice.

## Candidates
- **Done — the three-move call.** Three fixed moves in order (acknowledge → diagnose → offer). Each turn the student writes one line; the customer's next line is pre-written for each of three reply styles, so the student sees the consequence of their register.
- **Heard — rate the four calls.** Four short recordings of the same situation handled differently; the student rates which one de-escalates and says why.
- **Read — repair the chat.** A transcript where three lines pour petrol on the fire; rewrite them so the same information stops being a fight.

## Chosen
**Rate the four calls → repair the chat → the three-move call.** At B2 the fastest progress comes from *comparing* choices and seeing their effect, so the lesson starts with the student's judgement (which is already good in Portuguese) and only then asks them to produce. The three-move call comes last because it needs the vocabulary the first two beats install.

**First 30 seconds:** an angry voicemail plays; three replies appear; the student picks the one they would want to hear if they were the customer — then hears what each reply actually leads to.

## What the student produces (evidence)
The rating choices with a written justification, three repaired lines, three written call turns, a recorded 45-second call opening, and a quiz on all four core behaviours.

## Conflicts, and how I resolved them
- **The most natural vehicle here is a branching simulation (Tier 2) with consequences.** I am proposing it, not building it: the effect above is achieved with pre-written consequences per reply style, which is authorable today and honest about being finite.
- **"Escalate", "retention", "service recovery" invite corporate register.** Resolved by the track's register rule: confident business-casual, no "we sincerely apologise for any inconvenience". The super-formal variants are *shown once* so the student recognises them, never drilled.
- **An angry-customer script can tip into performance.** Resolution: the student always writes before they speak; recording comes after the written turn is solid.

## Notes for the build pass
English throughout except one Portuguese note on why Brazilians over-apologise in service English (it lands better in the student's own language). Audio for all four calls and the voicemail — the whole lesson rests on hearing tone.
