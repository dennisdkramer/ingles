# Lesson 15 — design (Pass A)

`sequence:` modals, slice 1 of 3 — the operators and their core meanings (slice 2: lesson 26, deduction and possibility; consolidation: lesson 41).

`recycles:` lesson 14 (gonna / hafta / gotta as *sound*, contractions can't / won't), lesson 10 (weak vowel, dark L),
lesson 8 (times and frequency), lesson 12 (prices, booking figures), lesson 13 (choosing between options), lesson 6
(leave / come / go).
`must not re-teach:` possibility and deduction — *might be, may be, could be, must have, can't have, should have*
(lesson 26 owns them); the four conditionals (lesson 19); backshift / reporting (lesson 11); *would like / Would you
mind…?* as the full polite-request system (lesson 19) — here *would* appears only inside offers and requests, as one
line in the grid.

## Core (what the student can do afterwards)
1. **Decide with one grid**: ability (*can*), permission (*can / could / may*), obligation (*have to / has to / must /
   gotta*), advice (*should / shouldn't / ought to*), offers and requests (*will / would / shall / can / could*).
2. **Obey the single rule**: modal + base verb — no *to*, no *-s*, no *do/does*, one negative per modal.
3. **Choose a future**: *will* (decided now), *be going to* (plan already made), present continuous (fixed
   arrangement with a time) — and hear why the third one sounds like a commitment.
4. **Hear the difference that changes the meaning**: weak *can* vs strong *can't*; the dark L of *will*.
5. Plan a trip out loud: leave early, visit the museum, book tickets in advance.

## Teaching beats (per core item) and dependencies
- **Meaning before form.** The grid is organised by *what the speaker is doing* (I can / I'm allowed / I must / I
  should / I'll), not by the word. One column per job, one modal per job as the default, the alternatives beside it.
- **The rule is one sentence and it is mechanical**: after a modal the verb arrives naked. The student already knows
  the third-person *-s* (lesson 4) and *do/does* (lesson 5), so the lesson shows the modal *deleting* both.
- **Obligation has a source.** *have to* = someone else requires it (the airline, the client); *must* = I require it
  (my own decision, or a rule I state); *gotta* = the same as *have to* in speech, already met as sound in 14.
- **Advice is not obligation**: *should* leaves the choice with the listener; the note makes that difference audible,
  because a Brazilian *deve* can land as an order in English.
- **The future is three degrees of commitment**, and the difference is *when the decision was made*: now (*will*),
  before now (*going to*), already fixed with another person and a clock (present continuous). The test question is
  "is it in the calendar with a time?"
- **Dependencies**: *can't* and *won't* arrive contracted from lesson 14; *hafta / gotta* are heard there and used
  here as forms; the time expressions (*at three, on Friday*) come from lessons 8 and 17 — used, not taught.

## Situation
Two colleagues planning a short trip to a conference: one has the prices and the times, the other has the
constraints (the budget, the client call, the museum that closes at five). Everything the lesson installs is what
they say to each other before anything is booked.

## Candidates (three channels)
- **Heard — the two readings of *can*.** Six short lines at speed; the student ticks *can* or *can't*, then hears
  the same six slowly. The vowel, not the *-n't*, is the signal in fast speech.
- **Done — the plan under constraints.** A short brief (budget, times, one fixed meeting) and eight sentences to
  complete with the right operator; the student then turns three of them into a spoken plan.
- **Read — the trip dialogue.** Two people negotiating: permission, obligation, advice, offer, and a fixed
  arrangement. The student marks each line with its job, then rewrites two lines more formally.

## Chosen
**Heard → Read → Done, with the future last.** The ear first (because *can / can't* is the one contrast that
silently reverses a sentence), then the dialogue as the carrier that shows all five jobs in one situation, then the
production under constraints. The future closes the lesson because it is the only part that is grammar of *time*
rather than of *attitude*, and it needs the dialogue's fixed arrangement as its example.

**First 30 seconds:** one line plays — *We can't leave early, we have to book first.* — and the student clicks
whether leaving early is possible. Nothing is defined yet.

## What the student produces (evidence)
Six listen-and-decide items (*can / can't*), a marked-up dialogue (five jobs), eight operator completions under a
written brief, four future choices, two spoken plans (one informal to a colleague, one formal to a client), then a
no-consultation quiz on all five behaviours with no reused item.

## Width note (for Pass B)
The operator grid, the future chart and the dialogue are full-width blocks: each row carries four to five fields
(modal · job · example · gloss · audio) and any half would wrap. Treino and Quiz split into halves only where two
short answer groups sit side by side (operator choice beside future choice) — the stated reason is *adjacent
compressible blocks*, each of which would leave a full slot more than half empty.
