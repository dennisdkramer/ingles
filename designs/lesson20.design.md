# Lesson 20 — design (Pass A)

## Core (what the student can do afterwards)
1. Answer *how long have you been…?* about their own work, with *for* and *since*.
2. Explain a present symptom with an unfinished past: *I've been working on the report — that's why I'm late.*
3. Choose between the finished and the unfinished version when the difference matters: *I've read it* / *I've been reading it*.
4. Close the block by explaining one thing they own to someone else.

## Situation
A Monday stand-up. Two things are not done yet, and the student has to say why without sounding like an excuse. A manager follows up with "how long have you been on it?".

## Candidates
- **Said — the stand-up.** The student records a 40-second update that must contain three targets; then hears the manager's follow-up question and answers it live.
- **Seen — the timeline wall.** Six timeline cards (started and still going / finished / repeated) are matched to six sentences; the student then places their own three tasks on the wall.
- **Read — the status inbox.** Four short status emails where the tenses contradict the timelines; find and fix the three that lie.

## Chosen
**The stand-up, with the timeline wall as the noticing step.** At A2 the student can already produce; what they lack is the *reason* to choose the continuous, and a status meeting is precisely the moment where the tense carries meaning (unfinished → nothing to show yet, but progress is real). The inbox is a good third beat and stays as practice.

**First 30 seconds:** a voice asks *"Why isn't the report ready?"* and the student picks the honest reply from three — one uses the past simple, one the perfect, one the continuous. No rule yet; just a choice they can feel.

## What the student produces (evidence)
A recorded 40-second update, the timeline matching, gap-fills with *for / since / just / already / yet*, choice items distinguishing perfect from continuous, the inbox repairs, and a final section where they explain one earlier lesson in their own words (the handover the entry asks for).

## Conflicts, and how I resolved them
- **The entry asks this lesson to finish the present perfect, teach the perfect continuous, and carry the review of lessons 1-19.** Three lessons in one slot. Resolution: the review is *one* short section ("explain one thing you own", recorded, 60 seconds) instead of a carousel; the present perfect is finished here only where it is needed to make the contrast visible. Flagged: the review deserves its own lesson if the owner wants it examined rather than rehearsed.
- **Recording twice (update + explainer) is a lot of talking for one session.** Resolution: the explainer is optional homework in the same recorded shape.
- **A stand-up is business-heavy; the entry's own examples are sports-team achievements.** Resolved in favour of the student's actual week: the map's achievement vocabulary is kept as the quiz's second half (results and milestones), not as the lesson's spine.

## Notes for the build pass
Bilingual by rule of thumb: instructions in English, Portuguese for the two places where the contrast is genuinely counter-intuitive (finished vs unfinished; *for* vs *since*). No table larger than four rows — this lesson is spoken.
