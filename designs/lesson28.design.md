# Lesson 28 — design (Pass A)

`recycles:` lesson 4 (the -ing form), lesson 21 (preposition + -ing — now systematised, not re-taught),
lesson 25 (would rather + bare verb — the same "bare verb" slot as make/let), lesson 24 (the -ing as a subject in a
report line), lesson 20 (the perfect continuous — the -ing that follows *been*).
`must not re-teach:` the continuous tenses (4/11/20); the preposition + -ing list as new material (lesson 21 owns
*good at / interested in / before / after / instead of* — here they are the rule applied); the passive (29).

## Core (what the student can do afterwards)
1. **Know which form the verb demands**: enjoy / mind / avoid / finish / suggest + -ing; want / decide / hope /
   plan / agree / refuse + to + base. Memorise the verb, not the translation.
2. **Hear the meaning move** in the pairs: stop, remember, forget, try, need, go on.
3. **Use the infinitive of purpose**: *To negotiate effectively, you need to listen* — and *in order to* as its
   formal twin.
4. **Drop the *to* after make / let / help**: *make them wait*, *let me check*, *help us finish*.
5. **Put -ing in the subject slot**: *Learning a new language improves memory* — and keep the verb singular.

## Teaching beats and dependencies
- **The pattern belongs to the verb, not to the speaker.** There is no meaning rule that predicts *enjoy + -ing*;
  there is a memory list, and the lesson gives the two lists as two pictures (an -ing verb is an activity you are
  *in*; a *to* verb is a target you are *towards*).
- **The pairs are two different actions**, not two forms of one: *stop smoking* (quit) against *stop to smoke*
  (pause in order to smoke). The lesson always gives the situation, because the form alone does not decide.
- **try** is the pair that Portuguese gets wrong: *try to open* (attempt) against *try opening* (experiment).
- **need** follows the same logic as the passive: *the car needs washing* = *needs to be washed*.
- **make / let / help take the bare verb** — the same slot *would rather* uses (lesson 25), which makes the rule
  recognisable rather than new.
- **The -ing subject takes a singular verb** — the detail that shows control in writing.
- **Dependencies**: the -ing form from 4; the preposition list from 21; the bare-verb slot from 25.

## Situation
A training plan for the team: what people enjoy, what they avoid, what they have decided to do, and what the
manager will make them practise. The student builds the plan out loud and then writes the one-paragraph proposal.

## Candidates (three channels)
- **Read — the training proposal.** Six sentences, each with the verb form blanked; the student restores the form
  the verb demands.
- **Done — the pair sort.** Eight sentences with *stop / remember / forget / try*, each with a situation; the student
  decides which action is meant.
- **Heard — the manager's brief.** Four spoken lines with *make / let / help*; the student writes what each person
  is expected to do.

## Chosen
**Read → Done → Heard.** The proposal first because the pattern is learned inside the register the student will
write; then the pairs, which are the only place meaning is at stake; then the brief, because *make / let / help*
are heard far more than written.

**First 30 seconds:** *She stopped to smoke.* against *She stopped smoking.* — the student clicks which one means
she quit. Nothing is defined.

## What the student produces (evidence)
Six pattern completions, four pair decisions, four preposition/-ing completions, four make/let/help items, four
listening items, a spoken training plan, a written proposal paragraph, then a no-consultation quiz with no reused
item.
