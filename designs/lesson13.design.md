# Lesson 13 — design (Pass A)

## Core (what the student can do afterwards)
1. Compare two things and choose between them: *cheaper, more expensive, faster, better, worse* + **than**.
2. Say the extreme: *the cheapest, the most efficient, one of the best options*.
3. Say **how much** the difference is: *much cheaper, a little more expensive, twice as fast, 25% cheaper* (the numbers of lesson 12).
4. State a decision out loud: *I'd go with the second one — it's slightly more expensive but twice as fast.*

## Teaching beats (per core item) and dependencies
- **Short words take endings, long words take a word**: one-syllable → *-er/-est* (*cheap → cheaper → the cheapest*), two syllables in *-y* behave like short (*easy → easier*), the rest → *more/most* (*more expensive*). This is a spelling-and-length decision, not a meaning decision.
- **Irregulars are five words, learned as words**: *good → better → best · bad → worse → worst · far → further → furthest*.
- **The comparison needs its other half**: *than* for the comparative, *the* for the superlative, *as…as* for equality, *not as…as* and *less/least* for the negative side. A comparative without *than* is a sentence waiting for a listener.
- **Intensifiers carry the business judgement**: *much / a lot / slightly / a little / twice* — saying *much cheaper* and *slightly cheaper* is the difference between a recommendation and a warning. Dependencies: lesson 12's numbers make *25% cheaper* and *twice as fast* possible.
- **Decision language is the payoff**: *I'd go with…, it's the best option we have, it's cheaper but slower* — the lesson must end in a recommendation, not in an adjective table.

## Situation
A supplier meeting: three quotes on the table (price, delivery time, quality), and the student has to recommend one out loud to a manager who will ask "why that one?".

## Candidates (three channels)
- **Heard — the three quotes.** Three short audio pitches; the student ticks which is cheapest / fastest / best quality.
- **Done — the decision table.** A table of three options with numbers; the student writes the comparison sentences and then the recommendation.
- **Read — the review.** Three short customer reviews of the same product; the student extracts the comparisons and finds the contradiction between two of them.

## Chosen
**Done (the decision table), entered through Heard.** The table forces the student to *use* the numbers from lesson 12 inside a comparison, which is where the forms stop being grammar and start being a decision. The reviews are good reading but the level of the prose at this point costs more than the forms are worth.

**First 30 seconds:** three quotes are read fast; the student clicks the one that was called *cheapest*. No rule yet — just the sound of *-est* doing its job.

## Shape of the session (what is met, in what order)
1. **Três orçamentos** — the Heard entrance, then the short/long split.
2. **A outra metade** — *than · the · as…as · not as…as*.
3. **Quanto maior?** — intensifiers + lesson 12's numbers (*25% cheaper, twice as fast*).
4. **Os cinco irregulares** — good/bad/far, as words.
5. **A decisão** — the recommendation frames, on the real table.
6. **Lab do som** — *-er* and *-est* do not add a syllable to short words; *than* with the voiced *th*.
7. **Treino** — comparisons, superlatives, intensifiers, the recommendation, recording.
8. **Quiz — sem consulta.**

## Notes for the build pass
Portuguese for the explanation; every comparison appears with its second half; the numbers come from lesson 12 and are not re-taught; the last teaching section is the decision, not another table.
