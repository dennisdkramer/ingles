# Lesson 12 — design (Pass A)

## Core (what the student can do afterwards)
1. Say any number up to a million and read a price out loud: **$1,250** = *twelve fifty* or *one thousand two hundred fifty*; **R$ 39,90** = *thirty-nine ninety*.
2. Read percentages, decimals and fractions aloud: *25% = twenty-five percent · 2.5 = two point five · a half, a third, three quarters*.
3. Say dates, years and ranges: *on July 5th · in 2020 · the 1990s · between 15 and 20 · up to 30*.
4. **Report a figure** instead of only pronouncing it: *Sales rose by 10% · revenue fell to 250 · up from 180 · down to 30*.

## Teaching beats (per core item) and dependencies
- **Big numbers are built in threes** — *two hundred fifty*, *one thousand*, *two thousand five hundred*, *a million* — and the comma is the breath, not a pause for thought.
- **Prices have a spoken twin**: the full reading exists and is what a contract demands; the short one (*twelve fifty*) is what a meeting says. Both are taught, and the choice is register, not correctness.
- **Percent, decimal, fraction, date, phone, e-mail, URL are six different scripts** — each has a fixed spoken shape (*twenty-five percent · two point five · three quarters · July fifth · double oh five · at · dot · underscore · dash*), so the student says them without inventing.
- **A number alone is not a sentence**: the reporting verbs (*rise, fall, increase, decrease*) plus the three prepositions that carry the direction — **by** (how much), **to** (the end point), **from** (the start). This is where the numbers become business speech, and it is why the lesson earns its place in a business track.
- **Dependencies**: *up/down*, numbers 1-100 and *hour* are assumed from everyday life; *price, cost, budget, discount, revenue, sales, target, invoice, receipt, deadline* are glossed here because they are the nouns the figures attach to.

## Situation
A quarterly review: the student reads figures off a slide aloud (some written with commas, one as a price, one as a percentage), then reports the movement to the client — *we went up from 180 to 250, that's a 39% rise* — without hesitating on a single digit.

## Candidates (three channels)
- **Heard — the earnings call.** Six short figure statements (audio only); the student writes what they heard as digits.
- **Done — the slide deck.** Three slides with real-looking data; the student says each figure aloud and writes one reporting sentence per slide.
- **Read — the invoice.** A page of prices, dates and a discount; the student reads it out and spots the two figures that do not match the total.

## Chosen
**Done (the slide deck), entered through Heard.** The bottleneck is production under time pressure: hearing a figure and writing it, then saying a figure and being understood. The invoice is the best carrier for prices and dates and is used inside the deck as one of its slides; as an entrance it is too dense to speak from.

**First 30 seconds:** a voice says *one thousand two hundred fifty* and four numbers stand on the slide; the student clicks the one they heard. No rule, no table.

## Conflicts, and how I resolved them
- **Numbers 1-100 are not taught anywhere in the plan** (they are assumed). Resolution: the lesson **drills only the shapes that go wrong** (hundreds, thousands, the comma, *and* omitted), and the first section is a fast check rather than a from-zero count.
- **Dates overlap lesson 17 (time prepositions) and lesson 8 (the clock).** Resolution: this lesson owns the **reading of a date/year** (*July 5th, in 2020, the 1990s*); the prepositions stay in 17 and the clock in 8, and both are used only inside example sentences.
- **Rise/fall could be read as lesson 13's comparison.** Resolution: comparison (*cheaper, faster*) is 13's; here the verbs only report a **direction and an amount** (*by / to / from*), with no comparative form required.

## Shape of the session (what is met, in what order)
1. **A voz e o número** — the Heard entrance: a figure is spoken, four are on the slide, one click.
2. **Grandes números e preços** — the three-group build, the comma, the two readings of a price.
3. **Porcentagem, decimal, fração** — the three scripts, each with its own shape.
4. **Datas, anos, telefone, e-mail** — the reading scripts a business card demands.
5. **O número vira frase** — *rise / fall* + *by / to / from*, on a real slide.
6. **Lab do som** — *-teen* vs *-ty* (thirteen/thirty), the *th* of *thousand*, and the weak *and*.
7. **Treino** — dictation, reading aloud, reporting, recording.
8. **Quiz — sem consulta.**
