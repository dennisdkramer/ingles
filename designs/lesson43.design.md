# Lesson 43 — design (Pass A)

`recycles:` the whole system — 4-7 and 11 (the tenses a working day actually uses), 12 (numbers said fast),
15/26 (modals: request, offer, deduction), 19/25 (the conditional offer), 29 (passive where the doer should stay
quiet), 33 (decisions, owners, dates out of a meeting), 34 (the e-mail ladder), 35 (the service/no under pressure),
36 (the story that gets told in a lift), 37 (the corridor), 38 (disagreeing in the room), 41 (speed), 42 (the spoken
verb and its written twin).
`must not re-teach:` any single system above. **This is integration, not instruction**: every section is a room in
one day, and the language arrives because the day demands it. Nothing new is introduced.

## Core (what the student can do afterwards)
1. **Carry one working day on the language alone** — stand-up, client call, lunch, negotiation, problem, written
   follow-up — without dropping into Portuguese or into silence.
2. **Make information travel**: the note taken at 9 a.m. is used at 11, the promise made at 11 constrains the
   negotiation at 2, the deal done at 2 is the content of the e-mail at 4.30.
3. **Change register on purpose inside the day**: peer-to-peer at lunch, business-casual with the client, written
   formality in the follow-up — and switch back without losing the thread.
4. **Write the follow-up that closes the day**: decisions, owners, dates, and nothing that was not agreed.
5. **Notice their own errors under integration** and log three of them.

## Teaching beats and dependencies
- **Integration is the last skill and the only honest test.** Every earlier lesson trained one thing at a time; the
  day trains the switching, which is what actually fails in a real Tuesday. So the page never says *today we study*:
  it says *it is 08:50*.
- **Information has to travel or the day is not real.** The stand-up notes are input, not decoration: two of the
  later items cannot be answered without them. That is the mechanic that makes the page a simulation instead of a
  quiz with a story on top.
- **Register switching is a decision with a visible marker**, not a mood: *Hey, quick one* to a peer at lunch, *Do you
  have five minutes?* to the same person in a meeting, *I'm writing to confirm* in the e-mail. The page shows the
  same content at three settings and lets the student hear the seam.
- **The negotiation here is enactment, not technique.** The moves (*if you can…, we can…*; confirm what was agreed)
  are lesson 47's and are used, not taught — a signpost says so. What is trained here is holding them while the rest
  of the day is still running.
- **Swearing belongs to one room and one register.** At lunch, with a peer, *we're screwed* is normal adult
  peer-to-peer speech; in the client call the same fact is *we're in a difficult position*. Taught as a sidenote on
  the lunch block, in its place, with the rule that makes it safe: same fact, different room.
- **The follow-up is where the day is judged.** A summary with no owner and no date is a description, not a
  decision; and anything in it that was not agreed becomes next week's argument.
- **The error log hands over to 44**: under load the student's real errors surface, and they are the syllabus now.

## Situation
One Tuesday, one project, one client, one deadline that moves. 08:50 stand-up · 10:30 client call · 12:30 lunch ·
14:00 the trade · 15:30 the number that does not add up · 16:30 the follow-up. A single continuous business day, with
the clock as the only structure.

## Candidates (three channels)
- **Done — the whole day as one document.** Six clock-stamped blocks, each opening with what the last one left behind
  and closing with what this one hands forward. The continuity is the pedagogy and cannot be got any other way.
- **Said — the day as a single recording.** The student speaks every spoken turn of the day in one take, with only
  the clock and the incoming note on screen. Highest fidelity, highest pressure.
- **Read — a transcript of somebody else's Tuesday.** A full day in the third person, with the seams marked, as a
  model to audit before the student runs their own.

## Chosen
**Done, with Said inside it.** The day is one artifact: each block ends by handing something forward, so the page
behaves like a working day rather than a lesson. The spoken turns sit inside their blocks — the call at 10:30 is
spoken, the stand-up at 08:50 is spoken — because separating the speaking out would destroy exactly the switching
that this lesson exists to train. The transcript is rejected for the same reason: reading someone else's fluent day
teaches admiration, not the seam.

**First 30 seconds:** *08:50. Stand-up. You have forty seconds and five things to report.* A clock starts. The list
is on screen in note form only — no sentences to read aloud — and the student has to say it. Whatever they cannot say
is the day's first honest datum. Nothing is defined.

## What the student produces (evidence)
A spoken 40-second stand-up from notes, six notes carried forward, three register-matched openings for the same
request, a nine-turn client call with the promise recorded, one register sidenote applied (same fact, two rooms),
four conditional offers and one confirmed agreement, one decision under incomplete information, a written follow-up
with decisions/owners/dates, three logged personal errors, then a no-consultation quiz drawn across the whole day
that reuses no training item.

## Width note (for Pass B)
Every clock block is a full-width `.panel` — the day is chronological and a two-column day is not a day. Within the
blocks: the register chart is full width (same content / three settings / the marker that shows the seam), the
client call is a full-width `.dialog`, and the follow-up is a full-width card with the three fields the e-mail must
contain. Only two drills are halves — the conditional offers and the "same fact, two rooms" pairs — because each is
four items and would otherwise leave a full slot more than half empty.
