# Lesson 5 (kids) — design (Pass A)

`band: kids · L05 · C05 · registers: 2b (friends and classmates) · 2a (a new classmate, the coach, a teacher) · contexts: the first week at a new school, the class group chat, the football team's group, the school app`

## Fundamental idea taken from `designs/kids/lesson04.design.md` and its parent `designs/lesson05.design.md`
The parent design's idea: **every sentence is a skeleton** — `[dono] [modo] [verbo] [resto]` — and the `-s` **muda de casa**: when the mode slot is empty the verb carries it (*she works*); when the mode is filled with the helper **do/does**, the verb drops it (*does she work? · she doesn't work*). Negation is a *mode*, not a word after the verb; short answers **stop at the helper** (*Yes, I do · No, she doesn't*); and *do* the helper must be told apart from *do* the verb (*What do you do?*). Kids keep the idea and drop the client call, the analyst, the tools and the "scope"; the skeleton is applied to a new classmate's routine — clubs, teams, the school app.

## Core (what the student can do afterwards)
1. Ask anything about someone's routine with **do / does** and put the `-s` in the right house: *Does she play in the school team? · Do you use the school app?*
2. Say no without saying "no": *I don't play on Fridays. · She doesn't use the app.*
3. Answer **short and right**: *Yes, I do · No, she doesn't* — instead of "Yes, I play" or the classic *"Yes, I does"*.
4. Tell **the helper do from the verb do**: *What do you do after school? — I play football.*

## Teaching beats (per core item) and dependencies
- **The skeleton, named once.** `[dono] [modo] [verbo] [resto]`. Empty mode → the verb carries the marker (*she plays*). Filled mode (*do / does*) → the marker moves to the mode and the verb goes back to base (*does she play*). The page says out loud that every later machine reuses this same slot, so the student meets the promise and not just a rule.
- **The -s moves house, it is never doubled.** *She plays* → *Does she play?* / *She doesn't play.* The verb drops it, the helper takes it. The Brazilian error is exactly the doubling (*"Does she plays?"*), so the move is shown as a move, not as a second rule. **Dependency:** lesson 4 installed the -s and *its owner*; this lesson only relocates it.
- **Negation is a mode, not a word put after the verb.** *I don't play* — never *"I not play"* — and the negative is where the -s *visibly* disappears from the verb, which is the cheapest proof that the move is real.
- **Short answers stop at the helper.** *Yes, I do · No, she doesn't.* Repeating the whole verb is understandable but is not what a native answers, and *"Yes, I does"* / *"No, she don't"* is the mismatch this lesson exists to kill.
- **The verb *do* must be shown, not mentioned.** *What do you do after school?* has two *do* doing two jobs in one sentence, and it is the most common first-week question between kids.
- **Silly stakes.** First week at a new school: everyone asks what you do, and a "Yes, I does" travels through the group chat faster than any correct sentence. Asking well and answering short is how you sound like you belong on day one.

## Situation
First week at the new school. The student wants to know who plays in the team, who uses the school app, who goes to the robotics club, and must answer the same questions back — in the corridor, in the class group chat, and to the coach. Three answers arrive fast and swallowed (*D'you play? · Does she? · I dunno*), so the ear has to find the helper before the mouth uses it.

## Candidates (three channels)
- **Heard — the corridor interview.** Six fast yes/no questions as audio, some about the student, some about a classmate; the student ticks who each one is about and then says the short answer out loud.
- **Done — the tick table.** A table of four classmates with ticks for three activities; the student writes the question and the short answer from the ticks. This is the move of the -s made mechanical.
- **Read — the club board.** A short notice where every line is a bare statement (*Ana plays on Tuesdays*); the student turns statements into questions and negatives. Best reading material, but it produces writing, not speech.

## Chosen
**Heard → Done.** *Do/does* is above all a listening problem: the helper is unstressed and swallowed (*D'you play?*), so the ear must find it before the mouth can use it — and the tick table then forces the -s to move mechanically. The club board is used later only as the material the recordings are about.

**First 30 seconds:** three questions play fast, back to back, and the student only ticks **who each one is about** — *eu* or *outra pessoa*. Nothing is explained yet; the -s is heard before it is named.

## What the student produces (evidence)
Six questions written from ticks, six negatives, six short answers, four questions of their own written as a "first week interview", one listen-and-tick on the swallowed helper, three recorded exchanges, then a quiz on all four behaviours with none of the training items.


## Conflicts, and how I resolved them
- **The reduced *do you* (d'you) belongs to connected speech (lesson 14).** Resolution: it is **heard** here and glossed in one note, never taught as a rule; lesson 14 explains why it shrinks.
- **Short answers overlap with lesson 3's questions.** Resolution: lesson 3 owns wh- + `be`; this lesson owns **yes/no with do**, and says the difference in one line instead of re-teaching the wh- set.
- **The roadmap previews the other operators (can, will, should) as the same slot.** Resolution: they are named in a **single preview line** (the mode slot takes them too), never drilled — lesson 15 owns the modal set.
- **`does` with its vowel and `don't` without the *t* are sounds, not grammar.** Resolution: both get a row in the sound lab, because they are the sound *of this lesson's own helper*; the consonant map stays lesson 10's.

## Shape of the session (what is met, in what order)
1. **A entrevista do corredor** — Heard entrance (who is each question about?), then the yes/no pair in one block.
2. **O esqueleto** — the four slots; the -s moving house (plays / does … play / doesn't play).
3. **O não** — don't / doesn't, and the -s visibly leaving the verb.
4. **A resposta curta** — *Yes, I do / No, she doesn't*, and what a native does **not** say.
5. **O outro do** — *What do you do after school?*, two *do* in one sentence.
6. **Lab do som** — the swallowed *d'you*, *does* with its vowel, *don't* without the *t*.
7. **Treino** — questions from ticks, negatives, short answers, listening, recording.
8. **Quiz — sem consulta.**

The interview comes first and the skeleton second: this persona needs to *hear* the helper before being shown the slot, and a question about a classmate is a question they actually want to ask.
