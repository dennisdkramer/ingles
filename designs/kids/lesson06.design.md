# Lesson 6 (kids) — design (Pass A)

`band: kids · L06 · C06 · registers: 2b (friends and classmates) · 2a (a teacher, the coach) · contexts: the class trip to the science museum, the group chat the day after, the bus, the lift home`

## Fundamental idea taken from `designs/kids/lesson05.design.md` and its parent `designs/lesson06.design.md`
The parent design's idea: **the direction decides the word, not the Portuguese**. *go* leaves, *come* arrives; *take* goes away, *bring* comes here; **buscar** is *pick up*, **pegar** (a bus) is *catch*; and one Portuguese verb splits in two (*sair* → *leave / go out*, *chegar* → *arrive / get there*, *voltar* → *come back / go back*). And the past **arrives with the verb** — *went, came, took, got, left, met, arrived* — with the three sounds of *-ed* (/t/ · /d/ · /ɪd/) and the time words (*yesterday, last night, two days ago, in 2020*) carrying the tense so the student can signal the past without conjugating anything else. Kids keep the idea and swap the business trip for the **class trip**: the museum, the bus, the lift home, the group chat the next morning.

## Core (what the student can do afterwards)
1. Choose **go / come**, **take / bring**, **pick up / catch** by **direction**, not by translating from Portuguese: *Come here! · I took my bag · She picked me up · We caught the bus.*
2. Say where they went and when, with the past form on the verb: *I went to the museum. We left at seven. I met my friends there.*
3. Say **-ed three ways** (*stopped* /t/, *arrived* /d/, *started* /ɪd/) and **hear** the American flap T (*water, better, later*).
4. Place the event in time: *yesterday, last night, two days ago, last week*.

## Teaching beats (per core item) and dependencies
- **go leaves, come arrives.** *Go* = movement **away** from where the speaker is; *come* = movement **toward** the speaker (or toward the person you are talking to — that is why *"I'm coming!"* means "I'm on my way to you"). The decision is about **where you are standing**, never about the Portuguese word.
- **levar splits into take and bring.** *Take* = away from here; *bring* = to here. **buscar** = *pick up* (collect a person or a thing); **pegar** (o ônibus) = *catch*. **Dependency:** *get* was introduced in lesson 1 and is now the third member of the set (*get = pegar / conseguir*).
- **sair / chegar / voltar: two each.** *leave* / *go out*; *arrive* / *get there*; *come back* (to here) / *go back* (away). One Portuguese verb, two English directions — and this is where the map pays for itself.
- **The past comes with the verb, not in a later lesson.** *went, came, took, got, left, met, arrived* are as frequent as their present forms; the class trip story is untellable without them. A participle column is shown **for shape only** (*gone, come, taken, got, left, met*) — the present perfect belongs to a later lesson.
- **-ed has three sounds, decided by the last sound of the verb:** /t/ after a voiceless sound (*stopped, watched, asked*), /d/ after a voiced one (*arrived, called, played*), /ɪd/ after *t / d* (*started, needed, waited*) — the only case that adds a syllable.
- **Spelling is mechanical:** +ed · *e* → +d · consonant + *y* → *-ied* · short stressed syllable → double the consonant (*stop → stopped*).
- **Time words carry the tense.** *yesterday, last night, two days ago, last week* let the student signal the past with one word instead of a conjugation they do not control yet.

## Situation
The morning after the class trip to the science museum. In the group chat, everybody tells the story at once: who went, who came late, who took the snacks, who picked up whom, when the bus left. The student must tell their own four-line version and understand three others read fast.

## Candidates (three channels)
- **Heard — the group chat voice notes.** Three short voice notes about the trip; the student ticks **who went where** and **when**, then hears the same lines again with the arrows on screen.
- **Done — the trip map.** A simple map with arrows (home → school → museum → home again); the student writes one sentence per arrow, choosing *go/come* and *take/bring* by the direction of the arrow. Direction becomes visible before it becomes verbal.
- **Read — the museum ticket.** A short printed line (*The visit starts at 9. We leave the museum at 12.*); the student rewrites it in the past. Cheap, but it teaches nothing about direction.

## Chosen
**Done (the trip map), entered through Heard.** Direction is a **spatial** decision, so a map with arrows makes the decision visible before it is verbal — the student stops translating "levar" and starts asking *away from here or toward here?* The voice notes then prove the choice in fast speech. The ticket is the weakest entrance at this level.

**First 30 seconds:** a voice note plays — one line only (*I'm coming!*) — and two arrows appear. The student clicks the arrow that matches what they heard. No rule yet.

## What the student produces (evidence)
Six direction choices, eight past forms written from the infinitive, six *-ed* sound classifications, a four-line trip narration written then recorded, three recorded lines of their own story, then a quiz on all four behaviours with none of the training items.


## Conflicts, and how I resolved them
- **A participle column is a preview of a later tense.** Resolution: the column exists only to show the **shape** of the system, is labelled *reconhecer*, and only one recognition contrast (*I went / I've gone*) is used — no production anywhere.
- **The past is a big topic for one session.** Resolution: the past is taught **only for these verbs** (the movement set), with the -ed machinery attached to them; no other verb is opened here.
- **Flap T could belong to the sound lessons (lesson 10).** Resolution: it is taught **here**, because it *is* the /d/ sound of these very words in American speech (*called, arrived*); the consonant map stays lesson 10's.
- **The museum ticket would make the page look like school paperwork.** Resolution: it is not used. The third channel stays described and rejected.
- **`went` vs `did go`** belongs to a later contrast. Resolution: only the plain past is produced; no *did* + past is mentioned.

## Shape of the session (what is met, in what order)
1. **As mensagens do grupo** — Heard entrance: three voice notes, who went where and when.
2. **O mapa** — the arrows: *go / come* by direction.
3. **levar, trazer, buscar, pegar** — *take / bring / pick up / catch* as the same decision.
4. **sair, chegar, voltar** — one Portuguese verb, two English directions.
5. **O passado chega junto** — present · past · participle for the whole set.
6. **O -ed e seus três sons** — spelling first, then /t/ /d/ /ɪd/, then the flap T.
7. **Palavras do passado** — the time words and the sentence frame they build.
8. **Treino** — forms, direction, sounds, narration, recording.
9. **Quiz — sem consulta.**

The map comes **after** the voice notes because this persona needs the sound of the trip first; the arrows then give a body to what the ear already caught.
