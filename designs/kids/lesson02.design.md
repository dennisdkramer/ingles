# Lesson 2 (kids) — design (Pass A)

`band: kids · L02 · C02 · registers: 2b (group chat, peers) with 2a for the teacher-facing strings · contexts: a game update screenshot, a class group chat, school subjects, a YouTube comment thread`

## Fundamental idea taken from `designs/kids/lesson01.design.md` and its parent `designs/lesson02.design.md`
The parent design's idea: **you already own a thousand English words — the ending is the key**, and the key has three parts: (1) seven endings turn a Portuguese word into its English twin, (2) the *used* word is often the short one, not the long twin, (3) four endings move the strong beat, and a handful of words lie outright (false friends).

Kids keep the idea; the office vocabulary, e-mail, slides and "meeting register" go. What replaces them: game updates, school subjects, group chats.

## Core (what the student can do afterwards)
1. Decode a long English word they have never seen from its ending: *competição → competition, curiosidade → curiosity, basicamente → basically, atualizado → updated, exclusivo → exclusive, equipamento → equipment, artista → artist*.
2. Say the word people actually use, not the dictionary twin: *photograph → photo, telephone → phone, television → TV, application → app, refrigerator → fridge, information → info, bicycle → bike*.
3. Not get caught by eight words that look Portuguese and are not: *actually, pretend, push, assist, lunch, college, parents, library* — and know the English word their Portuguese twin really needed.
4. Put the beat on the right syllable: *-tion / -sion / -ity / -ic* pull the stress to the syllable **before** the ending — *com-pe-TI-tion, curi-O-sity, spe-CI-fic*, against Portuguese *com-pe-TI-ção* (which happens to agree) and *cu-ri-o-si-DA-de* (which does not).

## Teaching beats (per core item) and dependencies
- **The ending is the key, and the meaning survives the swap.** *-ção → -tion · -dade → -ty · -mente → -ly · -ado → -ed · -ivo → -ive · -mento → -ment · -ista → -ist.* The student already knows what the Portuguese word means — so nothing is spent on meaning, everything on form. **Dependency:** every Portuguese word used is one a 14-year-old already says (competição, curiosidade, atualizado, exclusivo, equipamento, artista, basicamente, organização) — no new meanings to teach.
- **`-ado` works only on adjectives/participles.** *atualizado → updated*, *organizado → organized* work; *mercado → market* does not, *cuidado → care* does not. The test: if it describes how something **is**, the swap works.
- **Exceptions are words, not rules.** *razão → reason, saúde → health, vontade → will, mercado → market* — named once so the student stops trusting the pattern blindly instead of finding the failure alone in a game update.
- **The long twin is for reading; the short word is for talking.** Nobody says "I watched a *television*" or "send me the *information*" in a group chat — it is *TV* and *info*. This is the lesson's real value for this persona: the cognate is what the word *looks like*, the short word is what a friend *says*. **Dependency:** *photo, phone, TV, app, fridge, info, bike* are glossed inline (*foto, telefone, TV, aplicativo, geladeira, informação, bike*).
- **False friends are a two-way map, never a single warning.** Each one is shown as `PT word → what it really means in EN` **and** `the EN word the PT word needed`: *actually* = na verdade (≠ atualmente = *currently*); *push* = empurrar (≠ puxar = *pull*); *pretend* = fingir (≠ pretender = *plan/intend*); *assist* = ajudar (≠ assistir = *watch*); *lunch* = almoço (≠ lanche = *snack*); *college* = faculdade (≠ colégio = *school*); *parents* = pais (≠ parentes = *relatives*); *library* = biblioteca (≠ livraria = *bookstore*). **Dependency:** the right-hand column (*watch, pull, snack, school, relatives, bookstore, currently*) is glossed, because the student is learning two words per row.
- **Stress: one strong beat, the rest relaxed.** Four endings decide where it falls; the Portuguese stress does not transfer (*cu-ri-o-si-DA-de* → *curi-O-sity*). This is the first taste of the stress work of lesson 10 — nothing else of lesson 10's inventory appears here.
- **The vowels stay small.** *cat · cut · cute*, *pat · pet · pit* — the words are long but they are built from the same seven sounds. `-tion` is said *shən*, never "ti-on" (the giveaway of a word that was read, not said).

## Situation
The group chat is mid-argument about a game update. Someone screenshots the patch notes — all long words — and says *"bro read it"*. The student has to (a) work out what *animations*, *competitive*, *performance* and *basically unplayable* mean without leaving the chat, (b) answer in the words people actually use, and (c) not become the joke by replying *"Actually I play yesterday"* when they mean *"I actually play every day"* — or worse, *currently*.

## Candidates (three channels)
- **Heard — the voice-note explanation.** A friend explains the patch in four voice notes, each carrying two long words; the student ticks the meaning, then says the line back with the short word.
- **Done — the decode bench.** Portuguese words from the student's own week (a school subject, a game update, a friend's post) become English on demand; each hit is confirmed and swapped for the word people say.
- **Read — the patch notes.** A short block of game-update prose where almost every content word is a cognate; the student rewrites it as one chat message they would actually send.

## Chosen
**Done (the decode bench), entered through Heard.** The fastest honest win for this persona is "you already know a thousand words" — proven word by word in a chat-sized burst, and every hit turns immediately into the *spoken* form, which is the only part that transfers to speaking. The patch-notes reading is the best real material but at C02 it becomes translation; the voice notes alone would leave nothing written to look back at.

**First 30 seconds:** a voice note plays — *"Bro, the new animations are insane, it's so competitive now"* — and two chat bubbles appear: *"as animações"* / *"os competidores"*. Pick one. The suffix code is named only after two words have landed.

## What the student produces (evidence)
Twelve decoded words (disjoint from every quiz item), eight `used word` choices (*television → TV*), eight false-friend items in both directions, six stress marks on the strong beat, one recorded chat reply, then a quiz that re-tests all four behaviours on words the training never used.

## Conflicts, and how I resolved them
- **A 14-year-old's abstract vocabulary is thinner than an adult's.** Resolution: the bench draws only on words this persona genuinely says — *competição, curiosidade, basicamente, atualizado, exclusivo, equipamento, artista, organização, performance, específico* — and nothing from the business list a teen would not use (no *stakeholder*, no *onboarding*).
- **Eight false friends is a lot next to the suffix code.** Resolution: the two that matter most to this persona (*actually*, *push*) get the drill; the other six are the reference map and are tested once each in the quiz, never drilled twice.
- **The stress beat could collide with lesson 1's sound work.** It does not: lesson 1 was final consonants, `p/t/k` and /ɪ/ vs /æ/; this is the beat inside a long word. No overlap, and the full key stays in lesson 10.
- **Silly stakes vs. teaching the real short word.** Resolution: the silliness lives in the *chat frame* ("nobody says television"), never in the data — the word pairs are real and the forms are the ones a native says.

## Shape of the session (what is met, in what order)
1. **"Bro, read it"** — the Heard entrance: two voice notes, tick the meaning, say it back.
2. **O código** — the seven endings as one reference block, then the bench: twelve Portuguese words → English.
3. **A palavra que ninguém fala** — long twin vs short word (one chart), then eight `used word` choices.
4. **Cuidado: parece mas não é** — the eight false friends as a two-way map, then six chat-line corrections.
5. **A batida forte** — *-tion / -sion / -ity / -ic*, the *competition* family, six beat placements.
6. **Lab do som** — `-tion` as *shən*; the small vowels *cat · cut · cute* / *pat · pet · pit*.
7. **Treino** — decode, short word, false friend, beat, one recording.
8. **Quiz — sem consulta.**

The bench comes *before* the endings' explanation on purpose: the student produces first and the code arrives as the name for what they just did — the right order for a persona who closes a page that opens with a table.

## Notes for the build pass
Portuguese for explanations, tips and headers (C02 — PT still the default); English only for the target words, the chat lines and their audio. No phonetic symbols: the stress is shown as a marked beat inside the word itself, because the key belongs to lesson 10. The chat frame is the page's spine (a screenshot, a reply, a voice note), not a skin.

`recycles:` lesson 1's `a/an` on the nouns used here (*an update, an app, a photo*) and the final consonant (*app, chat* — not "app-ee") — used, never re-taught.
`must not re-teach:` lesson 3 owns wh- questions, the embedded polite question and the repair chunks; lesson 10 owns the full vowel/consonant map, the key and the schwa.
