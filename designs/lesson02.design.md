# Lesson 2 — design (Pass A)

## Core (what the student can do afterwards)
1. Take a Portuguese word they already own and turn it into the English one by its ending: *informação → information, universidade → university, rapidamente → rapidly, organizado → organized, objetivo → objective, departamento → department, jornalista → journalist*.
2. Know when the cognate is **not** the word people say: *photograph → photo, telephone → phone, television → TV, automobile → car, profession → job*.
3. Stop six words from lying to them: *actually, pretend, push, assist, lunch, college* (and their Portuguese false twins).
4. Put the strong beat on the right syllable: *-tion / -sion / -ity / -ic* pull the stress to the syllable **before** the ending — *informação → inforMAtion, fotografia → pho-TO-gra-phy → pho-to-GRAPH-ic*.

## Teaching beats (per core item) and dependencies
- **The suffix is a key, not a rule** — seven endings carry most abstract vocabulary; the meaning survives the swap even when the word is not identical. Dependency: the student already knows what the Portuguese word means (they are all words from their own working life), so the lesson spends nothing on meaning and everything on form.
- **The sound survives, the spelling does not** — *-dade → -ty* keeps the /t/ and the stress moves; *-mente → -ly* is transparent; *-ado/-ada* works only on **adjectives/participles** (*organizado → organized*), never on nouns (*mercado → market*, not "marketed").
- **Exceptions are words, not rules** — *razão → reason, saúde → health, vontade → will, mercado → market*. Named so the student stops trusting the pattern blindly instead of discovering the failure alone.
- **Register: the long word is often not what a native says** — *photograph* is what you hang on a wall; in a meeting it is a *photo*. This is the lesson's real business value: the cognate is for reading, the short word is for speaking.
- **False friends are a two-way map** — each is presented as `PT word → what it really means in EN` **and** `the EN word that the PT word actually needs` (*actually* = na verdade; *atualmente* = currently/nowadays). Dependencies: *watch, help, pull, snack, school, now* are glossed inline (they are the "other side" of the map).
- **Stress is the engine** — one strong syllable per word, the rest relaxed; the four endings decide where it goes; the Portuguese stress does not transfer (fo-to-gra-FI-a vs pho-TO-gra-phy). First taste of the stress work of lesson 10.
- **Short vowels survive the cognates** — the words are long but the vowels are the same small set: *pat · pet · pit*, *tan · ten · tin*, *cat · cut · cute*; and *-tion* is said *shən*, never "ti-on".

## Situation
Second day. An English e-mail or a slide with long words arrives and there is no teacher at the desk. The student must (a) guess a word they have never seen, (b) say it out loud without sounding like a dictionary, and (c) not answer a question with the wrong word (*"Actually…"* when they mean *"currently"*).

## Candidates (three channels)
- **Heard — the meeting that runs on long words.** Four short turns of a meeting (audio only), each carrying two cognates; the student ticks the meaning, then says the turn back.
- **Done — the decoding bench.** A list of Portuguese words from the student's own working life; they produce the English, and the page confirms and gives the used (short) form plus audio.
- **Read — the abstract paragraph.** A real-looking paragraph of business prose where every content word is a cognate; the student rewrites it in words they would actually say.

## Chosen
**Done (the decoding bench), entered through Heard.** At A0 the fastest honest win is "you already know 1,000 words"; the bench proves it in twenty seconds, word by word, and each hit is followed by the *spoken* form — which turns the win into usable speech instead of reading knowledge. The paragraph is the strongest *end-state* exercise but the weakest entrance: at this level a page of English prose is decoded letter by letter, not read.

**First 30 seconds:** one Portuguese word appears (*informação*), an empty box beside it, and the audio of the English word plays once. The student types what they heard, presses nothing, and the next word is already there. No rule has been stated yet.

## What the student produces (evidence)
Typed English forms for the bench (gaps), the used-word choice (*photo*, not *photograph*), six false-friend corrections, a stress-position choice per word, a listen-and-tick on the *-tion* sound, three recorded words, then a quiz that re-tests all four core behaviours on words the bench never used.


## Conflicts, and how I resolved them
- **The suffix table tempts a single huge chart.** Resolution: the bench and the chart are different sections, not one — the student produces first, the pattern is named after, and the chart is the *reference* they will come back to.
- **"-ado → -ed" is true for adjectives and false for nouns.** Stated as a scope inside the rule (*mercado → market*) rather than dropped, because the noun case is exactly the word a Brazilian will try first.
- **Stress at A0 could be too abstract.** Resolution: no phonetic notation on the page — beats are shown by capitals and dots (*pho-TO-gra-phy*), the way a dictionary shows them, and the pronunciation key itself is lesson 10's, so this lesson only says *where* the beat falls, never how to write a sound.

## Shape of the session (what is met, in what order)
1. **A bancada** — the bench (Done): eight Portuguese words, English boxes, audio of the answer — the entrance.
2. **O código** — the seven endings as a reference table with the examples the student just produced.
3. **Quase igual, não igual** — the used word vs the cognate, then the six false friends as a two-way map.
4. **A sílaba forte** — the four endings that move the stress, the *photograph* family, the "one strong, the rest relaxed" law.
5. **Lab do som** — *-tion/-sion/-ity/-ic* said as sounds; the three short-vowel series (p/t/k).
6. **Treino** — decode, choose the used word, correct the false friend, mark the beat, record.
7. **Quiz — sem consulta.**

Everything in Portuguese except the target words and their audio; the bench is the spine; audio on every word; no phonetic symbols (the key is lesson 10's).

### Decisions taken inside the design (Pass B merges reported back)
- **Bench answers are not spoken before the student writes.** The design's "audio of the English word plays once" would hand over the answer the bench exists to make the student produce, so the audio sits in the **confirmation chart** instead (rule: never show the answer in a prompt). Flagged as a deliberate deviation from the design's first-30-seconds description.
- **False friends grew from six to eight** (*parents/relatives, library/bookstore* added): both are the same failure mode, cost nothing extra, and are in the track's daily register.
- **`-graphy` / `-logy` are named in a merged note row**, not given their own chart: the roadmap's stress rule is `-TION/-SION/-ITY/-IC`, and the note keeps the pattern honest without inventing a fifth rule.
- **The quiz never reuses a bench or Treino item**: bench words, Treino A words and Quiz A words are three disjoint sets; the stress quiz uses *population/specific/reality/department* (none trained with a marked beat before).
