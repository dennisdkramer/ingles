# Lesson 29 — design (Pass A)

`recycles:` lesson 6-7 (the participles, previewed), lesson 18/23 (phrasal verbs whose particle must survive the
passive), lesson 20 (the perfect — *has been done*), lesson 26 (the reporting hedge — *is expected to* is its formal
cousin), lesson 24 (the report register), lesson 27 (the reduced clause is *not* the passive — the contrast is
named).
`must not re-teach:` the participle forms (6-7); phrasal verbs (18-23); the reduced clause (27); reported speech
(30); the perfect (20).

## Core (what the student can do afterwards)
1. **Move the focus off the doer**: *The report was submitted before the deadline* — and decide whether to name the
   doer at all (*by the client*).
2. **Wear the passive in any tense**: *is done / was done / has been done / will be done / has to be done*.
3. **Use the everyday *get* passive**: *he got promoted, I got paid, the meeting got cancelled*.
4. **Keep the particle**: *the meeting was called off*, *the form has to be filled in*.
5. **Report without claiming**: *the decision is expected to be announced on Friday*; *she is said to…*.
6. Choose active or passive as a decision about **agency**, not as a habit.

## Teaching beats and dependencies
- **The passive is a camera move, not a tense.** The event is the same; the subject slot is given to the receiver.
  That is why the lesson always shows the active twin next to the passive line.
- **The tense lives in *be*.** The participle never changes; only *be* moves. *Is / was / has been / will be* + done
  — one column, four tenses, no new morphology.
- **The *by* phrase is optional and meaningful**: naming the doer is a decision. Leaving it out protects, hides or
  simply says the doer is obvious (*the form has to be filled in*).
- **The *get* passive is the spoken register**: it sounds like something happened *to* someone, often badly
  (*got cancelled, got paid, got promoted*). Taught as register, not as a variant to prefer.
- **A phrasal verb keeps its particle** in the passive: *called off*, *filled in*, *sorted out* — the same rule as
  lesson 18, seen from the other side.
- **Reporting passives are the professional hedge**: *is expected to, is said to, is believed to* let the writer
  report a claim without owning it — the formal twin of lesson 26's *seems*.
- **The active/passive choice is about agency**, and the lesson ends on that judgement: who did it, and does the
  sentence say so?
- **Dependencies**: participles from 6-7; phrasal verbs from 18/23; the report register from 24/27.

## Situation
A procedure and a report in the same week: the procedure says what must be done and by when (no doer needed); the
report has to describe a failure without accusing anyone (the doer is deliberately left out). The student writes both
and then defends one *by* phrase.

## Candidates (three channels)
- **Read — the procedure.** Six steps in the passive, each with a modal on top; the student restores the passive and
  decides whether the doer belongs.
- **Done — the active/passive pairs.** Eight pairs; the student chooses which version a report would use and why.
- **Heard — the corridor.** Four lines in the *get* passive; the student writes what happened and to whom.

## Chosen
**Read → Done → Heard.** The procedure first because the passive is the native voice of procedure; then the pairs,
where the agency decision is visible; then the ear, because the *get* passive is almost only spoken.

**First 30 seconds:** *Someone submitted the report late.* — the student clicks the version a report would print, and
sees the doer disappear. Nothing is defined.

## What the student produces (evidence)
Six passive completions across tenses, four *by*-phrase decisions, four *get* passives, four phrasal-verb passives,
four reporting passives, a written procedure of four steps, a spoken incident report, then a no-consultation quiz
with no reused item.

## Width note (for Pass B)
