# Lesson 14 — design (Pass A)

`sequence:` the sound work, slice 2 of 2 — contractions and reductions, built on the key of lesson 10 (slice 1). No later lesson re-teaches connected speech; later lessons only use it.

## Core (what the student can do afterwards)
1. **Hear** the reduced forms and stop losing the sentence: *gonna, wanna, hafta, gotta, dunno, kinda, lotta, lemme, gimme, 'cause, outta*.
2. **Say** them too, in the register where they belong (a call with a colleague, not a contract).
3. Recognise the **'d ambiguity** (*I'd = I would / I had*) and the contractions that carry the grammar: *I'm, he's, don't, didn't, can't, won't, haven't*.
4. Place a speaker by a few features: **non-rhotic Boston, Southern *y'all*, British long *bath* vowel, Australian rising endings** — described with the sounds already taught.

## Teaching beats (per core item) and dependencies
- **The written form and the spoken form are two registers of one sentence.** Nothing here is "wrong English": *going to* becomes *gonna* the way *cannot* becomes *can't*. The lesson teaches the mapping, never a spelling to write in an e-mail.
- **Reduction follows frequency and position**: the words that shrink are the ones that carry no stress (function words and helpers), exactly the six from lesson 10 (*a, the, of, to, and, for*). This is the same law, now with the full inventory.
- **The 'd is decided by what follows it**: *I'd go* (would + verb) vs *I'd finished* (had + participle). One apostrophe, two tenses — and the ear resolves it, not the eye.
- **Dependencies**: the contractions of *be* and the negatives come from lessons 1-7 (*he's, don't, can't*); *hafta/gotta* belong to the modal lesson (15) and are taught here only as **sound**, used there as forms.
- **Accents are described, not imitated**: each feature is named with a sound the student already has (rhotic *r* from lesson 10, the *bath* vowel from the vowel map), so the student can *place* a speaker without performing a parody.

## Situation
A fast call with a colleague in another country: the student understands every word on the slide and almost nothing on the phone, then has to answer in the same register — and later repeat what was said in a more formal e-mail.

## Candidates (three channels)
- **Heard — the fast call.** Six lines at natural speed, each carrying one reduction; the student ticks the full form they heard underneath.
- **Done — the two-column rewriter.** Sentences in full form on the left; the student writes the reduced version, then records it and compares with the model.
- **Read — the chat transcript.** A text thread with *gonna / wanna / dunno* written out; the student rewrites it as a formal e-mail.

## Chosen
**All three, in this order: Heard → Done → Read.** This is the one lesson where the ear must come first and the pen last: a student who meets *gonna* in writing first will write it. The transcript at the end proves the register boundary — the same content, two audiences.

**First 30 seconds:** one line plays fast (*I'm gonna hafta call you back*) and the student clicks the written sentence that matches. Nothing is defined.

## What the student produces (evidence)
Six listen-and-match items, six reductions written from the full form, four 'd decisions, a formal rewrite of a chat thread, two recordings (reduced and full), then a quiz on all four behaviours with no reused item.
