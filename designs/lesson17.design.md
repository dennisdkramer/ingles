# Lesson 17 — design (Pass A)

`recycles:` lesson 9 (at/on/in for place — recapped as the same point/surface/box logic), lesson 8 (what time /
how often), lesson 12 (deadlines, dates, prices), lesson 15 (arrangements with a clock — the carrier for *by* and
*until*), lesson 6 (go/come — the movement verbs).
`must not re-teach:` *since* + a point in time (lesson 20 owns it, with the present perfect); the future forms
(lesson 15); at/on/in for place as a new system (lesson 9 — here it is a one-line recap); the article system
(lesson 24); *for* as a purpose word (not taught here — only duration).

## Core (what the student can do afterwards)
1. **Place a moment**: *at* a clock, *on* a day/date, *in* a month/year/part of the day — and the fixed blocks
   (*in the morning, at noon, at night, on the weekend*).
2. **Say how long and by when**: *for* two hours (duration), *during* the meeting (inside it), *until* noon (up to
   it), *by* Friday (not later than it) — the pair *until / by* is the lesson's sharpest contrast.
3. **Locate a point in a sequence**: *before, after, between…and, from…to, ago*.
4. **Move a thing through space**: *to, from, into, out of* — with the same point/surface/box logic of lesson 9.
5. Run a calendar conversation: what is fixed, what is a deadline, what is a duration.

## Teaching beats and dependencies
- **Prepositions are not vocabulary, they are geometry.** *at* = a point, *on* = a surface/day, *in* = a box/
  container (a month is a box, a morning is a box). The same three words do time and place — that is the lesson's
  elegance and its memory hook.
- ***by* against *until***: both translate *até*, and they are not the same. *by Friday* = the deadline, the latest
  acceptable moment (often before it). *until Friday* = the whole stretch, continuously, up to Friday. If the verb
  is a one-off action, *until* is wrong: *send it by Friday*, never *send it until Friday*.
- ***for* against *during***: *for* measures a length (*for two hours*), *during* locates inside an event
  (*during the meeting*) and takes a noun, not a number.
- ***ago* points backwards from now** and sits after the amount (*two days ago*), unlike Portuguese *há dois dias*.
- **Movement is the place logic with a direction**: *to* = the point you aim at, *from* = the point you leave,
  *into* = entering the box, *out of* = leaving it. *in* (position) against *into* (movement) is the pair.
- **Dependencies**: the clock and the calendar vocabulary comes from 8 and 12; the arrangements come from 15; the
  place trio from 9.

## Situation
A week with three fixed points: a client meeting, a delivery, a flight. The student builds the week out loud —
what happens at nine, what must be finished by Thursday, how long the trip lasts, when the taxi comes, and where
each thing goes — then answers a colleague who keeps confusing the deadline with the duration.

## Candidates (three channels)
- **Done — the week grid.** A small calendar with seven slots and eight phrases to place; the student decides the
  preposition by the shape of the slot (clock, day, month, duration, deadline).
- **Read — the handover note.** A colleague's note about the week with the prepositions missing; the student fills
  them and then says the week back in five sentences.
- **Heard — the travel instructions.** A short set of spoken instructions (*the taxi comes at six, be at the gate
  by half past, the flight is in the evening*); the student writes the times and marks the deadline.

## Chosen
**Read → Done → Heard.** The note first, because prepositions are only decidable inside a real sentence; then the
week grid, which forces the *by/until* and *for/during* decisions one at a time; then the ear, because *at six* and
*by six* are one sound apart and a whole obligation apart.

**First 30 seconds:** three phrases — *at Friday, on Friday, in Friday* — and the student clicks the one that is
English. The rule arrives as a rescue, not as a lecture.

## What the student produces (evidence)
Eight placed phrases in the week grid, six preposition completions, four *by/until* decisions, four movement
choices, five spoken sentences describing the week, two written instructions for a colleague, then a
no-consultation quiz with no reused item.

## Width note (for Pass B)
The time chart (three shapes + fixed blocks) and the movement chart are full width: five fields per row. The
*by/until* and *for/during* contrast sit as halves in one row — two four-item groups that would each leave a full
slot more than half empty, and the contrast between them is the teaching.
