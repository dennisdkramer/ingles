# CONTENT_EXAMPLES — A menu of **mechanisms** and skins for content authored in the page's HTML

## [BUILD] - Ignore this section for lesson generation

### [GAMES]
1. There is **no house style**. Look, palette, motion and mood are chosen per game.
2. **The page must still work if the engine never loads.** The authored `.qitem`s are ordinary radios: they render, save and print with no JS. That fallback is not optional.
3. **No network at run time.** Art is CSS, inline SVG, or a local file. Nothing fetched from another host, so a page that ships today still renders in two years.
4. **Fits its slot.** A vehicle or stage has a defined .itemsize or range of .itemsizes with which it is compatible and never overflows.
5. **Reduced motion is honoured** — `prefers-reduced-motion` removes travel and keeps the state change, so the game still tells the student what happened.
6. **Cheap frames.** Animate `transform` and `opacity`; avoid animating layout properties, which jank on a school laptop.
7. **Difficulty and content are data.** Every game reads its items from the page and its knobs from `data-*` attributes, so a teacher can add items or change difficulty without editing an engine.
8. **The content in the page.** Games drive authored `.qitem`s (stem + optional `.listen` + radios), `input.gap`, or a textarea — nothing else. Whatever the engine animates is presentation; the saved answer is always one of those controls. If a mechanism cannot end in one, it is not a vehicle, it is a decoration. 
    
### [BLOCKS]
- **A figure is optional.** The cheapest visual for a point inside a phrase is a class on the phrase, not a picture, and a picture that only repeats the sentence is worse than the sentence. Draw when the point is *where something is* or *how something moves*.
- **If the beat is scored, a control saves it.** `.qitem` radios, `input.gap` and an authored `textarea` are what the engine records; a slider, a hotspot, a hover-reveal or a `<details>` looks interactive and records nothing. A figure may illustrate an item and never stands in for the control — and an item with no figure at all is the normal case.
- **CSS is fair game.** `style.css` for anything a lesson page will use, `qa/preview-visual.css` while a shape is still a draft. A new **top-level block** class must be added to `qa/overflow.mjs`'s selector list or it is never width-checked; an inline span needs no entry, because it cannot widen a block.
- **Where a label lives is per figure.** An HTML label reflows, wraps at any width and is announced by a screen reader; an SVG `<text>` stays at a fixed point in the drawing but cannot wrap and is read out of order. Prose-length labels go in HTML beside the figure; a one-word mark that must sit *at* a point (a gap, a `1`) may live inside the drawing.
- **Every shape here is prototyped as real markup** in `qa/preview-blocks.html`, under its own `<section class="qa-item" id="item-NN">`, so a build copies a working thing rather than a description of one.

### [VISUALS]
- **Depth, in this order of importance.** **Occlusion** first — the near face painted over what is inside, so a ball in an open tin loses its bottom while a ball on the closed lid keeps its whole circle: this is the cue that separates *in* from *on*. Then **the opening** — an ellipse filled dark says you are looking into something, without a single perspective line. Then **a contact shadow** — an ellipse the object sits on, not a circle floating in space. The primitives are `.lid`, `.side`, `.cav`, `.shadow`, `.screen`, and every figure that needs depth draws its near face **last** for exactly that reason.
- **Flat on purpose** where depth would add noise: an abstract diagram (a decision tree, a single time axis, a gauge) and a true sagittal section, which is a genuinely 2-D object.
- **Inline marks — the phrase is the figure.** Most of what a lesson shows about pronunciation is a property of one or two letters inside a sentence: which syllable is loud, where the tune turns, which sound joins the next word, which letter is written and not said. That needs a `<span>` with a class and no figure at all — it fits a full panel and a `.third` column without a width decision, prints black and white, cannot overflow, stays glued to the word if the sentence is edited, and is the only visual that survives a screen reader with its meaning intact. All of it `.mk-*`, all of it due in `style.css`:

| class | draws | suits |
|---|---|---|
| `.mk-stress` | a filled dot above the syllable | stress placement, `RE-cord` against `re-CORD` |
| `.mk-rise` / `.mk-fall` | a small ↗ or ↘ at the point of the turn | intonation, statement against question |
| `.mk-link` | a dotted tie between two words | connected speech, `an_apple`, `go_on` |
| `.mk-drop` | a struck letter | a letter written and not said, `Wed(d)nesday`, `(h)our` |
| `.mk-syl` | a thin rule between syllables | syllable counting, `com|pu|ter` |
| `.mk-pair` | a highlight with an underline | the two letters that differ, `s(i)t` / `s(ea)t` |
| `.mk-slow` | opened letter-spacing | a sound held longer, `so(oo)n` |

- **The shape carries the meaning; colour only reinforces it.** A dot, an arrow, a tie, a strike and a rule are five different shapes, so a grey print, a projector or a red/green confusion still reads all five. `posMarks` adds the same rule to underlines: the line style separates the classes and the colour reinforces it.
- **The mark never replaces the control.** A marked sentence is still an item: `.qitem` radios whose stem carries the marks, or an `input.gap` sitting inside the phrase, which is the same insight as the mark. Display alone records nothing.
- Authoring is one span at the point of the mark, empty where the mark is the whole content:
```html
<p>I <span class="mk-stress">RE</span>cord it, then we
   <span class="mk-pair">s</span>it on the <span class="mk-pair">s</span>eat<span class="mk-rise"></span></p>
```
- **The element-as-plate list** — where the element itself is the teaching device, the element *is* the plate and needs no class: `ruby`/`rt` (a key over a word), `figure`/`figcaption` (a picture and its line), `<s>` (a rejected line, an erasure), inline `<svg>` (a drawn map, timeline, mouth), `<img>` from `assets/` (a photograph, sign or form). An image is always a local file (`[GAMES] 3`), and a vehicle whose `markup` uses any of these names the ones it uses.
- **Outside art: vendored, never hotlinked** (`[GAMES] 3`), so an outside picture means downloading the file into this repo, with provenance and licence in `assets/README.md`, referenced as `../assets/<name>.<ext>` and captioned with its source on the page that uses it. **The licence travels with the file** — a share-alike plate means a recoloured or highlighted derivative carries the same licence. And **a real anatomical plate teaches anatomy, not articulation**: nothing licensed shows where *this* student's tongue must go, so the arrow, the highlighted path and the Portuguese instruction line do that; draw the schematic and use outside art as the accuracy reference.
	
## [INSTRUCTIONS] for selection and implementation
- **A menu, not a checklist.** Nothing here is required in any lesson or in any order; a vehicle is used when it is the optimal way to present content the build already has.
- **Shape is the vehicle's use, in four atoms** joined by `|`. A combination means exactly the union of its parts and carries no definition of its own.
	- `TEACH` -> Explains, elaborates , gives translations, or otherwise passes understanding or knowledge to the student.
	- `TRAIN` -> Any activity that makes the student utilize their knowledge.
	- `SHOW` -> Presents or demonstrates but does not 'teach' - must be combined with another vehicle that [explains or uses] it as a [teaching or training] example, which is why it is never a shape on its own.
	- `OPTION` -> The block declares, in its **markup**, a designated slot *inside its own container* — the same `.panel` or `.card` — that may hold any one content type, or another vehicle's markup. The slot never becomes a container of its own.
- **Headings are the index.** Every `### ` heading reads `[NN] <shape> <itemsize> <what it is and/or does> <items> <options>`. `itemsize` is the block's default width as the stylesheet names it (`full`, `half`, `twothird`, `third`, `half->`, `third->`). In the counts, omit either number when it does not apply — `<3|>` is three or more with no ceiling, `<|4>` at most four — and write `< , >` when neither applies. The phrase is in the third person, naming **wha	t the vehicle does** and/or what the **student** does("the student ranks…", "…a box the student fills"), never left implied by an imperative, which reads equally as a build instruction and as a task. `node qa/brief.mjs` prints this index; the lines under a heading are read only **after** a shape is chosen (`qa/brief.mjs --section=NN` prints one).
- **The tone of a lesson comes from the lesson's copy, never from here.** Every vehicle is track-neutral.
- **`<h3>` and `<h4>`.** `<h3>` begins with a letter by its order in the section, and is followed by the text the vehicle's **markup** defines it to be — or, where markup defines none, a super-minimal phrase that names the block's function or contents, never filler. `<h4>` is mandatory when the vehicle's **markup** defines one, and otherwise an optional one-line explainer of the contents that never restates `<h3>`. **A block's introduction or explainer is an `<h4>` and never a `<p>`**: `<p>` inside a block always carries the block's own content, so the element alone says whether a line introduces the block or *is* the material.
- **The saved answer is always a control** (`[BLOCKS] 2`): `.qitem` radios, `input.gap` or a `textarea`. A figure, a mark, a fold or a highlight shows; it never scores, and an entry that cannot end in one of the three is not a vehicle.
- **A figure with no item of its own is not a vehicle.** Where a drawing, a mark or a highlight has nothing that saves, the *host* block owns the container and the control and the figure is drawn inside it — `[INSIDE]` lists those six, and it is the whole of that list. Such an entry carries `< , >` for both counts, is never chosen on its own, and is written to be dropped into a block that already exists.

### [02] <TEACH|OPTION> <THIRD-> Plays a recording with lyrics and shows which line is being sung < , > < , >
- **markup**: a `.panel`; `<h3>` = the song's own title, never the vehicle's name; **`<h4>` = who performs it, and nothing else, italic because every `<h4>` is** — it is the block's credit line, and it is the last thing above the player. Inside: `figure.vz-play` with **no `figcaption`**, so nothing is printed above the `<audio>`; `<audio class="vz-audio" src="../assets/<name>.mp3" data-duration="<decoded length>" controls preload="metadata">`, `data-duration` being the length measured at authoring time, which the bar, the clock and `End` use (a browser's own `duration` for a VBR file is an estimate); the drawn controls — a `.vz-btn`, two `.vz-time` readouts, a `.vz-bar` with `.vz-fill` and `.vz-knob`, the bar `role="slider"` with `aria-valuenow`; `<ol class="vz-lyrics">`, one `<li>` per line, `data-t` in seconds on any line whose time is known. The native `controls` attribute is the floor: with no JavaScript the student still plays, pauses and scrubs, and `qa/player.js` removes it only after wiring the drawn bar, the keyboard (`←`/`→` five seconds, `Home`/`End`), click-a-line-to-seek and the lyric highlight. `data-kind="teach"`, no `load` — nothing is scored. At full width the same figure carries lines too long to hold at a half and the highlight still falls on one `<li>`. One optional `<p>` under the `<ol>` — content, not an introduction, so it is a paragraph and not an `<h4>` — one paragraph explaining the words and never the block, present only when the point is inside the words. **The `OPTION` slot sits inside this same `.panel`**: one `<h4>` naming what it holds, then any one content type — a `.chart`, an item list, a paragraph — omitted when the lesson has nothing to put in it, and never a `.panel` of its own.
- **source**: `qa/AUDIO.md` — the clip is cut by one command that prints this markup: the routes, the flags and their defaults, the steps, and the fix for a sync that is a second or two out are there. The song is chosen for the words the lesson needs, not for its title.

- **markup**: **one** `.panel`, and never a second one appended below it. `<h3>` = the song's own title; `<h4>` = who performs it, and nothing else, italic because every `<h4>` is — the block's credit line and the last thing above the player; inside the panel one `figure.vz-play` with **no `figcaption`**, so nothing is printed above the `<audio>`; then `<audio class="vz-audio" src="../assets/<name>.mp3" data-duration="<decoded length>" controls preload="metadata">`, `data-duration` being the length measured at authoring time, which the bar, the clock and `End` use (a browser's own `duration` for a VBR file is an estimate); the drawn controls — a `.vz-btn`, two `.vz-time` readouts and a `.vz-bar` with `.vz-fill` and `.vz-knob`, the bar `role="slider"` with `aria-valuenow`. ### [04] <TEACH> <THIRD-> Presents a word's etymology and how that is relevant or useful to the student <2|4> < , >
- **markup**: one `.panel`; `<h3>` = **the word being presented**, its first letter capitalised (`Nice`) and never the vehicle's name; a `dl` — a `<dt>` per stop carrying the date and the language, a `<dd>` under it carrying the definition (the form and what it meant then) and, **on the line below that definition**, after a `<br>`, the one-line description of what happened to the word at that stop (`the word enters meaning foolish`) — because a list of stops is a definition list, and a table would rule the journey into separate boxes; one `.dim` line at the end carries the payoff ("the spelling froze around 1400; the sound kept moving"), the sentence the student should leave with. `data-kind="teach"`.
- **info**: it is used only once the lesson has made the word strange, it explains present oddity and never present usage, and every date and form is checked — a made-up etymology is an error the student keeps for life. Its relevance is the payoff line's job: an etymology that is merely interesting is a fact the lesson paid a block for and cannot use, so the line says what it is for now.

### [07] <TEACH> <FULL> One contrast across three word positions <2|4> <3|3>
- **markup**: one `.chart` whose first column is the written form and whose other columns are the positions (start, middle, end), each cell a word with its own `.listen` chip. ### [12] <TEACH|TRAIN> <FULL> 'Unknown plan' block, beside block with fields to have the student ask questions and learn the details that they need <1|1> <4|8>

### [27] <TRAIN> <FULL> Words crossing the screen, shot inside a clock <1|> <2|6>
- **markup**: `<div class="game" data-game="skeetRange">` — `.game > .game-list` of `.qitem`; the stem is the prompt line, the options are the targets — one disc each, so the item's own options decide how many fly. Art: one flat ring per option, the word on a plaque over the disc; a thin crosshair that is the cursor; a single puff on impact. Every disc takes the SAME line — one arc, one speed, no wobble, no sine wave — and the lanes are spread over the full height of the range, so the words read as one rack, not a scatter. `data-kind="train"`.
- **info**: the range carries a clock 30% slower than the block's own — `data-time` is stretched by 1.3 (`PLUGINS.skeetRange.timeScale`, applied by the runner), so the discs and the timer slow together and a word can be read before it has to be shot at, so a block writes the deadline it *wants minus 30%* or leaves the default and takes the slower range as it comes. Nothing else is needed: `engine4.js` ships it. No production and no spelling; one shot per item means a careless click can hide what the student knew.

### [28] <TRAIN> <FULL> Lookalike options surfacing, the fitting one struck <1|> <2|6>
- **markup**: `<div class="game" data-game="whackMole">` — the same `.qitem` list; the prompt sits on the HUD line, options become the moles' labels. Art: inline SVG mole + radial-gradient hole with a dirt rim, rise via `translateY`, reduced-motion path. The garden is **always six holes in a 3×2 grid**, so six options fill it exactly and the layout never reflows between items. `data-kind="train"`.
- **info**: the prompt is the thing to FIND, so it is written as that thing and nothing more — `past`, `a place you sleep`, `sorry, say that again?` — never `which one is past`, because the moles are already the candidates and the sentence only restates the game. Below 2 options there is nothing to spot among; above 6 there is no hole left for the seventh. Nothing else is needed: `engine4.js` ships it.

### [29] <TRAIN> <FULL> Items falling, sorted into two to four baskets <1|> <2|4>
- **markup**: `<div class="game" data-game="catchBasket">` — one word per item (`.qstem`), one pot per option (the radio labels); a correct drop checks that option's radio; the word card IS the prompt, so the stage does not print the same words again on the HUD line. Art: inline SVG terracotta pot, water drops over the rim on a right drop, a flower grows out of the soil and stays there (six to a pot); the word lifts and tilts while held, a wrong drop shakes; the pots sit in a row on the stage. `data-kind="train"`.
- **info**: two to four pots is the ceiling — four is one row at a size the words still read at, a fifth has no soil left, fine distinctions don't fit, and a seventh flower would swamp the pot. Dropping outside springs the word back, so a slipped finger costs time, not the item. Nothing else is needed: `engine4.js` ships it.

### [30] <TRAIN> <FULL> A drill answered item by item, the stack built as it is earned <6|10> <2|4>
- **markup**: `<div class="game" data-game="stackDrop">` — one `.qitem` per block; the question block is the stem, the buttons are the options; the question block IS the prompt, so it is not printed a second time on the HUD line. The tower is half the stage's width and each block's height comes from the run's own length, so the whole stack stands inside the stage up to ten blocks and a longer run keeps that height and slides. Art: flat blocks with hard edges, two ladder rails and a rung per answer, a drawn climber, a chest that lights up at the top; the tower slides down to keep the newest block in view once it outgrows the stage. `data-kind="train"`.
- **info**: one item is one block of the tower, so the item count IS the height it climbs — under six there is nothing to climb, and past ten each block is too short to hold its own word. Nothing else is needed: `engine4.js` ships it. Timing dominates, so keep the language load to a phrase per block.

### [31] <TRAIN> <FULL> Recognition at speed, one gate per right answer <1|> <2|4>
- **markup**: `<div class="game" data-game="flapGate">` — gaps are options; the prompt is fixed on the HUD. Art: a drawn bird that flaps its wings on every lift, including the one automatic flap as the round opens, so it is never falling before the student has touched anything; a brick wall with the words sitting in its gaps; sky as a flat fill; fall is half what it was, lift unchanged. `data-kind="train"`.
- **info**: **the stream** — walls are things in flight, not a state of the stage: each one is cut once, flies from the right edge to past the left one, and is retired while the next is already behind it; answering does not stop the flight, so a run is one continuous flight through every gate in it, and only the bricks end it. A click on a gap answers at once (the honest "I know it" path, and the only one a keyboard or reduced-motion student has); a click anywhere else flaps. **The wall is the stake** — a right gap is a point and the flight carries on into the next question; a wrong word costs nothing; flying into the brick costs one of three hearts *and the run*, so the block starts again at its first question, in the same order, with what was already answered left as it was written, and a restart is a second attempt, so the score a finished block shows is the best attempt, not the last. The wall is a fixed height and a gap is 65px, so four fill it; a fifth would have to be cut narrower than the word standing in it. It needs the shared runner + gravity loop, and it is the most expensive of the arcade set.

### [32] <TRAIN> <FULL> A word guessed to a five-try limit, letter feedback each try <1|> <2|4>
- **markup**: `<div class="game" data-game="wordleBoard">` — one target word per item, typed into a `gap` at the end, so the evidence is spelling the student produced rather than letters guessed; the stem is the clue, so it is not printed a second time on the HUD line. Art: the stage reads left to right — the clue on the left, the tile grid and the box you type into as the centre pair, and a message on the right ("too many letters", "too few letters") that fades in over .5s, holds 2s and fades out over .5s; the three are centred vertically and the board never grows, so nothing moves when a word lands — a wrong length costs a message, not a row. `data-kind="train"`.
- **info**: every item of a block holds the same word length, because the board is built from the answers; five tries per word and two lives per run. Only words of one fixed length fit — nothing for phrases, meaning or listening. Hangman is the same engine with a different face (a revealed blank row instead of coloured tiles), and nothing else is needed: `engine4.js` ships it.

### [33] <TRAIN> <FULL> A grid where a wrong move costs a mark <1|> <4|4>
- **markup**: `<div class="game" data-game="mineGrid">` — four options is what the street holds — one door per option. The lives belong to the RUN, not to the item, so they are set on the way in and never between items (a life that came back with the next question would mean a miss cost nothing); the count is the HUD's, and the stage draws no second copy of it. Marking is exact: on a right answer only the door he opened turns green; on a wrong one that door turns red with the snake in it and the right door is outlined, and nothing else on the street reacts. Art: a street and a boardwalk, a signed doorframe per word with a drawn batwing door that swings on its hinge, a drawn snake, and a figure with hurt and down states. `data-kind="train"`.
- **info**: exactly four doors — four is both the floor and the ceiling, so a lesson that writes two or three options leaves the street short. Three bites end the run, so a guess has a price and the student reads before moving; every wrong pick leaves its radio blank, so the blanks are the teacher's evidence.

### [34] <TRAIN> <FULL> A board of crossing words, one clue per word <2|6> <2|>
- **markup**: `<div class="game" data-game="crosswordBoard">` — each `.qitem` carries `data-answer` (the word) and its `.qstem` is the clue; the engine lays the whole set out at once — the first word across, every later word placed so it crosses an existing letter, anything that cannot cross dropped into its own row below. A shared letter is one cell, so typing it once fills both words and solves both items. Art: the site's own grid — a flat block per gap, a small number in the corner of each word's first cell; the cells are sized to fill the stage (10px of margin all round, height and width each get a say), and the clue for the word you are typing sits in its own panel beside the grid — one clue on the stage, never the same clue twice. `data-kind="train"`.
- **info**: every answer is spellable (letters only, at least two of them) or the board refuses the run, and six short words is the ceiling — the grid sizes its cells to fill the stage down to an 18px floor, and a seventh word drops below it. A full word typed wrongly costs nothing: nothing clears, nothing is scored and its cells stay as they were typed, so the student keeps at it until the word goes green. A right word is a point and five points wins the board; under five it stays open with whatever it has, because there is no clock and no lives. Nothing else is needed: `engine4.js` ships it. Authoring: `data-answer` on each `.qitem`, clue in `.qstem`.

### [35] <TRAIN> <FULL> A sentence built one word at a time from pads <2|> <4|4>
- **markup**: `<div class="game" data-game="echoPads">` — one `.qitem` per word of the sentence, `data-a` the word that fits, three near misses as the other pads; `data-first` the given opening; `data-lives` the countdown of points (default 10), the HUD pair counting `left/start` down, one point per wrong pad; `data-time` does not apply, because it is a board you fill at your own pace; the sentence and the hint line are the prompt, so nothing is printed a second time on the HUD line. Art: a paper sentence strip that fills in as words land — the given words in a dashed tan chip, the placed words in green, a caret holding the next place; four word pads on the neon arcade scene; "Say the sentence" reads out the sentence as it stands. `data-kind="train"`.
- **info**: **levels — two of them, and neither is shown.** `data-level` on the block decides, else the lesson page's own `L`, and a lesson numbered 20 or above is level 2. Level 1 prints the hint line; **level 2 prints no hint at all** — not "Next word:", not even the wrong-pad line — so the reading has to come off the sentence, and every other pad in an item must then be **impossible** in that position, not merely unlikely, which is an authoring rule the engine cannot test. **Guardrail**: the sentence comes FIRST and the pads are generated second, one word at a time, left to right — for each `.qitem`, read the words already on the board (`data-first` plus every item before it), then write the one word that fits and check the three distractors against that same reading; a distractor that could follow what is placed is a broken item, and generating the three options before the phrase is written, or from the whole sentence at once, is what produces them. One sentence per block — a second sentence is a second block, not a longer run — and nothing else is needed: `engine4.js` ships it (the voice is the built-in speech).

### [36] <TRAIN> <FULL> A card of words with the spoken form called <1|> <2|9>
- **markup**: `<div class="game" data-game="bingoCard">` — tiles are options, so the words on the card are the lesson's words; the instruction is the block's own `.game-how` and nothing else — nothing inside the stage prints "listen" as a second heading. Marking is exact: the tile the student stamped turns green or red, and nothing else on the card reacts; the right tile is never revealed, so what the caller said is never shown. - **info**: the key is copied from `dictionary.json` rather than written from the author's ear, or it contradicts the popup on the same word; `rt` cannot mark a syllable, so a stress pattern inside a word still needs its own block; a key over every word is unreadable in a narrow box, so it is never squeezed; and it is never chosen as a block of its own, because it has nothing that saves — it goes into an item that already exists, at whatever width that item has.
