## Curriculum, Planning, and Course structure

1. Order is derived from this book's own difficulty model — not from any external grammar syllabus. 
2. Concepts and vocabulary items are assessed by how far they sit from Portuguese (transparent / partial / opaque) times how important they are for understanding and producing spoken english  (core / common / marginal). 
3. Balance between concept(s) presentation/explanation and training time is flexible based on model reasoning.

### The difficulty model

- **Distance from Portuguese (transfer).** Transparent = a direct anchor exists (a cognate, or a structure whose meaning maps cleanly). Partial = an anchor exists but misleads (the form maps, the meaning does not — or vice-versa). Opaque = no anchor; it must be built from nothing.
- **Utility in TRACK-appropriate context** Core = the sentence cannot be formed without it (word order, do-support, the dummy subject, the top workhorse verbs). Common = frequent and useful but not sentence-blocking. Marginal = rare, formal, or replaceable.
- **Placement = the two axes combined by judgment.** Transparent + Core → earliest (fast wins). Opaque + Core → early, taught as a structural rule. Partial + Core → middle, taught as a meaning trap. Transparent + Marginal → a deliberate choice (early-with-its-kin OR late as "formal only"), recorded with a reason. Opaque + Common → mid-to-late, sized to importance. Opaque + Marginal → late or optional.
- **Vocabulary is concept → context → mapping.**

### Pedagogy principles
- **Unique ideas** Creatively synthesize teaching possibilities.. but dont throw the baby out with the bathwater: When appropriate, research several outside sources on the lesson's topic to brainstorm ideas, not to copy. 
- **Contrast with Portuguese** The highest-value content is where English does NOT behave like Portuguese. The simple present is the flagship example:
  - EN Simple present = FACTS and IN-GENERAL statements only. It never touches the past, and touches the future only as a scheduled FACT. (vs Portuguese "trabalho aqui há 5 anos" = "I HAVE worked here for 5 years" ,never "I work here for 5 years").
- **Keep training past lesson concepts and vocabulary** - NEVER FILLER, never redundant - **defensible training for words and concepts that are commonly used and/or difficult to get accustomed to**
- **Early foundations** Lesson 1 begins with an introduction to the track, teaching method, and a summary overview of "vowels have no fixed sound" and "cognates map with their suffix codes". Lesson 2 elaborates and contextualizes those vowel and cognate topics.
- **reductions and connected speech are introduced early** and used from then on - acceptable formal reductions ALWAYS used (I'm/don't), use of informal reductions depends on track/register (gonna/wanna/woulda).
- **words that translate to more than one word or that dont translate 1:1 must have their intricacies explained as they are introduced.** - eg. Take is not just `pegar`,`get` is taught as the wildcard ("coringa"), `on` something does not mean "em" or "no", it is a preposition of abstract connection, etc..
- **"gonna" is a future MODE, not a verb** Teach `[be] + gonna + verb` as the spoken future ("I'm gonna call" = *vou ligar*), and keep it separate from the verb "go" with a PLACE after it ("I'm going to the office" = *estou indo ao escritório*). "I'm gonna go to the office" is therefore not a contradiction — "gonna" marks future and "go" is the verb. The student should use "gonna" (not avoid it); it is the natural spoken form, while "going to" is the written/formal twin.
- **Carrier content escalates with the course.** The example phrases and contexts that carry each lesson's grammar are matched to the student's level at that point: they draw on everything already taught and grow in length and complexity (conforming to the register) as the course advances. For any item that is recycled (must have a defensible purpose) it reappears inside a richer frame.
- **Phrasal verbs first, formal to map meaning/in its proper context only** Where a native speaker would say the phrasal verb (put off, call off, set up, get back to, go over, wrap up), that is what the student learns and produces 
- **"verb + preposition" units.** (look AT, go TO, give TO, talk TO, depend ON, wait FOR) Verbs should be introduced with accompanying prepositions. Where appropriate, contrast with related verb+preposition pairs. Understanding of prepositions is emphasized where appropriate throughout the course
- **The [mode] carries the grammar — the verb returns to base.** The sentence skeleton is [subject][mode][verb][object][place][time] - When [mode] is filled (do/did/will/would/could/should/might/can/must, etc..) it is the mode that carries the verb tense, and thus the verb has no conjugation. Teach this as ONE rule reused at every tense — not as separate "do-support", "negation", "past", "future" and "modal" rules.
- **High-frequency prepositions are taught by MEANING, early.** TO = direction/movement/change + the infinitive marker; FOR = purpose or duration; ON = connection/surface contact; IN = inside a larger thing; AT = a point in space or time / a target. The PT single word "para" splits into TO and FOR — flagged and drilled because it is a persistent PT→EN error. These meanings are integrated into the early routine/place/time lessons and recycled, not parked in one grammar lesson.
- **Group related items into families.** When vocabulary, phrases or structures can be taught at a similar grammatical level, teach them together as a set rather than scattered — e.g. a family of phrasal verbs built on ONE root verb (get back / get on / get up; look at / look for / look up / look over). Grouping by root or theme reveals the system and improves retention; a random assortment hides the pattern.
- **Factuality.** Every factual or statistical claim in the materials must be true and defensible. Never assert anything you havent verified, never include a 'term' that is not in common use in real english. (eg. 'intensifiers' are not called 'boosters')
- **Lesson size.** Each lesson robustly teaches its pedagogical concept(s) and presents relevant connections and vocabulary with no quantitative limit on lesson content.
