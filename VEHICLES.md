# VEHICLES — the blocks a lesson is built from

The `###` lines are the index and the parser reads them unchanged: `node qa/brief.mjs --index` lists every
entry here, `--section=NN` prints one. A section is read only **after** a shape is chosen, and a vehicle is
used when it is the best way to present content the lesson already has — never to fill a slot.

What lives elsewhere: a figure a block draws inside itself → `IN-BLOCK-VISUALS.md`; the rules of the build
(engine constraints, drawing primitives, the heading grammar) → `CONTENT_EXAMPLES.md`.

## [TONE] — what the words the student reads are allowed to be

Five tags, written on the element that carries the line, and only when the line sits above neutral:

| tag | is | is not |
|---|---|---|
| `neutral` | the default; never written | — |
| `sarcasm` | the meaning under the words, said flat | an insult |
| `funny` | a line that earns a smile and still teaches | a pun that needs the tag to work |
| `heat` | anger, urgency, a raised voice in the words | profanity |
| `offensive` | profanity, a slur, mockery of a person | the only way to write street register |

Authored as `data-tone="sarcasm|funny|heat|offensive"`. Nothing in the stylesheet or the engines reads it
today, so it is a note to the teacher and to the next build, never a mechanism; and a joke whose point
disappears when the tag is deleted is not a joke, it is a label. `offensive` appears only where the track's
register file makes the street the lesson, and never as the lesson's own voice.

### [31] <TRAIN> <FULL> Recognition at speed, one gate per right answer <1|> <2|4>

It needs the shared runner + gravity loop, and it is the most expensive of the arcade set.

### [34] <TRAIN> <FULL> A board of crossing words, one clue per word <2|6> <2|>
- **markup**: `<div class="game" data-game="crosswordBoard">` — each `.qitem` carries `data-answer` (the word) and its `.qstem` is the clue; the engine lays the whole set out at once — the first word across, every later word placed so it crosses an existing letter, anything that cannot cross dropped into its own row below. ### [35] <TRAIN> <FULL> A sentence built one word at a time from pads <2|> <4|4>
- **markup**: `<div class="game" data-game="echoPads">` — one `.qitem` per word of the sentence, `data-a` the word that fits, three near misses as the other pads; `data-first` the given opening; `data-lives` the countdown of points (default 10), the HUD pair counting `left/start` down, one point per wrong pad; `data-time` does not apply, because it is a board you fill at your own pace; the sentence and the hint line are the prompt, so nothing is printed a second time on the HUD line. Art: a paper sentence strip that fills in as words land — the given words in a dashed tan chip, the placed words in green, a caret holding the next place; four word pads on the neon arcade scene; "Say the sentence" reads out the sentence as it stands. `data-kind="train"`.
- **info**: **levels — two of them, and neither is shown.** The lesson page's own `L` decides, and a lesson numbered 20 or above is level 2. Level 1 prints the hint line; **level 2 prints no hint at all** — not "Next word:", not even the wrong-pad line — so the reading has to come off the sentence, and every other pad in an item must then be **impossible** in that position, not merely unlikely, which is an authoring rule the engine cannot test. **Guardrail**: the sentence comes FIRST and the pads are generated second, one word at a time, left to right — for each `.qitem`, read the words already on the board (`data-first` plus every item before it), then write the one word that fits and check the three distractors against that same reading; a distractor that could follow what is placed is a broken item, and generating the three options before the phrase is written, or from the whole sentence at once, is what produces them. One sentence per block — a second sentence is a second block, not a longer run — and nothing else is needed: `engine4.js` ships it (the voice is the built-in speech).

### [36] <TRAIN> <FULL> A card of words with the spoken form called <1|> <2|9>

### [37] <TRAIN> <FULL> A quiz run that carries the score already earned <5|> <2|4>
- **markup**: `<div class="game" data-game="ladderRun">` — the `.qitem` list is the ladder; the options are the buttons under it. One rung per right answer, a **safe rung every five**, and the height is **heard** as well as seen (a rising motif as you climb); a wrong answer slides the climber back to the last safe rung, so what was safely earned is kept, which is what makes a ladder a ladder and not a list. A fall back to the safe rung is deliberately harsh, so keep items fair. Nothing else is needed: `engine4.js` ships it.

## [TIPS] — small vehicles: a tip, a hint, a piece of trivia

A tip is a block that costs one line and earns its place by making something stick. It carries no item of
its own unless the tip asks the student to do something, never restates the block above it, and never
becomes the lesson's main teaching — a page of tips is a page that taught nothing. The heading grammar is
the vehicle's: `### [NN] <shape> <itemsize> <what it does> <items> <options>`.
### [04] <TEACH> <THIRD-> Presents a word's etymology and how that is relevant or useful to the student <2|4> < , >
- **markup**: one `.panel`; `<h3>` = **the word being presented**, its first letter capitalised (`Nice`) and never the vehicle's name; a `dl` — a `<dt>` per stop carrying the date and the language, a `<dd>` under it carrying the definition (the form and what it meant then) and, **on the line below that definition**, after a `<br>`, the one-line description of what happened to the word at that stop (`the word enters meaning foolish`) — because a list of stops is a definition list, and a table would rule the journey into separate boxes; one `.dim` line at the end carries the payoff ("the spelling froze around 1400; the sound kept moving"), the sentence the student should leave with. `data-kind="teach"`.
- **info**: it is used only once the lesson has made the word strange, it explains present oddity and never present usage, and every date and form is checked — a made-up etymology is an error the student keeps for life. Its relevance is the payoff line's job: an etymology that is merely interesting is a fact the lesson paid a block for and cannot use, so the line says what it is for now.
