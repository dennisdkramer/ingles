Saying No — Don't & Doesn't

Half of being 14 is saying no. I don't sing. She doesn't dance. One small word carries the whole refusal — and the -s from last lesson jumps onto it.

1. The karaoke incident

Duda signed Nando up for karaoke. Nando is not having it. Watch what happens to the verbs.

A. Grupo "karaokê da festa" — 19:41

karaokê da festa 🎤

Duda: I signed you up. you don't have a choice 😌

Nando: I don't sing. I don't dance. I don't do karaoke

Caio: he don't sing 😂

Duda: Caio, he doesn't sing. the -s lives on doesn't now

Caio: so he doesn't sings?

Duda: NO. one -s per sentence. he doesn't sing — the verb goes back to normal 🧠

Nando: and I don't need this stress. I'm going home

B. The rule in one look

Who does the refusingMarkerVerbExample
I / you / we / theydon'tbase formI don't sing.
he / she / itdoesn'tbase formShe doesn't sing.
The -s that lived on sings in lesson 6 moves to does: she sings → she doesn't sing. It never stays in two places. doesn't sings ❌ — that's two. One -s per sentence, always.

3. Two ways to say no: don't and be + not

Verbs use don't / doesn't. The verb be refuses on its own — it never touches don't.

A. Same sentence, flipped

✅ doing it❌ not doing it
I sing.I don't sing.
She sings.She doesn't sing.
They dance.They don't dance.
It works.It doesn't work.
I'm tired.I'm not tired.
It's far.It isn't far.
They're hungry.They aren't hungry.
be builds its own negative: am + not, is + not, are + not. So "I don't be tired" ❌ and "I don't tired" ❌ are both wrong — tired is not a verb, so it rides on be: I'm not tired ✅. Also note: there is no "amn't" in English. The short form of "I am not" is I'm not, full stop.

B. don't, doesn't, isn't or aren't?

4. How the no-sound shortens

Nobody says "do not" in a chat, and nobody says it out loud either. English squeezes it:

A. Full form → what people actually say

FullShortSounds like▶
do notdon'tdont — one syllable
does notdoesn'tDUZ-unt — two syllables, z in the middle
is notisn'tIZ-unt
are notaren'tarnt — one syllable
The apostrophe is not decoration — it marks the missing letter. doesn't has a z sound because the -s moved onto do, where it is pronounced duz. The vowel in the middle is the short one of up — /ˈdʌzənt/ — not the o of avô. Say "dóznt" and English speakers hear a word that doesn't exist.

5. Saying no to an adult

Saying no to a friend is easy. Saying no to your mom or your teacher needs the same grammar, softer words. This is the register you use with adults:

A. At home, before the party — 18:05

in the kitchen

Mom: Your aunt wants you to sing at the party.

You: Sorry, I don't sing in public.

Mom: It isn't a big party.

You: I know, but I really don't sing. I can help with the snacks, though.

Mom: Fine. And your brother doesn't help at all.

You: He doesn't cook either. Ask him to wash the dishes.

Mom: You're not funny.

You: I'm not tired either. I can stay up, right? 😇

B. Did you catch it?

1.Does the teenager sing?
2.What does he offer to do instead?
3.Who doesn't help at all?
4."I'm not tired" — why not "I don't tired"?

C. Soften it: polite refusals

Blunt (friends)Polite (adults)
I don't sing.Sorry, I don't sing in public.
No.I can't today — I'm not free. (hoje não dá / não posso)
I don't want to.I'd rather not, if that's ok. (prefiro não)
The grammar never changes — don't / doesn't in every one. What changes is the wrapping: Sorry at the front, a reason at the end. Mesma negação, embalagem diferente.
