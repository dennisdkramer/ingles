Right Now: Be + -ing

This is the form for this second — the action in progress. A kid narrating a phone is its natural habitat: I'm charging my phone, he's downloading the update, I'm scrolling, leave me alone.

B. The formula: be + verb-ing

Subjectbeverb + ingSentence
Iam → 'mchargingI'm charging my phone.
he / she / itis → 'sdownloadingHe's downloading the update.
you / we / theyare → 'rescrollingThey're scrolling TikTok.
negativebe + notmovingHe isn't moving.
questionbe firstdoingAre you doing homework?
Same frame from lesson 6 — subject, then slot 2. Only now slot 2 has two pieces: be + -ing. The -s never appears here: "he's downloading", never "he's downloads". The be already carries the person.

4. The spelling of -ing

A. Three groups

Type of verbWhat happensExamples
most verbsjust add -ingplaying · searching · watching · streaming · doing
ends in a silent edrop the e, add -ingmake → making · type → typing · charge → charging · dance → dancing · write → writing
one syllable, ends consonant-vowel-consonantdouble the last letterget → getting · run → running · sit → sitting · chat → chatting · log → logging · stop → stopping
Why double? Because English protects the short vowel sound. hoping (hope + ing) and hopping (hop + ing) are different words — the double letter keeps the vowel short. Say them out loud and you'll hear it.

6. Temporary: this month, for now

A. Permanent vs temporary

Permanent — plain formTemporary — be + -ing
I live in Recife.I'm living with my uncle this month.
My mom works at the mall.She's working at my uncle's shop this week.
I study English at school.I'm studying English with Nando these days.
Same verb, two meanings: the plain form says "this is how my life is"; be + -ing says "this is how it is for now". The markers this week, this month, these days, for now give it away. Both sentences are correct English — they just say different things.
