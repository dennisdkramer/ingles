Your Day, In Order

Your routine is ten sentences you repeat every single day — which makes it the cheapest place to make English automatic. Here's how a day is told: frequency words in their slot, steps in order, and in, on, at in front of the clock.

1. Six forty, and the alarm wins

A. In Nando's room — 06:40

quarto do Nando

Alarm: BEEP. BEEP. BEEP.

Nando: I always hit snooze. I never get up at six forty

Mom: It's seven o'clock. Are you up?

Nando: I'm up, I'm up. I usually have a shower first, then breakfast.

Mom: On weekdays you always forget your uniform.

Nando: I sometimes forget it. Sometimes is not always. 😤

Mom: It's eight at night and you say the same thing about your homework.

B. The three pieces of a routine

PieceExampleWhere it sits
frequency wordI always hit snooze.before the verb, after be
order wordFirst a shower, then breakfast.at the front of its step
time prepositionat seven · in the morning · on Mondayin front of the time
Get these three right and your whole day can be told in English without thinking. That's the goal of this lesson: not new grammar, but automation.

2. Your day, word by word

A. Word bank

Wordo que éIn your day▶

3. Frequency words: how often

A. The five words and where they stand

WordfrequênciaExample
alwayssempre — 100%I always hit snooze.
usuallynormalmenteI usually have a shower first.
oftenfrequentementeHe often forgets his uniform.
sometimesàs vezesI sometimes forget it.
nevernunca — 0%I never get up at six forty.
The slot: before the verb — I always hit, he never eats. But after be: I'm always late, she's usually tired. And sometimes is allowed at the front too: Sometimes I forget. Nunca "I hit always" — o advérbio não vai depois do verbo.

4. The routine, step by step

A. One morning, in order

First, I have a shower. Then I have breakfast — usually bread and milk. After that, I put on my uniform and brush my teeth. Finally, I walk to school with Nando.

Order wordo que éWhere it goes
Firstprimeirostep 1, at the front
Thendepois / entãostep 2 and every step after
After thatdepois dissoany later step, at the front
Finallypor últimothe last step
Only First opens and only Finally closes. Everything in between is Then or After that. "After that" sempre no começo da frase — nunca no fim.

4.I have breakfast. ___ that, I brush my teeth. (depois disso)

5. in, on, at — in front of the clock

A. Three prepositions, three jobs

PrepositionGoes withExamples
ata clock time · the word nightat six forty · at seven o'clock · at night
ina part of the dayin the morning · in the afternoon · in the evening
ona dayon Monday · on weekdays · on the weekend
at night is the rebel: "de noite" takes at, not in. But in the morning, in the afternoon and in the evening all take in. And notice the pattern: at + clock hours, on + days. Portuguese "às / de / na" doesn't map across — memorize the three groups.

6. The clock needs an empty "it"

A. English never starts a sentence with "is"

portuguêsEnglishWhat "it" means
São sete horas.It's seven o'clock.nothing — just a filler
É segunda-feira.It's Monday.nothing
São oito da noite.It's eight at night.nothing
Está frio hoje.It's cold today.the weather — also empty
Look at the Portuguese: "são" is plural, "é" is singular, and neither has a subject. English demands a subject every time — so the empty it takes the job for the clock, the day, the date and the weather. It's + time is a sentence you'll say twenty times a day.

B. Build the time sentence

1. eight at night. (São oito da noite.)
2. Monday today. (É segunda hoje.)
3. six forty. Time to get up. (São seis e quarenta.)
4. cold in the morning. (Está frio de manhã.)

2.São oito da noite.
3.É sábado.
4.Está quente hoje. (hot)

8. You say it — tell me your day

Six sentences about your own morning. Use a frequency word, an order word, or a time preposition in each one.

A. Six sentences

1. It's seven o'clock in the morning. What do you do first?
2. Eu nunca tomo café da manhã.
3. Eu sempre esqueço a escova de dentes.
4. Nos fins de semana eu durmo até mais tarde.
5. São nove e meia. Hora de dormir. (half past nine)
6. Primeiro eu tomo banho, depois eu como alguma coisa.

B. Tell it out loud, in order

Say your whole morning out loud from the moment the alarm rings: "First… Then… After that… Finally…" Record it, play it back, and fix the one word that came out wrong. Se o dia sai em ordem, a gramática está automática.
