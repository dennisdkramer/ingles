Get Understood, Not Native

By most estimates, most of the English spoken in the world today is not spoken with native speakers. It is spoken between people whose first language is something else. So the target was never “sound American”. The target is be understood.

Everything here happens inside one video call: four countries, one frog, one decision that nobody writes down. You will hear every word of that call and still get the meaning wrong — that is the lesson, not a failure. Then you fix it with six moves: drop the tax, set the frankness dial, read the “yes”, treat silence as a turn, pay with face, and accommodate instead of imitating.

A ideia inteira em uma linha: gaste a atenção de quem ouve onde está o sentido — nunca em decodificar a sua gíria.

1. The call where nothing got decided

Listen once, straight through, at normal speed. Then answer two questions about it. You have enough English for every single word in this call — and that is exactly the trap.

2. Slang is a tax, and your listener pays it

Every expression charges the person decoding it. Inside your own group chat the tax is near zero: everyone already owns the same dictionary. Send the same expression to Yuki and the attention she needed for the actual meaning goes into working out what “cooked” means. So the rule is not “never use slang”. The rule is spend the listener’s attention where the meaning is — and slang is the first thing to go when the room changes.

Gíria não é proibida: gíria é cara, e quem paga é quem ouve. Você decide onde gastar.

C. Three questions, in this order

Before any expression leaves your mouth, run these three. They take about a second, and they are the whole rule:

1. Who is decoding this? A friend who shares your feed, or a person who learned English from a textbook?
2. Can they decode it at speaking speed? Not “could they look it up” — now, while I am still talking.
3. Is the meaning in the slang word, or in a word next to it? “That’s cooked” carries the whole meaning in cooked. “The clip is mid” survives without mid.

Se qualquer resposta for “não”, o que sai da frase é a expressão — nunca o sentido.

A yes without an owner and a date is a mood, not a commitment. The test of a yes is not the question repeated louder — it is the next step asked once, plainly.

4. Frankness is a dial, and the culture sets the factory setting

Being blunt is not honesty, and being indirect is not politeness. They are two settings of one dial, and the dial measures how much of the meaning travels in the words themselves. Edward Hall described the difference in 1976 as low-context and high-context. In a low-context room the words carry almost everything: the conclusion comes first and a bare “no” is information. In a high-context room a lot of the meaning travels in the relationship, the tone and what is not said: the relationship comes first and a bare “no” lands as a break. Nobody in either room is being dishonest — but a message written for the wrong room is read as either an attack or a dodge.

Isso não é regra por nacionalidade — é o que a sala faz. A mesma pessoa muda de configuração quando muda de sala. Observe antes de decidir.

5. “Yes” is at least four things

Only one of the four is a commitment:

I agree · I heard you · I’ll try · I won’t say no to your face. The last one is not a lie and it is not agreement — it is a politeness engine, and it runs in most of the world. The mistake is walking away with the first meaning when the room gave you the fourth. And you cannot fix it by repeating your question louder: louder tests nothing. The test is to ask for the next step.

As quatro leituras do “yes”: concordo · ouvi · vou tentar · não digo não na sua cara. Só a primeira é compromisso.

A. Six yeses, one job each

6.“Yes! Absolutely, I’m on it.” — then two days of nothing

6. Silence is a turn

In one room silence is thinking. In another it is refusal. In another it is the interpreter working. In another it is a dropped connection, or three people typing at once. This page will not hand you a lookup table for it, because that table does not exist — and a fake table is worse than no table, since you would act on it. What replaces it is one habit: watch two calls in that room before you decide what its silences mean, then put the decision in writing afterwards.

Silêncio é informação, não falta de educação. E não dá para ler de fora: observe duas chamadas antes de decidir o que o silêncio daquela sala significa.

A. Four silences, one defensible reading

For each scene, pick the reading you can actually defend with the information you have.

1.Yuki goes quiet for eight seconds after “can you present on Friday?”
2.Noor says “fine.” and moves to the next slide.
3.The teacher asks “any questions?” and the whole room is silent for five seconds.
4.You are interrupted twice in the same call.

B. Declared caution, and the line you keep

From outside a room you cannot reliably tell thinking from refusal, and nobody who tells you otherwise has tested it. So the plan is: no conclusions from one silence, two calls of watching before a judgement, and the decision written down after every call — a silence that was never written down becomes an argument three weeks later.

1.Write the line you will keep for silence — the one that turns a pause into information instead of a guess.

7. Face is the currency

Face is a person’s public standing in the room — how they look to the others while the sentence is still in the air. A message that costs someone their face costs you the task, because from that moment their priority is defending their standing, and your fix becomes optional. Face is not bought with compliments. It is bought with sentences that leave a way out.

Face = a imagem pública da pessoa. Se a sua frase tira a face de alguém, essa pessoa passa a defender a imagem — e o seu problema fica para depois.

8. Accommodating is not imitating an accent

You do not have to sound British to be understood by a British person, and copying an accent badly makes you harder to follow, not easier. What actually helps is three moves and one habit — and all four are free.

Acomodar não é imitar sotaque. São três movimentos e um hábito, e nenhum deles custa nada.

A. The three moves

1. Slower, not louder. Loudness fixes nothing — a listener who cannot decode a word will not decode it at a higher volume. Slow the sentence: a short pause where the meaning turns, not a gap between every letter.

2. Shorter sentences. One idea per sentence. The clause stacked on the clause is what kills you: by the third one, the listener is still holding the first one.

3. Less slang. The tax from section 2 — and it is the first thing to drop, because it is the thing that costs the most attention per word.

B. The one habit: write the key line

Before the call ends, the line the decision hangs on goes into the chat — in writing, in plain words. Spoken English disappears the second it is said; written English is the version the quiet room can answer an hour later, and the version that stops the same conversation happening twice.

The line is never a summary. It is the decision:

“Frog clips: Noor, Monday, 09:00. Cut: you, Tuesday. Video: Yuki — final answer Thursday.”

Escrito assim, ninguém precisa lembrar do que foi dito. E quem estava calado na chamada tem uma chance de responder.
