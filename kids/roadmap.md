# KIDS-ROADMAP

*Every entry is one lesson. The heading names the concept and the general context it lives in. `Focus` says the concept in plain words. `Vocabulary` is what that context needs, and a word moves lessons with its scene. `Examples` carries only the phrases the vocabulary and the rule cannot build, so a lesson with none says nothing there. `Real-world relevance` is one line on where a 14-year-old meets it.*

## [1] - Letters Are Not Sounds, and a Portuguese Ending Is a Shortcut - Game titles, stream captions and words a kid already reads

   - Focus: English letters do not have fixed sounds, and a Portuguese-looking ending tells you how the word is actually said - the two codes that make English readable.
   - Deepens: -
   - Uses: -
   - Vocabulary: video, live, clip, stream, player, game, action, invention, famous, natural, possible, distance
   - Examples: "we're live"
   - Real-world relevance: These are the words on every stream, clip and game menu a 14-year-old reads, and the Portuguese reading of them is wrong in the same two ways.

## [2] - The Silent E, Two Vowels as One Sound, and the Cognates That Lie - Reading words out loud with a friend, and a bet on who gets them right

   - Focus: a silent e at the end flips the vowel in front of it, two vowels together make one long sound, and six words that look Portuguese mean something else.
   - Deepens: letters are not sounds (lesson 1) - the overview becomes a worked rule: two spellings that predict the sound, and the cognates the rule cannot rescue.
   - Uses: -
   - Vocabulary: parents, relatives, actually, currently, library, bookstore, college, school, push, pull, pretend, meet, mean, sheesh, slay
   - Examples: -
   - Real-world relevance: `library` when you meant a bookstore, and `push` at a door that says pull, is the wrong word with the wrong action in front of the person who notices.

## [3] - 'To Be' and Its Contractions - Descriptions, and how a day at school is going

   - Focus: 'be' as a state and what its contractions hide, plus 'get' and an empty 'it': how you are, what you got, and what the weather is doing.
   - Deepens: the silent e and the vowel pairs (lesson 2) - the vowel that was read alone last lesson now disappears inside I'm and it's, so the spelling stops predicting anything.
   - Uses: -
   - Vocabulary: ready, late, sick, dead, free, battery, cooked, "locked in", hungry, bored, tired
   - Examples: "dead tired" · "I'm good, thanks"
   - Real-world relevance: The state of a kid's day is what they report all day long, and every one of those sentences is be or get.

## [4] - Have, Do, Make and Go - Chores, schoolwork and pocket money at home

   - Focus: the four verbs that cover what Portuguese splits and merges with ter and fazer: what you own, what you do, what you make, and where you go.
   - Deepens: 'be' as a state (lesson 3) - be named how you are; these four name what you own, perform or move toward, so the same short sentence now carries an action.
   - Uses: the wildcard 'get' (lesson 3) - it is still the fallback when none of the four fits, and it is checked against them here.
   - Vocabulary: chore, homework, dish, mess, room, project, money, job, idea, "have fun", drip, glow-up
   - Examples: "I have no idea"
   - Real-world relevance: Pocket money, chores and school projects are a kid's whole economy, and Portuguese covers all of it with ter and fazer.

## [5] - a, the and there is - Places around the neighborhood and things in a room

   - Focus: a introduces a thing, the points back at one already known, and there is says that something exists.
   - Deepens: have and go (lesson 4) - Portuguese "tem" covers both owning and existing, so this lesson splits have from there is and drills the split.
   - Uses: the place words that go lands on (lesson 4).
   - Vocabulary: mall, cinema, gym, field, court, "bus stop", park, home, snack, baddie
   - Examples: "go home"
   - Real-world relevance: Every description of where something is starts with a or the, and "tem um parque perto daqui" is the sentence a Brazilian gets wrong most often.

## [6] - The Sentence Frame and the Only Mark the Verb Takes - Talking about other people in a group chat

   - Focus: the frame every English sentence sits in, and the one place the verb itself changes: the -s of he, she and it.
   - Deepens: 'be' as a state (lesson 3) - be was one verb with its own forms; the same slot now takes any verb, and only he, she and it leave a mark on it.
   - Uses: the place words from go (lesson 4) - they fill the last slot of the frame.
   - Vocabulary: walk, run, eat, watch, study, sleep, play, like, want, ghost, yap
   - Examples: -
   - Real-world relevance: The frame is what makes a sentence the kid has never heard sayable, and the third-person -s is what turns ghost and yap into the two verbs a group chat uses most about other people.

## [7] - Saying No: don't and doesn't - Turning down a request, an invite or a dare

   - Focus: the -s leaves the verb and sits on don't or doesn't, and the verb goes back to its base form.
   - Deepens: the third-person -s (lesson 6) - the mark the verb carried alone moves onto does, and the verb itself goes bare.
   - Uses: the sentence frame (lesson 6) - the negative is the frame with the mode slot filled and nothing else moved.
   - Vocabulary: know, need, speak, care, mind, worry, bother, "don't worry", "not really", "no way", "sleep on"
   - Examples: "I don't bite" · "never mind" · "it's not that deep"
   - Real-world relevance: Refusing, disagreeing and saying "I can't" is most of what a teenager says to a parent, and "he doesn't plays" is the mistake that never dies.

## [8] - Asking: do, does and the Short Answer - Joining a lobby, swapping handles and finding the score

   - Focus: do and does move in front of the subject, a wh-word goes first, and the short answer repeats the mode instead of the verb.
   - Deepens: do-support in the negative (lesson 7) - do already filled the slot to say no; here it only changes place, so nothing new enters the sentence.
   - Uses: the frame and the -s (lessons 6 and 7) - the -s is what decides do or does, and it stays on the subject side in who plays.
   - Vocabulary: password, username, nickname, score, seat, team, price, name, phone, favorite, rizz, "main character"
   - Examples: "what's up?" · "you good?" · "how come?"
   - Real-world relevance: Joining a lobby, swapping handles or finding the score are all questions, and a kid who cannot ask stays outside the group.

## [9] - The Simple Present Is a Fact, Never Right Now - Arguing about games, animals and songs

   - Focus: the simple present means a fact, a habit or a rule of the world, and it never means happening now.
   - Deepens: the third-person -s (lesson 6) - the -s gets its real job here: it marks a fact, and a fact never marks a moment.
   - Uses: the sentence frame (lesson 6) - every sentence here is the frame with an empty mode slot.
   - Vocabulary: cat, dog, spider, snake, bite, sting, hate, slaps, "hits different"
   - Examples: "it is what it is"
   - Real-world relevance: Facts are how a kid argues about games, animals and rules, and judging a song as one that slaps is a fact statement, not a moment.

## [10] - Frequency, Routine and the Words for Time - A school day, from the alarm to bedtime

   - Focus: frequency words sit in their own slot, a routine is told in order, and in, on and at take their places when the routine needs a time.
   - Deepens: the simple present as a fact (lesson 9) - a habit was one kind of fact; here it is placed in time, with the frequency word in its slot and the steps in order.
   - Uses: the third-person -s and the empty 'it' (lessons 6 and 3) - the routine is third person half the time, and the clock needs the empty it.
   - Vocabulary: alarm, "hit snooze", breakfast, shower, uniform, weekday, weekend, "on time", doomscroll, lowkey, lunch, dinner
   - Examples: "all the time"
   - Real-world relevance: The routine is the only thing a kid repeats every day, which makes it the cheapest place to make the grammar automatic.

## [11] - be + -ing: the Thing Happening Now - A phone that is charging, scrolling and downloading

   - Focus: be + -ing carries the action in progress, and -ing has its own spelling rules.
   - Deepens: 'be' as a state (lesson 3) - be carried a state alone; with -ing behind it the same be carries something happening, which is the one thing the fact form cannot say.
   - Uses: the frame and the fact form (lessons 6 and 9) - the frame is unchanged, and the fact form is the contrast this lesson is measured against.
   - Vocabulary: upload, search, scroll, type, click, charge, "log in", chat, download, update, lol, wtf
   - Examples: "on my way"
   - Real-world relevance: A kid narrating what the phone is doing is this form's natural habitat, and "estou baixando" said as "I download" exposes the mistake instantly.

## [12] - Fact or Moment: One Decision per Sentence - A group chat while everyone is doing something different

   - Focus: the same verb in both present forms, standing side by side, so the choice stops being a guess.
   - Deepens: the fact form and the right-now form (lessons 9 and 11) - the two are put against each other, so two separate rules become one decision made per sentence.
   - Uses: the frame and the do-question (lessons 6 and 8) - the two question forms are the sharpest test of the choice.
   - Vocabulary: charger, headphones, noise, volume, screen, sofa, fridge, "living room", blanket, curtain, tweaking, "crash out"
   - Examples: -
   - Real-world relevance: "O que você está fazendo?" answered with "I study" is what a Brazilian sounds like, and a kid who says "you're tweaking" in the moment and "he crashes out every time" as a fact has made the choice twice in one breath.

## [13] - The Verbs That Refuse -ing - Opinions about people, food and videos

   - Focus: know, like, want, need, hate, believe and the senses take no -ing, even when the moment is right now.
   - Deepens: the right-now form (lesson 11) - these verbs are defined by what they refuse, so the -ing form has to be on screen to be refused.
   - Uses: the fact form (lesson 9) - the fact form is forced on this set of verbs even when the moment is now.
   - Vocabulary: taste, sound, smell, opinion, truth, joke, secret, bussin, "the ick", "understand the assignment"
   - Examples: "that's not it" · "I can't stand it"
   - Real-world relevance: A teenager states opinions and wants all day, and "I'm wanting" is the mark of a student who learned -ing as a decoration.

## [14] - can: One Word, Three Jobs - What a kid can do, and what they are allowed to do

   - Focus: can carries ability, permission and requests, and it never changes shape.
   - Deepens: the base form after a filled mode (lesson 6) - can is the first mode with no -s to give, so the rule finally shows on a word that cannot change.
   - Uses: the question slot that do takes (lesson 8) - can takes the same slot, which is the whole reason it counts as a mode.
   - Vocabulary: skate, guitar, trick, match, coach, level, skill, clutch, carry, "ride a bike"
   - Examples: "I can't help it"
   - Real-world relevance: "I can't" and "can I" open every door in a lobby and in a house with rules, and the spoken can/can't difference decides whether the answer was yes.

## [15] - to and for: One Portuguese Word, Two English Ones - Gifts, favors and thanks at home and at school

   - Focus: to marks direction and the infinitive, for marks purpose and the person who benefits, and para splits into both.
   - Deepens: go plus a place (lesson 4) - go needed a place; here the word in front of that place is explained, so to stops being copied and becomes a marker.
   - Uses: requests with can (lesson 14) - every request here is built on can.
   - Vocabulary: gift, card, favor, prize, ride, winner, coin, tip, balloon, "for the plot", "no cap"
   - Examples: "give me a sec"
   - Real-world relevance: "para" is one word in Portuguese and two in English, so this is the split that marks a speaker as translating instead of speaking.

## [16] - in, on and at: Contact, Container, Point - Where things are in a room, and when things happen in a week

   - Focus: on is contact, in is a container, at is a point - in space and in time.
   - Deepens: to as a direction (lesson 15) - to showed movement toward a place; on, in and at describe the shape of where it landed.
   - Uses: the place words and their articles (lessons 4 and 5) - the place needs its article before the preposition can be chosen.
   - Vocabulary: box, drawer, pocket, wall, shelf, corner, floor, roof, gate, door, "on god", "in my feels"
   - Examples: "at the moment"
   - Real-world relevance: Finding things and fixing times is most of what a kid texts about, and one wrong preposition is the error a native speaker hears first.

## [17] - Verbs That Carry Their Preposition - A group chat about plans, payback and worries

   - Focus: some verbs take their preposition with them, the pair is the word, a different preposition is a different meaning, and the object can wait at the end of a question.
   - Deepens: to as a marker (lesson 15) - to stood on its own there; here it is welded to verbs, so listen to becomes one unit instead of a choice.
   - Uses: wh-questions and requests (lessons 8 and 14) - they are the two frames the pairs are drilled in.
   - Vocabulary: "wait for", "depend on", "listen to", "agree with", "belong to", "worry about", "ask for", "pay for", "laugh at", "think about", "hit me up", "hop off"
   - Examples: "it depends" · "it's on me"
   - Real-world relevance: These pairs cover waiting, paying, worrying and listening, which is what a group chat is made of.

## [18] - was, were and there was - Where you were and how it was: a party, a trip, a game

   - Focus: was and were are the past of be, there was and there were say what existed, and be is the one verb that never borrows did.
   - Deepens: 'be' as a state (lesson 3) - the same verb takes the past here, and it is the only verb in the course that never borrows did.
   - Uses: there is and the articles (lesson 5) - there is becomes there was, and the noun after it still decides the form.
   - Vocabulary: weather, party, trip, crowd, line, beach, hill, farm, hotel, beef, gucci
   - Examples: "there was this one time"
   - Real-world relevance: Where you were and how it was is the opening of every story a kid tells, and "I did be" is what comes out when be is treated like any other verb.

## [19] - -ed and the Words That Force It - Yesterday at school and at home

   - Focus: regular verbs build their past with -ed, -ed makes three different sounds, and the finished time words force it.
   - Deepens: was and were (lesson 18) - be had its own past form; every other verb builds one, and -ed is the default build.
   - Uses: the time words that take no preposition (lesson 10) - yesterday and last night take none, which is the contrast drilled here.
   - Vocabulary: visit, start, finish, stop, call, help, move, order, arrive, answer, fumbled, washed
   - Examples: -
   - Real-world relevance: Recounting yesterday is the most common past act in a kid's day, and the -ed ending is the first place a Brazilian accent shows.

## [20] - The Verbs That Ignore -ed - A lost charger, a broken controller and a fight

   - Focus: the verbs that build their own past, and did: the past mode that takes the mark so the verb can go back to base.
   - Deepens: regular -ed (lesson 19) - -ed was the default; these verbs refuse it, and because they are the most frequent verbs in the language they are learned as families.
   - Uses: the mark moving onto does (lesson 7) and the do-question (lesson 8) - did is the same migration and the same fronting.
   - Vocabulary: take, keep, leave, find, buy, bring, catch, throw, break, fall, "took an L", "caught a W"
   - Examples: "you had to be there"
   - Real-world relevance: Every story about a lost charger, a broken controller or a fight is made of these verbs, and "did you went" is the error that survives years of lessons.

## [21] - Telling It in the Past, in Order - Retelling a match, a stream or a school fight

   - Focus: the finished time that forces the form, the choice between an action and a state, and the linkers that make the order audible.
   - Deepens: the irregular past and the time markers (lessons 20 and 19) - the two past families come together as one story: the time word opens it and the irregulars carry it.
   - Uses: did-questions and there was (lessons 20 and 18) - the questions are asked with did, and the scene is set with there was.
   - Vocabulary: then, "at first", "in the end", suddenly, later, finally, luckily, anyway, immediately, afterwards, "plot twist", "out of pocket"
   - Examples: "long story short" · "at the end of the day" · "out of nowhere"
   - Real-world relevance: Retelling a match or a school fight in order is the most requested act of a teenage speaker, and the linkers are what keep the listener oriented.

## [22] - Whose Is It: Pronouns and Possessives - Arguments over a charger, a seat or a controller

   - Focus: object pronouns, possessive adjectives and pronouns, and the reflexive that points back at the subject.
   - Deepens: the articles (lesson 5) - articles point at a thing without naming an owner; possessives name the owner instead, and the two never both appear.
   - Uses: the verbs with prepositions (lesson 17) - the object form is what follows the preposition: with me, for them.
   - Vocabulary: cousin, neighbor, uncle, aunt, mom, dad, twin, brother, sister, group, "my bad", "your loss"
   - Examples: "give it back"
   - Real-world relevance: Ownership fights over a charger, a seat or a phone are settled with these forms, and "the my phone" marks a beginner forever.

## [23] - Commands, let's and the Polite Version - App tutorials, game controls and asking for things

   - Focus: the bare verb with no subject, don't for no, let's for us, and the softeners that keep a command polite.
   - Deepens: the sentence frame (lesson 6) - the frame loses its subject and keeps everything else, which is why the verb cannot take -s.
   - Uses: don't as a negative (lesson 7) - it negated an action there; here it negates a command with no subject in front of it.
   - Vocabulary: press, hold, tap, swipe, drag, skip, pause, mute, restart, "log out", chill, dip
   - Examples: "hold on" · "just kidding"
   - Real-world relevance: Every tutorial, every app and every request to a parent is an imperative, and it is the same form that makes "chill" a joke with a friend and an insult in a teacher's mouth.

## [24] - How Much and How Many - The fridge, the school canteen and the bill

   - Focus: countable against uncountable, some and any, much and many, and a lot of as the safe default.
   - Deepens: the general statement with no article (lesson 5) - the general statement took no article; here it takes a quantity word, and which one depends on whether the noun is countable.
   - Uses: there is and the wh-question (lessons 5 and 8) - how much and how many open the question, and there is finishes the answer.
   - Vocabulary: soda, juice, bread, rice, sugar, meat, fruit, bottle, packet, aura, copium, brainrot
   - Examples: "none of your business"
   - Real-world relevance: Ordering food, splitting a bill and checking what is left in the fridge all live here, and "many money" is the giveaway that the countable split was never made.

## [25] - EXAM: the First Block, as One Conversation - A routine told in facts, a live call in the middle of something, and a text about last weekend

   - Focus: the block is crossed as one conversation in three moves - a routine told in facts, a live call in the middle of something, and a text about last weekend - and each answer is scored against ten families instead of one question per concept, so a family that fails is re-asked inside a different scene before the lesson ends.
   - Uses: the sentence frame, saying no, the -s of the third person, facts against right now, the verbs that refuse -ing, the irregular past, did, to and for, the prepositions of time, and the wh-question.

## [26] - gonna: the Future You Say Out Loud - Weekend plans in a group chat

   - Focus: gonna is how English says the future out loud, and it is not the verb go.
   - Deepens: go plus a place (lesson 4) - go with a place was movement; with gonna in front of a verb the same words point at the future, and the two are judged against each other here.
   - Uses: the frame and the right-now form (lessons 6 and 11) - the future sits in the mode slot, and the right-now form is what it is contrasted with.
   - Vocabulary: reminder, "group chat", selfie, movie, concert, camping, barbecue, finna, "fit check", invite
   - Examples: -
   - Real-world relevance: Plans are a kid's main social currency, and "I'm going to the mall" against "I'm gonna go to the mall" is a difference they hear on stream every day.

## [27] - will: the Decision You Make Mid-Sentence - Promising, offering and turning a favor down

   - Focus: will is the decision taken as you speak, against gonna, which is the plan that was already made.
   - Deepens: gonna (lesson 26) - gonna covered a plan already made; will is the other half of the future, so this lesson is a choice between two modes rather than a new tense.
   - Uses: requests with can and the wh-question (lessons 14 and 8) - the offer and the question are the two frames every will sentence sits in.
   - Vocabulary: promise, deal, "in a minute", probably, sure, maybe, "of course", definitely, "no worries", "say less", bet
   - Examples: "count me in"
   - Real-world relevance: Promising, offering and refusing politely is how a kid negotiates a favor with a parent or a friend, and will is what makes it a promise instead of a plan.

## [28] - The Arranged Future: a Time Turns -ing into Tomorrow - A practice, a tournament and a deadline this week

   - Focus: be + -ing with a time is an arrangement already in someone's calendar, and the time word is what makes the future audible.
   - Deepens: the right-now form and gonna (lessons 11 and 26) - the continuous meant now and gonna meant intention; a fixed arrangement is the third future and is built from those two.
   - Uses: the time prepositions and the wh-question (lessons 16 and 8) - the appointment needs in, on or at, and the confirmation is a question.
   - Vocabulary: practice, tournament, deadline, tomorrow, tonight, "next time", "the day after", midday, "link up", "pull up"
   - Examples: "same time next week"
   - Real-world relevance: A practice, a tournament and a deadline are the three fixed points of a kid's week, and dropping the time word is what makes English sound vague instead of arranged.

## [29] - The get Family: One Root, Four Particles - Getting up, getting the bus, and getting along with people

   - Focus: one root plus a particle is a different action each time, and the object has its place.
   - Deepens: get as the wildcard (lesson 3) - get did four jobs alone; with particles it becomes a family, and the wildcard finally has directions.
   - Uses: on as contact, and the preposition that changes meaning (lessons 16 and 17) - the particle behaves like that preposition, and the pair is the unit in both.
   - Vocabulary: "get up", "get back", "get on", "get off", "get in", "get out", "get over", "get along", "get together", "get away", "get mogged", "get gassed"
   - Examples: "I don't get it"
   - Real-world relevance: Getting up, getting on a bus and getting along with someone are three sentences a kid needs daily, and English says all three with get.

## [30] - The look Family, and the Particle That Splits - Searching a word, checking a message and watching a little brother

   - Focus: the same pattern with a second root, plus the particle that splits when a pronoun shows up.
   - Deepens: the get family and the object after the particle (lesson 29) - get showed the family pattern; look repeats it with a new root, so the pattern becomes a method.
   - Uses: preposition pairs and the object pronouns (lessons 17 and 22) - the split exists because the pronoun is an object form, so the two rules meet here.
   - Vocabulary: "look at", "look for", "look up", "look over", "look after", "look out", "look forward to", "look around", "look into", "look away", looksmaxxing, "skill issue"
   - Examples: "look who it is"
   - Real-world relevance: Searching a word, checking a message and watching a sibling are all look, and the formal twins - consult, verify, supervise - are words to recognize, never to use by default.

## [31] - Fast English: wanna, gotta, kinda - A stream, a rap verse and a group voice note

   - Focus: what English sounds like at speed - wanna, gotta, kinda - plus the words that shrink and the consonants that slide into each other.
   - Deepens: gonna as a mode and a reduction (lesson 26) - the rest of the set joins it here, and linking is added under them.
   - Uses: the spoken difference between can and can't (lesson 14) - the weak-form principle was already met there, so this is one rule and four more examples of it.
   - Vocabulary: wanna, gotta, kinda, sorta, dunno, lemme, gimme, yeah, nope, yep, ain't, tryna
   - Examples: "wait, what?" · "ain't no way"
   - Real-world relevance: This is the English of a stream, a rap verse and a voice note, and a kid who knows only the written forms cannot hear the spoken ones.

## [32] - have + Past Participle - Gossip about what someone has and has not done

   - Focus: the third form of the verb, have or has in front of it, and the pile of participles that are neither the base nor the past.
   - Deepens: the irregular past (lesson 20) - the irregulars gave the second form; this needs the third, so the table gains one column and the pattern carries over.
   - Uses: have as possession and -ed as the regular participle (lessons 4 and 19) - have is the mode now rather than possession, and -ed is the participle the regular half already knows.
   - Vocabulary: write, win, lose, hear, feel, send, spend, sell, tell, glazing, "beating the allegations"
   - Examples: "I'm done" · "I've had enough"
   - Real-world relevance: "I've seen it" and "I saw it" split the two pasts a kid mixes most, and the third column is where a Brazilian gets stuck because Portuguese has no such form.

## [33] - The Perfect for a Duration - How long you have had a phone, a friend or a game account

   - Focus: the perfect for something that started in the past and is still true now, which is the trap Portuguese sets with the simple present.
   - Deepens: have + participle (lesson 32) - the form is put to one job here, and the job is a duration rather than a finished event.
   - Uses: the fact form and the finished time words (lessons 9 and 19) - the simple present is exactly what this lesson forbids, and a finished time word is what would move the sentence into the past.
   - Vocabulary: "all day", "all week", "for ages", "a long time", "so far", lately, recently, "these days", still, "since forever", "down bad"
   - Examples: "long time no see" · "so far so good"
   - Real-world relevance: "Trabalho aqui há cinco anos" said as "I work here for five years" is the single sentence that identifies a Brazilian speaker.

## [34] - The Perfect for Experience - Trips, and the things you have never tried

   - Focus: ever, never, just, already and yet for experience, and the when-question that forces the past instead.
   - Deepens: the perfect for duration (lesson 33) - duration was one use of the perfect; experience is the other, and the time word is what decides between them.
   - Uses: did-questions and the wh-question (lessons 20 and 8) - the when-question belongs to the past and is drilled as the counter-example.
   - Vocabulary: try, abroad, mountain, waterfall, cave, island, jungle, snowboard, hike, surf, "never have I ever", "touch grass"
   - Examples: "I've been there"
   - Real-world relevance: Comparing what you have and have not done is the conversation a kid has with a stranger on the first day of anything.

## [35] - since and for: a Point and a Length - A friendship, a phone and a game account with a start date

   - Focus: since marks the starting point, for marks the length, and the how-long question needs both.
   - Deepens: the perfect for duration (lesson 33) - duration had no anchor; since and for are the two anchors, one a point in time and one a length of it.
   - Uses: the time prepositions and the wh-question (lessons 16 and 8) - the point after since behaves like an at or an in, and the question is the wh-frame again.
   - Vocabulary: age, childhood, "high school", "first time", "last time", month, year, decade, anniversary, "day one", "ride or die"
   - Examples: "we go way back"
   - Real-world relevance: How long you have had a phone, a friend or an account is small talk a kid runs on, and mixing since with for turns a clear answer into a puzzle.

## [36] - Comparing Two Things - Phones, teams and prices in a school corridor

   - Focus: -er or more, the than that follows, and the four forms that refuse both.
