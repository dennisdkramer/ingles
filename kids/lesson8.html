Asking — Do, Does & Who

A kid who can't ask stays outside the lobby, outside the group, outside the joke. Good news: asking in English isn't new vocabulary — it's the same sentence with the do moved to the front.

1. Joining the lobby

Nando is trying to get into Duda's game. Watch how many questions it takes.

A. Grupo "entra no lobby" — 20:12

entra no lobby 🎮

Nando: what's your username?

Duda: dudazord_2011. do you have the password?

Nando: no, I don't. send it

Duda: does your brother play on your team?

Nando: no, he doesn't. he's 8, he's cooked

Duda: who plays on your team then?

Nando: just me. is the lobby open?

Duda: are you serious rn

B. The move: the marker jumps in front

✅ Statement❓ Question
You have the password.Do you have the password?
He plays on your team.Does he play on your team?
It is far.Is it far?
Nothing new enters the sentence. The do you already use for don't slides to the front, and the verb gives its -s back: he plays → does he play?. Portuguese asks with intonation — "Ele joga no seu time?" — English asks with word order. That's the whole change.

Watch what happens to have: she has → does she have. The -s packs its bags and moves onto does, and the verb goes back to the plain form. Same rule as doesn't — this is the same do, just standing at the front instead of carrying a not.

4. Short answers: repeat the do, not the verb

A. "Sim, eu tenho" is not "Yes, I have"

Question✅ Short answer❌ never
Do you have the password?Yes, I do. / No, I don't.Yes, I have.
Does he play on your team?Yes, he does. / No, he doesn't.Yes, he plays.
Do they live near the mall?Yes, they do. / No, they don't.Yes, they live.
Is it far?Yes, it is. / No, it isn't.Yes, it's far.
The short answer echoes the first word of the question — do, does or is. That's why "Yes, I have" sounds wrong even though "sim, eu tenho" is perfect Portuguese. English repeats the helper, not the verb.

5. Wh-questions: the question word takes the front seat

A. Wh + do/does + subject + verb

Whdo/doesSubjectVerbQuestion
WhatdoyouneedWhat do you need?
WheredoesheliveWhere does he live?
WhendotheyplayWhen do they play?
WhydoesshestudyWhy does she study English?
What—is—What's your username?
Two shapes only. If there's a normal verb, the do/does is compulsory: Where does he live? If the verb is be, the be moves instead and do never shows up: Where is the lobby? — never "Where does the lobby is?". And the question word always goes first, before everything.

B. Build the wh-question

1. Where he live? (ele mora)
2. What you need? (você precisa)
3. When the game start? (o jogo começa)
4. Why they play every day? (eles jogam)
5. Where my phone? (está)
6. What time the stream start? (a live começa)

6. Who is doing it? The one question that breaks the rule

A. Who as the subject

QuestionWho is…What happens
Who plays on your team?the subject — the person doing itno do, and the verb keeps its -s
Who do you play with?the object — the person you play withdo appears, verb is plain
The test: is there another subject in the question? "Who plays on your team?" — nobody else is doing anything, who is the doer, so no do. "Who do you play with?" — you is the doer, so do is needed. English never uses do when the question word is the one doing the action. "Who does play?" só existe para dar ênfase, não como pergunta normal.

9. You say it — ask the question

The answer is on the page. You write the question that produces it. Watch the first word: do, does, is or a wh-word.

A. Six answers, six questions

1. Pergunte o nome de usuário dele. → Answer: dudazord_2011.
2. Pergunte se ela tem a senha. → Answer: Yes, she does.
3. Pergunte onde fica o lobby. → Answer: In the mall.
4. Pergunte quem joga no time dele. → Answer: Nando does.
5. Pergunte quando o jogo começa. → Answer: At eight.
6. Pergunte se eles moram perto do shopping. → Answer: No, they don't.

B. Ask them out loud

Read all six questions out loud with a rising voice at the end — that rise is what makes a question land. Then record three of them. Pergunta sem subida de voz vira ordem.
