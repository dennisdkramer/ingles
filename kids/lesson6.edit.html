One Frame, One -S — That's It

Every English sentence — meme, threat, love letter, FIFA excuse — runs on the same six slots. And out of all of them, only he, she and it leave a mark on the verb: one -s.

1. The frame you already use without knowing

Look at what Nando writes in the group chat. Then look at what his little brother writes.

A. Grupo "fifa depois da escola" — 17:22

fifa depois da escola 🎮

Caio (8 anos): he play FIFA every day 😭

Duda: Caio. he playS. one letter, bro

Caio: why

Duda: because he, she and it always get the -s. that's the rule. everything else gets nothing 😌

Caio: that's cooked

Nando: he's 8 and he already hates grammar. legend.

Only slots 1 and 2 are compulsory. The rest fill up as your sentence needs them: I run. is a complete English sentence. Portuguese would say "Eu corro" — same shape, so this is one rule your brain already has.

2. The words that fill slot 2

Ten verbs carry this whole lesson. Learn each one inside a phrase — a naked verb is a dictionary, not English.

3. The one place the verb moves: he, she, it

Slot 2 is the laziest slot in English. I play, you play, we play, they play — the verb never changes. But a single person or thing gets one letter: he plays.

A. Same verb, one mark

I / you / we / theyhe / she / it
I play FIFA.Nando plays FIFA.
they watch moviesDuda watches movies
we study Englishshe studies English
Why only he, she, it? Old English used to put an ending on every person, like Portuguese still does: eu jogo, tu jogas, ele joga. English dropped all of them except this single -s. It survived doing one job: telling you "one person, not many".

B. How the -s lands

Type of verbWhat happensExamples
most verbsjust add -splays · likes · wants · lives · walks · runs · eats · sleeps
ends in -ch, -sh, -ss, -x, -oadd -eswatches · finishes · goes · does
consonant + yy → iesstudies · tries · cries
vowel + yjust add -splays · buys
one rebelchanges shapehave → has
Say them out loud: watches has an extra syllable (watch-iz), plays doesn't (playz). That extra syllable is why the -es exists at all — "watchs" is unsayable.

4. be vs every other verb

Last lesson be was the star: I am, you are, he is. It changes shape for every person. Now slot 2 accepts any other verb — and those are far lazier.

A. Two different animals

Subjectbe — changes every timeplay — changes once
Iamplay
youareplay
he / she / itisplays
weareplay
theyareplay
be is the odd one out — five shapes, and you already learned them. Every other verb has TWO: the plain form and the -s form. That's the whole system. If you can say "he plays", you can build thousands of sentences.

B. One verb per sentence — never both

❌ cooked✅ EnglishWhat it means
He is play soccer.He plays soccer.action — every day
He play tired.He is tired.state — right now, so be
I am want a snack.I want a snack.want is a verb on its own
Slot 2 holds ONE verb. Either you describe how somebody is (am / is / are) or you say what they do (play, want, eat…). Never glue them: "I am want" is the single most Brazilian sentence in existence.

5. Where the extra words go

Slots 3, 4 and 5 line up in a fixed order: object → place → time. Portuguese does the same thing most of the time, so this part is a gift — but the order is not optional.

A. The stacking order

SubjectVerbObjectPlaceTime
IplayFIFAat homeevery day
Shewatchesmoviesat the mallon the weekend
TheystudyEnglishat schoolin the morning
Read one row out loud as one breath: "I play FIFA at home every day." The place always comes before the time. If you flip them, English still understands you — it just sounds translated.

B. The two usual wrecks

❌ translated from Portuguese✅ the frame
I play every day FIFA at home.I play FIFA at home every day.
He watches at the mall movies.He watches movies at the mall.
Time at the very front is allowed in English for drama — "Every day I play FIFA." — but put it at the end unless you're making a point. Object never splits from the verb.
