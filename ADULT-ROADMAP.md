# ADULT-ROADMAP — real life, self-expression, and the language people actually speak

## [1] - `sound/letter-vs-sound`, `sound/cognate-suffix-code`, `sound/reduction-preview` · nation, natural, television · "This restaurant is a f---in' rip-off, bro."

   - Focus: open the track, show that English vowels have no fixed sound, and prove that Portuguese already hands you thousands of English words.
   - Deepens: —
   - Uses: —
   - Vocabulary: restaurant, problem, important, different, music, possible, conference
   - Examples: "Music is f---in' important to me — that shi- is my whole life." · "My conference is different this year — last year was pure bullshi-." · "Television? Nah, I watch everything on my phone."
   - Real-world relevance: These are the words the student already meets in every song, menu, meme and work email, so the first win is loud and instant.

## [2] - `sound/vowel-pairs`, `sound/false-friend`, `sound/cognate-stress` · ship, sheep, pool · "Don't f---in' push, man — it's a pull door."

   - Focus: drill the vowel pairs that decide meaning and flag the cognates that look safe but set traps.
   - Deepens: `sound/cognate-suffix-code` — now where the stress actually lands on a -tion/-ic/-ity word, just before the ending.
   - Uses: `sound/letter-vs-sound`
   - Vocabulary: pull, push, actually, parents, college, library, pretend
   - Examples: "Pull up to the pool at nine — the whole crew's f---in' there." · "Actually, my parents would lose their shi- if they saw this." · "You pretend you don't know, but you f---in' do."
   - Real-world relevance: The beach-beach/bitch, pull/push and actually/atualmente mix-ups blow up in real conversations, so they are drilled here before a friend corrects the student in public.

## [3] - `word-order/skeleton`, `verb/be-identity`, `mode/empty-vs-filled` · guy, kid, buddy · "That guy is my f---in' buddy from the gym."

   - Focus: lock the fixed word order and the be-verb identity sentences that let the student say who and how they are.
   - Deepens: —
   - Uses: —
   - Vocabulary: bro, chill, tired, drunk, broke, hungry, done
   - Examples: "I'm broke, bro — I'm f---in' done this week." · "The kid is tired as shi-, leave him alone." · "You're drunk, you're not chill — go the f--- home."
   - Real-world relevance: The whole skeleton runs a bar scene, a group chat, and a family dinner, so ordering words right is the very first thing a stranger hears.

## [4] - `present-simple/facts-not-now`, `present-simple/third-person-s`, `present-simple/pt-now-trap`, `adverb/frequency-position` · "wake up", "get up", shower · "She always f---in' sleeps in on Sunday, so don't call her."

   - Focus: teach the simple present as facts and habits only, and kill the Portuguese habit of using it for the present moment.
   - Deepens: —
   - Uses: `word-order/skeleton`, `verb/be-identity`
   - Vocabulary: commute, "hang out", gym, "sleep in", "pass out", "eat out", routine
   - Examples: "He never eats out — the guy's broke as shi-." · "I wake up at six, hit the gym, and I'm done by nine." · "We usually hang out, then I pass the f--- out on the couch."
   - Real-world relevance: First dates and office small talk run almost entirely on routines, so getting the -s and the fact-not-now rule right is the difference between sounding fluent and sounding translated.

## [5] - `present-continuous/now`, `present-continuous/temporary`, `present-simple/vs-continuous-now` · "chill out", "mess around", "work out" · "What the f--- are you doing right now?"

   - Focus: put the student inside the moment with be + -ing, and draw the hard line against the simple present.
   - Deepens: `present-simple/facts-not-now` — the same scene now split by meaning: in general takes the simple, this minute takes the continuous.
   - Uses: `present-simple/pt-now-trap`, `sound/letter-vs-sound`
   - Vocabulary: "hang on", "kick back", "pig out", "right now", currently, "at the moment", "be kidding"
   - Examples: "I'm just chilling out, so come the f--- over." · "You're f---in' kidding me — at the moment? Seriously?" · "We're pigging out, so don't f---in' judge me."
   - Real-world relevance: Answering "what are you doing?" wrong is the single most common tell of a Portuguese speaker, so this is drilled in texts, calls and doorways.

## [6] - `question/be-inversion`, `question/short-answer`, `question/wh-be` · "what's up", "how's it going", "what's going on" · "What's up, you good? What the f--- happened to you?"

   - Focus: build questions and short answers with be, the one verb that never borrows do.
   - Deepens: `verb/be-identity` — the same am/is/are statement, now flipped into a question and a two-word answer.
   - Uses: `word-order/skeleton`
   - Vocabulary: "who's that", "you good", "you okay", "what's wrong", "how come", "what for", "where you at"
   - Examples: "Who the hell is that guy, what's wrong with him?" · "How come you're here, bro — where you at?" · "What's going on with her? She's not okay, no shi-."
   - Real-world relevance: These are the exact openers of a group chat, a bar hello and a worried check-in, so a student who can't flip be into a question can't start a conversation.

## [7] - `question/do-support`, `question/wh-do`, `negation/dont-doesnt`, `mode/marker-moves` · "give a shi-", "give a damn", mind · "Do you even care, or do you not give a shi-?"

   - Focus: give every non-be verb its question and negative with do/does, and move the -s onto the helper.
   - Deepens: `present-simple/third-person-s` — the -s leaves the verb now and lives on does/don't.
   - Uses: `word-order/skeleton`, `present-simple/facts-not-now`
   - Vocabulary: care, bother, even, "at all", "what the hell", "how often", "how much"
   - Examples: "Why the hell do you bother — how often do you two fight?" · "She doesn't mind, she doesn't give a damn." · "I don't give a damn — I don't like it at all."
   - Real-world relevance: The everyday fight, the group-chat poll and the nosy relative all arrive as a do-question, so a student who forgets the helper sounds foreign in every argument.

## [8] - `future/gonna-mode`, `verb/go-to-place`, `future/gonna-vs-going-to` · gonna, "gonna be", "gonna get" · "We're gonna get f---ed up tonight, so don't bail on me."

   - Focus: teach [be] gonna + verb as the natural spoken future and separate it cleanly from go + place.
   - Deepens: `sound/reduction-preview` — the squeezed form previewed in lesson one is now the real spoken future.
   - Uses: —
   - Vocabulary: party, crash, bail, "hit the gym", "call it a night", "hook up", "show off"
   - Examples: "I'm gonna call it a night — I'm crashing at your place, no shi-." · "He's gonna try to hook up, so f---in' watch him show off." · "I'm going to the office, I'm gonna be late."
   - Real-world relevance: Plans made out loud at a bar, a party or in a DM are nearly all gonna, and mixing it up with "going to the office" is a classic beginner slip.

## [9] - `future/will`, `future/wont`, `future/gonna-vs-will` · promise, swear, dare · "I won't f---in' tell anyone, I swear."

   - Focus: add will/won't as the future of promises, offers and decisions made in the moment.
   - Deepens: `future/gonna-mode` — will joins gonna as a second future: one for a plan, one for decided-now.
   - Uses: —
   - Vocabulary: "no way", "for sure", definitely, probably, maybe, deal, fine
   - Examples: "I dare you to say that shi- to her face." · "I'll get the next round, for sure — probably." · "No way — f--- that, I'll do it tomorrow."
   - Real-world relevance: Promises, dares and last-minute "I'll do it" decisions are the currency of friendships, so distinguishing will from gonna keeps the student from sounding stiff or unreliable.

## [10] - `verb/want-to-need-to`, `verb/like-enjoy-ing`, `get/wildcard`, `get/root-phrasal-family` · "get back", "get out", "get on" · "I want to get the f--- out of here — I can't get used to this city."

   - Focus: pair the preference verbs with their to/-ing shapes, then hand over get as the one word that does everything.
   - Deepens: —
   - Uses: `word-order/skeleton`
   - Vocabulary: "get over", "get along", "get it", "get used to", "get rid of", "get together", "get lost"
   - Examples: "Do you get it? Get rid of that garbage." · "We get along, so let's get together later, no shi-." · "Get back to me, and don't get f---in' lost."
   - Real-world relevance: Get appears in nearly every casual plan and complaint, so owning it plus the want/need/like shapes unlocks real requests fast.

## [11] - `preposition/to-vs-for`, `preposition/on-surface-and-days`, `preposition/in-inside-at-point`, `preposition/verb-plus-prep` · "depend on", "wait for", "talk to" · "I've been waiting for you for an hour — f---, talk to me."

   - Focus: give each core preposition one meaning, split "para" into to/for, and glue the right preposition to its verb.
   - Deepens: —
   - Uses: —
   - Vocabulary: "listen to", "look at", "go to", "belong to", "afraid of", "good at", "interested in"
   - Examples: "I'm interested in her, but I'm too f---in' scared to talk to her." · "I depend on this job, and I'm good at it." · "Listen to this shi- — look at that on the table."
   - Real-world relevance: The "para" split and shaky verb+preposition pairs are the most persistent error a Brazilian makes, so they are drilled with real verbs from the very start.

## [12] - `question/tag-check`, `discourse/backchannel`, `discourse/follow-up-question` · "for real", "no shi-", right · "For real? No shi- — tell me everything."

   - Focus: keep a conversation alive with back-channel noises, check-tags and a follow-up that returns the turn.
   - Deepens: `question/short-answer` — the short answer grows into a whole back-channel: an echo plus a check-tag.
   - Uses: `question/be-inversion`
   - Vocabulary: "you know", really, seriously, "come on", "tell me", "go ahead", "fair enough"
   - Examples: "Come on, you're f---in' kidding me, right?" · "Yeah, right — for real though, go ahead." · "Fair enough, so what the hell happened?"
   - Real-world relevance: Real talk is 50% listening noises and 50% questions; a student who only answers and never fills the gaps kills every conversation.

## [13] - `past-simple/regular-ed`, `past-simple/irregular-set`, `past-simple/empty-mode-marker`, `past/recounting-sequence` · "last night", yesterday, "the other day" · "Last night I ran into my ex — we broke up back then, it was a f---in' mess."

   - Focus: put events in the past with -ed and the core irregulars, then chain them in order.
   - Deepens: `mode/empty-vs-filled` — the empty mode now carries a past marker instead of a present one.
   - Uses: `word-order/skeleton`
   - Vocabulary: "back then", "meet up", "break up", "make out", "end up", "come across", "run into"
   - Examples: "The other day I met up with the guys and we ended up f---in' wasted." · "I came across her profile, we made out once, whatever." · "Yesterday? I worked, I crashed — that's f---in' it."
   - Real-world relevance: Every story about a night out, a fight or a hookup is a run of past simples, so the irregulars are memorized through gossip rather than tables.

## [14] - `question/did-support`, `negation/didnt`, `past-simple/was-were` · "how was", "what happened", "where were you" · "Where the f--- were you last night? What happened?"

   - Focus: run past questions and negatives off did, and keep was/were as the exception.
   - Deepens: `question/do-support` — the same helper move reused in the past, with the verb back to base.
   - Uses: `past-simple/irregular-set`
   - Vocabulary: "long time no see", "catch up", "fill in", "spill the beans", "come clean", "bring up", "go through"
   - Examples: "Did you f---in' spill the beans? Don't bring it up again." · "Long time no see — catch me up, fill me in." · "It was a mess, we went through a lot of shi-, don't ask."
   - Real-world relevance: Interrogating a friend about a night out is a did-question drill by nature, and the -ed slip ("did you went") is the loudest tell there is.

## [15] - `past/used-to`, `past/would-nostalgia`, `past/used-to-vs-simple` · "back in the day", "way back", "grow up" · "Back in the day I used to get f---ed up every weekend."

   - Focus: add used to and would as the shapes for habits and nostalgia that stopped.
   - Deepens: `past-simple/empty-mode-marker` — a third past shape now sits beside the plain past: a habit that stopped.
   - Uses: `past-simple/irregular-set`
   - Vocabulary: "move out", "settle down", "drop out", "keep up", "fall behind", "stay out", "get by"
   - Examples: "We would drink on the beach, way back, before anyone f---in' settled down." · "I dropped out, it was hard to keep up — I f---in' fell behind." · "I grew up there, then I moved out, and we got by."
   - Real-world relevance: Nostalgia talk with friends and family runs on used to and would, and mixing the two up (or using would for states) marks a learner instantly.

## [16] - `noun/countable-uncountable`, `noun/much-many`, `quantifier/a-lot-of`, `quantifier/some-any` · "a couple of", "a bunch of", "loads of" · "There's a shi- ton of beer, plenty of ice — we're f---in' fine."

   - Focus: split count and mass nouns and give each its quantifiers, then teach a lot of as the safe default.
   - Deepens: —
   - Uses: —
   - Vocabulary: "plenty of", "a bit of", enough, "tons of", "a shi- ton of", beer, cash
   - Examples: "I've got loads of cash, and a bunch of f---in' people are coming." · "A bit of music, a couple of games, enough for tonight." · "Any f---in' beer? Some cash? We need tons of it."
   - Real-world relevance: Ordering, shopping and splitting a bill all force the count/mass choice, so it is drilled on beers and cash, the two things an adult actually counts.

## [17] - `number/counts-and-prices`, `noun/plural-forms`, `contrast/this-that-these-those` · bucks, change, "rip off" · "Fifteen fifty for that? A f---in' rip-off, not worth it."

   - Focus: say numbers and prices correctly and stack the demonstratives so pointing actually works.
   - Deepens: `noun/countable-uncountable` — the numbers and plurals now ride on top of the count/mass split.
   - Uses: `quantifier/a-lot-of`
   - Vocabulary: "for free", "on sale", "half price", "a fortune", "worth it", overpriced, cheap
   - Examples: "These drinks are f---in' overpriced — those are for free." · "I got it on sale, half price — a fortune saved, no shi-." · "Got change? This shirt is cheap, but it's garbage."
   - Real-world relevance: A bar tab, a market and a group chat splitting the bill all run on prices and bucks, and getting the plural wrong makes the number sound wrong.

## [18] - `there/existential`, `there/negative-question`, `article/a-the-zero` · "there's", "over there", "around here" · "There's a f---in' bar on the corner, next to the one across from mine."

   - Focus: introduce things into the room with there is/are, then negate and question it, and pick the right article.
   - Deepens: `preposition/on-surface-and-days` — the place words now sit inside an existence sentence.
   - Uses: `word-order/skeleton`
   - Vocabulary: "in the middle of", "on the corner", "next to", "across from", "a few", "a lot", "no idea"
   - Examples: "Are there any f---in' seats? There isn't — try over there." · "A few people, a lot of noise, in the middle of nowhere." · "There's no f---in' beer around here, and I've got no idea."
   - Real-world relevance: Finding a table, a bathroom or a party is all there is/are plus a place phrase, so this is the exact language of arriving somewhere new.

## [19] - `possessive/adjective`, `possessive/s-genitive`, `possessive/mine-pronoun` · "my bad", "your call", "my ex" · "My bad — that's your f---in' call, it's their place, not ours."

   - Focus: cover the possessive adjectives, the 's/of split and the standalone possessive pronouns.
   - Deepens: —
   - Uses: `verb/be-identity`
   - Vocabulary: "my people", "a friend of mine", mine, yours, "ours", "their place", "his fault"
   - Examples: "A friend of mine hooked up with my ex — it's a whole f---in' mess." · "That's mine, this one is yours." · "My people are coming; it's his f---in' fault they're late."
   - Real-world relevance: Claiming, blaming and inviting all need possession (my bad, your call, their place), so this is the grammar of the group chat and the shared apartment.

## [20] - `adjective/position`, `adjective/order`, `intensifier/very-so-really`, `intensifier/swear-booster` · dead, mad, "f---in'" · "That was a f---in' good night, I'm dead serious."

   - Focus: place and order adjectives, then load them with intensity, including the taboo boosters.
   - Deepens: —
   - Uses: `verb/be-identity`
   - Vocabulary: hella, super, pretty, kinda, sorta, totally, such
   - Examples: "She's mad cool, hella funny, super chill, no shi-." · "It's kinda late, sorta cold — such a f---in' long day." · "That's a pretty big deal — so worth it, I'm dead."
   - Real-world relevance: Real opinions are almost never neutral, so a student who can't say "hella good", "dead tired" or "f---in' perfect" sounds like a textbook read aloud.

## [21] - `comparison/comparative-er`, `comparison/irregular`, `comparison/as-as`, `intensifier/much-way` · "way better", "way worse", "way too" · "This bar is way better — that one sucks."

   - Focus: build comparatives and superlatives, mark equality, then widen the gap with much/way/a bit.
   - Deepens: `intensifier/very-so-really` — those boosters now sit before a comparative to widen the gap.
   - Uses: `adjective/position`
   - Vocabulary: "a lot more", "just as", "not as", "the best", "the worst", suck, "blow away"
   - Examples: "The new job is way worse, a lot more f---in' work." · "It's just as loud, not as cheap — the worst deal." · "Their food blows mine away, way too f---in' good."
   - Real-world relevance: Ranking bars, jobs, exes and food is what friends do for fun, so comparisons have to come out fast and street-ready, not in a grammar table.

## [22] - `connector/time-order`, `connector/so-because`, `past/scene-then-event` · first, then, "after that" · "First we were at the bar, then all of a sudden it f---in' kicked off."

   - Focus: chain events with time connectors, give causes and results, and set a scene before dropping the event.
   - Deepens: `past-simple/irregular-set` — the past verb now chains events in order instead of naming one.
   - Uses: `past-simple/was-were`, `past/recounting-sequence`
   - Vocabulary: later, suddenly, "in the end", so, because, "all of a sudden", "long story short"
   - Examples: "We were broke, so we left, because nobody f---in' paid." · "Later we ate, after that we crashed, and in the end it was fine." · "Long story short: the shi- hit the fan."
   - Real-world relevance: Gossip, complaints and excuses all live or die on ordering and cause, so this is the first lesson where the student can actually hold the floor with a story.

## [23] - `sound/linking`, `sound/elision`, `sound/weak-forms`, `sound/assimilation` · whaddaya, dunno, gimme · "Whaddaya want? Gimme a f---in' sec, lemme see."

   - Focus: teach linking, elision, weak forms and assimilation so the student hears real fast speech, not dictionary English.
   - Deepens: `sound/vowel-pairs` — now whole phrases move their sounds, not just single vowels.
   - Uses: `sound/letter-vs-sound`
   - Vocabulary: lemme, betcha, coulda, shoulda, woulda, "c'mon", whatcha
   - Examples: "Dunno — coulda been worse, shoulda f---in' called." · "Betcha woulda stayed, c'mon, whatcha doing?" · "I dunno, gimme five minutes — what's the f---in' rush?"
   - Real-world relevance: Nobody says "what do you want" — they say "whaddaya want", so a student who only learned the full forms can't follow a fast friend or a song.

## [24] - `swear/core-verbs`, `swear/where-not-to-use`, `swear/masked-forms`, `slang/street-basics` · "f---", "shi-", "bullshi-" · "That party was lit — no bullshi-, it was a f---in' great night."

   - Focus: teach the core swears, mask them correctly, and give the honest where-you-can-say-it advice.
   - Deepens: `intensifier/swear-booster` — the swears are now the words themselves, with their weight and their no-go zones.
   - Uses: —
   - Vocabulary: "crap", "damn", lit, sketchy, flex, "low-key", "high-key"
   - Examples: "This bar is sketchy, low-key I'd rather leave, high-key." · "Shi-, I forgot my wallet — crap." · "He's flexing again, low-key annoying."
   - Real-world relevance: Friends swear constantly and music is soaked in it, so a student who freezes at f--- or misreads the masking in a subtitle is locked out of the very scenes this course is built on.

## [25] - EXAM — can the student hold a casual night out, from sound to swearing

   - Focus: a single running scene (a night out with friends) where each family is crossed by a task, not one label per question — describe the routine and the right-now in the same breath, then flip be and do questions on the fly, plan the next move with gonna and will, run a get family, place to/for/on/in/at correctly inside it, narrate what happened last week in the past, rank the bars, and finally drop one swear in the right place and one in the wrong place to prove they know the difference.
   - Uses: `present-simple/facts-not-now`, `present-continuous/now`, `question/do-support`, `future/gonna-mode`, `get/wildcard`, `preposition/to-vs-for`, `past-simple/irregular-set`, `past-simple/was-were`, `comparison/comparative-er`, `swear/where-not-to-use`

## [26] - `present-perfect/duration-for-since`, `present-perfect/ever-never`, `present-perfect/vs-past-simple`, `present-perfect/already-just-yet` · "for a while", "since day one", "so far" · "I've f---in' had it — five years in this job, since day one."

   - Focus: teach have/has + participle for an action that runs from the past to now, and kill the "trabalho aqui há 5 anos" trap.
   - Deepens: `past-simple/empty-mode-marker` — a new shape where a helper, not the verb, carries the link to now.
   - Uses: `past-simple/irregular-set`
   - Vocabulary: lately, ever, never, already, yet, just, "up to now"
   - Examples: "Have you ever f---ed up that bad? Never, not yet." · "I've already eaten — I just got home, no shi-." · "So far, up to now, I've had enough of this shi-."
   - Real-world relevance: This is the single biggest Portuguese-to-English correction there is, so it is drilled with real jobs, relationships and streaks a friend would actually ask about.

## [27] - `present-perfect/result-now`, `present-perfect/been-gone`, `present-perfect/pt-divide` · "mess up", "screw up", "blow" · "I've f---ed up big time — I'm screwed, I'm cooked."

   - Focus: use the perfect for a past action whose result is visible now, and separate have been from have gone.
   - Deepens: `present-perfect/duration-for-since` — from pure duration to the result that is staring you in the face.
   - Uses: `present-perfect/vs-past-simple`
   - Vocabulary: "screwed", "cooked", "done for", fix, "sort out", "make it", "figure out"
   - Examples: "She's f---in' blown it, we're done for." · "Have you figured it out yet? Sort it out." · "We've f---in' made it — it's over."
   - Real-world relevance: The excuse, the panic and the "I'm cooked" text all hinge on a result-now perfect, so this turns apology and disaster into fluent English.

## [28] - `past-continuous/scene`, `past-continuous/while-when`, `past-continuous/vs-simple`, `past/kept-doing` · "was about to", "right then", "at that point" · "I was about to leave when he blew up — he f---in' freaked out."

   - Focus: set a past scene with was/were + -ing, drop an interruption with when, and mark repeated past annoyance with kept + -ing.
   - Deepens: `past/scene-then-event` — the scene is now a full continuous tense, not just was/were.
   - Uses: `past-simple/irregular-set`, `past-simple/was-were`
   - Vocabulary: "keep doing", "blow up", "freak out", "lose it", "black out", "go off", "stand by"
   - Examples: "She kept doing it, and right then I f---in' lost it." · "At that point he blacked out, the guy f---in' went off." · "I was just standing by while all that shi- happened."
   - Real-world relevance: Every dramatic story ("I was just standing there when he went off") is scene-plus-interruption, so this is the tense of real gossip and real fights.

## [29] - `present-perfect/continuous`, `present-perfect/continuous-vs-simple`, `present-perfect/continuous-pt` · "been trying", "been meaning to", "been putting off" · "I've been trying to call you all f---in' day."

   - Focus: use have been + -ing for a recent ongoing activity and its visible result, and map "estou ... há" onto it.
   - Deepens: `present-perfect/duration-for-since` — from counting time to feeling the activity still running.
   - Uses: `present-perfect/result-now`, `present-continuous/now`
   - Vocabulary: "all day", "all week", "these days", "for ages", "at it", grind, hustle
   - Examples: "I've been meaning to sort it out, but I've been putting it off for f---in' ages." · "These days I've been grinding, hustling all week — I'm f---in' wrecked." · "I've been at it since morning, no shi-."
   - Real-world relevance: The apology, the excuse and the grind update ("I've been grinding all week") are all perfect continuous, so it carries both complaint and flex.

## [30] - `modal/can-could-ability`, `modal/be-able-to`, `modal/could-polite`, `modal/permission` · "be able to", "manage to", "get away with" · "Can you f---in' manage tonight? — Yeah, no problem."

   - Focus: teach can/could for ability and requests, be able to for the other tenses, and permission with tone advice.
   - Deepens: —
   - Uses: —
   - Vocabulary: let, allow, "go for it", "feel like", "up for", "down for", "no problem"
   - Examples: "Could you keep the music down? I'm trying to f---in' sleep." · "I'm up for it, I'm down for it — let's f---in' go for it." · "I'll be able to make it if you let me crash at your place."
   - Real-world relevance: Asking a favour, getting away with a rule and saying yes or no are everyday moves, and could versus can is exactly the politeness dial a stranger hears.
