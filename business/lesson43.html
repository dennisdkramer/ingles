One Tuesday

What this lesson installs: nothing new, and no section says today we study. It says 08:50. This is the integration day: one project, one client, one deadline that moves, and six rooms in nine hours — stand-up, client call, lunch, negotiation, a number that does not add up, and the written follow-up. Everything you need was taught in lessons 4 to 42; the skill being trained here is switching, which is what actually fails on a real Tuesday. Two rules run under the whole page. The first: information has to travel — the note you take at 08:50 is used at 11:00, the promise you make at 11:00 constrains the negotiation at 14:00, and the deal done at 14:00 is the content of the e-mail at 16:30. The second: same fact, different room — the sentence that is normal with a peer at lunch is not the sentence you use with the client, and the marker that shows the seam is a decision, not a mood.

11:00 · The client call

Nine turns. The client is calm, informed, and asking for one thing you cannot give — the old date. Listen ▶ first, then read the turn where the promise gets made: it is the sentence that will be quoted back at 14:00.

Client: "Thanks for making the time. So — we're still on for the fourteenth?"

You: "We're not, and I'd rather tell you that now than on the twenty-fifth. The date is the twenty-eighth."

Client: "Right. That's the second move this quarter."

You: "It is, and I'm not going to pretend otherwise. Two weeks, not six — and the only thing still open is a certificate."

Client: "What do you need from us?"

You: "Nothing today. What I'd like is a decision on Thursday: if the certificate lands by then, we hold the twenty-eighth. If it doesn't, I'd rather we talked about scope than about dates."

Client: "Fine. Send me that in writing today and I'll take it to our director on Thursday."

You: "I'll have it with you by five. And the two hundred beta users — we'll handle that comms ourselves, from tomorrow."

Client: "Good. That's the first thing today that's made my life easier."

Nove turnos: má notícia primeiro, número concreto, uma decisão pedida com data, uma promessa escrita até às cinco.

B. The same request, three rooms

One content — I need five minutes — at three settings. The marker is the part that changes; the request does not.

The settingWhat it sounds likeThe marker that shows the seam
a peer, at lunch"Hey — quick one." no verb of asking at all; the request is the tone
a colleague, mid-meeting"Do you have five minutes after this?" a full question, a time, and no urgency
a client, in writing"I'm writing to confirm the decision we discussed." a performative verb — writing to confirm is the act itself
Register is not politeness. All three are polite; they are different rooms. The mistake is using the lunch marker in the e-mail (reads as careless) or the e-mail marker at lunch (reads as a machine).
Handed forward → the phrase if the certificate lands by Thursday is now a condition, and conditions are what the 14:00 trade is made of. If you softened it on the call, you have nothing to trade this afternoon.

12:30 · Lunch, peer to peer

Same project, same facts, no client. This is the only room today where you are allowed to be tired. Listen ▶ to how a peer says the morning back to you, then look at the sidenote underneath — it contains one rule and no moral.

Peer: "So how bad is it?"
You: "Honestly? We're screwed if that certificate doesn't land by Thursday. The client's fine — that call went better than I expected — but the comms to the two hundred users still has nobody's name on it, and Priya's workaround only buys us a week."

Aqui o "we're screwed" é normal entre colegas — mesma pessoa, mesma sala errada, e a frase vira problema.

The sidenote, in its place: we're screwed, that's a mess, we're on the back foot are ordinary adult speech between peers — and they belong to one room. In the client call the same fact was we're in a difficult position. The rule is not "never swear"; it is same fact, different room, and the room is decided before the sentence starts, not after it lands.

14:00 · The trade

Same project, different company: this is the negotiation with your own delivery lead, not with the client. Two things are on the table — a week of Priya's time and the twenty-eighth. The moves below are lesson 47's (if you can…, we can…; confirm what was agreed) — here they are used, not taught.

Lead: "If the certificate lands Thursday, we hold the twenty-eighth. If it doesn't?"
You: "Then I'd rather move scope than the date. If I can have Priya for two days, we can ship without the import feature and add it in the first patch."
Lead: "Two days is a lot this week."
You: "It is. If I can have one day now and one after launch, we can still hold the date — and I'll write the patch note myself."
Lead: "Do that, and the import feature goes in the follow-up mail so nobody forgets it."
You: "Agreed — one day now, one after, import in the follow-up mail. I'll confirm it in writing tonight."

A troca: o que se move é o escopo, não a data; e o acordo só existe depois de dito em voz alta por outra pessoa.

16:30 · The follow-up

Three fields go in the mail, and nothing else: decisions (what was agreed), owners (whose name is on it), dates (by when). A summary with no owner and no date is a description, not a decision — and anything in it that was not agreed becomes next week's argument. The 4.30 mail is where the day is judged, because it is the only part of the day that survives.
