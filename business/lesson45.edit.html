BUSINESS LESSON 45

The Idiomatic Chunk Bank & Register Refresh

This lesson is a bank, not a new rule. You already control the grammar; what separates a fluent speaker from a merely correct one is the short, fixed expression a native reaches for without thinking. We collect them by the job they do, then refresh the register swap — moving one message between careful and casual English without changing what it says.

1. The chunk bank — learn it whole

A chunk is a group of words a native speaker produces as one piece. Learn it whole; don't build it word by word.

JobThe chunkWhat it does
Softening · heads-upJust a heads-up… a friendly warning before the news
Just so you know… a useful fact, up front
To be honest… softens a direct opinion
Checking you're clearDoes that make sense? checking you were clear
Are we on the same page? checking agreement
Am I making sense? a mid-explanation self-check
AgreeingSounds good. light agreement
Works for me. agreeing with a plan
Fair enough. accepting a reasonable point
Disagreeing softlyI hear you, but… acknowledge, then disagree
I'm not so sure. gentle doubt
I see it differently. direct but polite disagreement
Buying timeThat's a good question. a polite pause to think
Let me think for a second. buying a moment
Off the top of my head… from memory, roughly
Closing · follow-upI'll keep you posted. I'll keep you updated
Let's touch base later. let's check in later
We'll take it from here. we'll handle the rest

2. The register refresh — careful ↔ natural

One message, two registers. The careful form suits writing and first contact; the natural form is what you actually say to a colleague. The bold one is your production target.

Careful / writtenNatural / spoken
I regret to inform you Sorry to say
We are unable to We can't
Please find attached Here's the file
At your earliest convenience When you get a chance
I would like to inquire I just wanted to check
Thank you for your prompt response Thanks for getting back to me
In conclusion To wrap up
Should you have any questions Just let me know

3. Idioms that survive real speech

An idiom's meaning is not the sum of its words — "touch base" has nothing to do with baseball. These are grouped in families, so you learn the system, not a list.

Contact & staying in sync

on the same page — sharing the same understanding
in the loop — kept informed (out of the loop = left out)
touch base — check in briefly
keep you posted — keep you updated
get back to you — return with an answer

Decisions & status

up in the air — not yet decided
on the fence — undecided, neutral
a no-brainer — an easy, obvious decision
a win-win — good for both sides
back to the drawing board — start over

Effort & judgment

get the ball rolling — start things moving
hit the ground running — start well, immediately
cut corners — do it the cheap, quick way
call the shots — be in charge
read between the lines — infer the real meaning
