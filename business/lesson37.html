The Second Question

What this lesson installs: you can open a conversation with a stranger at a conference, keep it alive with the other person's own words, and leave it cleanly. The law is that the first question is the door and the second question is the conversation — nobody is after the weather; they are finding out, cheaply, whether you are easy and safe to talk to. The second law: depth is not a topic, it is a follow-up — you can go deep on the weather and stay shallow on work for an hour. One mapping to fix: Portuguese puxar assunto is not to pull a subject; it is to make conversation / to break the ice, and the thing you make is small talk (conversa fiada, mas aqui é trabalho sério), never small conversation. By the end you run the same four moves — open, follow, disclose, exit — at a coffee break, a lunch table and a reception.

2. The first question is the door

An icebreaker (a primeira frase que quebra o gelo) is not clever; it is low-stakes (de baixo risco — ninguém se compromete) and it hands the other person something easy to hold. Three that work, and what each one costs. Listen ▶.

OpenerWhat it invitesWhat it risks
How's the week going? a state, a complaint, a small story — anything about themnothing, which is why it is the safest door there is
Did you see the keynote this morning? an opinion, safely attached to a shared objecta yes/no answer if you do not follow up, and a lie if they skipped it
First time at this one? their history with the event, and the story of how they got thereit marks you as new; some people read that as junior
Vocabulary of the room: small talk (conversa de circunstância — literalmente, conversa pequena) · icebreaker (quebra-gelo) · rapport (sintonia, a sensação de que dá para conversar) · follow-up (a pergunta seguinte, feita em cima da resposta) · back-channel (o "hmm", "certo", "sério?" que mantém a pessoa falando) · echo (devolver uma palavra dela) · thread (o fio da conversa) · disclosure (contar algo seu, aos poucos) · authenticity (soar verdadeiro — vem do específico, não do adjetivo) · boundary (o limite do que não se pergunta) · cultural sensitivity (saber que o limite muda de país) · exit line (a frase de saída).

3. The second question is the conversation

The skill is not a better opener — it is what you do with the answer. Three recipes cover almost all of it: echo a word back, ask the how or the why, ask for the small story. Tick the follow-up that goes deeper, in each set below.

A. Which follow-up?

1."Busy week — we're migrating three systems at once."
2."I've just moved here from Curitiba."
3."We had a supplier pull out nine days before launch."
4."I'm here mostly for the workshops, not the talks."
5."Second year for me. Last year was smaller."
6."I'm learning to sail, badly."

4. Signals that keep the floor open

Silence after an answer reads as a verdict. The back-channel is what tells the other person to continue — it costs one word and buys a paragraph. Listen ▶ to the four, then judge the four lines below.

SignalWhat it doesWhere it fails
Right. / Yeah, no way. keeps the floor open without taking itsaid twice in a row, it starts to sound like a verdict
That makes sense. marks the answer as received, not judgedafter a complaint, it can sound like agreement with the complaint
Mm. + a nod the cheapest signal there is, and the most invisibleon the phone it is ambiguous — say a word instead
An echo: "Nine days?" signals you heard the detail and asks for more in one movea rising echo on every detail sounds like an interrogation
The rule is mechanical: signal → echo → follow-up. The signal holds the floor, the echo proves you were listening, and only then does the question land as interest rather than as a form.

A. Open the floor, or close it?

1."Mm. And then what happened?"
2."Right." (then silence, eyes on the phone)
3."Nine days? That's tight."
4."Yeah, no way. So how did you sell it internally?"

5. Disclose a little to get a little

Disclosure is a trade, and it is asymmetric on purpose: you offer first, small, and specific. Specificity is what authenticity sounds like — I'm learning to sail, badly beats I like sports. Make each general line specific.

A. From general to specific

1. I like sports. → I ____, badly, every Saturday morning.

um esporte específico, com um detalhe
2. I travel a lot. → I ____ three cities a month, mostly by train.

diga o número
3. I'm new here. → I ____ here in March and I still get lost downtown.

um fato com data
4. I work in marketing. → I ____ the e-mails nobody reads.

o detalhe que faz rir
One rule for the whole page: the detail you offer is low-stakes — it must be interesting and cost you nothing. A health problem, a salary or a divorce is not disclosure; it is a bill the other person has to pay.

6. Leave cleanly

The exit line needs three parts: the value (this was useful), the reason (I should let you get back to it) and the door (let's pick this up at the next one). Two out of three sounds like an escape.

A. Which exit is complete?

1.A talker has held you for ten minutes.
2.You must leave to catch a train.
3.You want to end a good conversation and continue it later.

B. The specific version

1. I have a lot of experience. → I ____ this team for four years.

verbo + número
2. We had a problem. → We ____, nine days before launch.

o fato específico
3. It went well. → We ____ and kept the account.

o resultado com número
4. I'm quite busy. → I'm ____ this week — three launches.

o motivo concreto

7. What travels, what does not

Small talk is being graded the whole time, and some doors are closed before you knock. The reliable no-go list on a first meeting — and the list is cultural, not personal.

Travels wellDoes not travelWhy
the journey, the city, the food, the conference itselfsalary, someone's health, age or weighteach one asks the other person to disclose something they cannot un-say
the work and how it is goingpolitics and religion, first meetingthe stakes are high and the exit is not obvious
a hobby, badly donecomplaints about the host or the eventit marks you as a risk to be seen with
what they are hoping to learn herefamily and personal questionsfriendly in Brazil, forward in Germany or the UK — cultural sensitivity is knowing which room you are in
There is no universal list, so use the test: would I be happy to answer this in front of their boss? If not, it is not a question, it is a risk.

1."How did you get into this line of work?"
2."Are you planning to have children?"
3."What's the part of the job you'd happily never do again?"
4."How much does your company pay for this?"
5.A Dutch colleague answers a personal question in three words. That means…
6.You cannot tell whether a topic is safe. The honest move is…

8. The eleven-line arc, with the moves removed

A reception conversation that worked. Every third line is a move you now know: open, follow, signal, disclose, exit. Four of them have been taken out — put them back from the list.

Ben: Long. We're moving two warehouses at once.

Ana: (1) ____

Ben: Badly. The systems went first and the stock is still here.

Ana: (2) ____ I did that in 2023 — 400 pallets in the wrong depot.

Ben: Really? How long did it take to sort out?

Ana: Eleven weeks. I'm not proud of the number. (3) ____

Ana: Then you want the workshop at four — it's the only practical one. (4) ____

Ben: Yes — and I'll be at the four o'clock.

The moves, if you need them: Badly how? · Mm, that's rough. I did that in 2023 — 400 pallets in the wrong depot. · What are you hoping to get out of this week? · I should let you get to it — but let's pick this up there. Only one fits each gap, and one of the four is a trap: a signal with no follow-up.
