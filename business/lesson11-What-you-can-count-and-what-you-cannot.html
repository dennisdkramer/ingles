What you can count and what you cannot

Two kinds of noun, and one test that separates them

Duas espécies de substantivo: o contável (a file, two files) e a massa (water, money). Um teste separa: dá para dizer "um … dois …"? Se sim, é contável; se não, é massa.

One question decides the group

Every English noun sits in one of two groups, and the group decides which quantity words may stand in front of it: a, two, many for the first group, some, much, a little for the second. A countable noun names something you can put a number in front of: one report, two reports, three calls. An uncountable noun names a mass, a material or an idea: information, money, water, music. You can always have more of a mass. You never have two of it.

The test is one question: can I say "two ___s"? If the plural sounds wrong, the noun is uncountable. Two informations is wrong. Two reports is right.

The answer changes the words around the noun. Countable: a or an, a plural -s, and the question how many. Uncountable: no a, no plural, and the question how much. How much time do we have? asks about hours. How many times? is a different question — it counts occasions, not hours.

Six words from your inbox — countable or uncountable?

Run the test on the word, not on the sentence. The word report takes two, so it counts. The word feedback never takes two, however many people send it.

Two of the six are countable. The other four are the ones that cost people the most, because Portuguese counts every one of them.

To count a mass, you count its container

Para contar uma massa, conta-se o recipiente ou a unidade: a glass of water, a piece of news, two slices of bread. A contagem sai da massa e passa para o recipiente.

The count moves onto the container

If a noun refuses a number, English counts something around it instead. A piece of information, a piece of advice, an item of news, a bottle of water, a loaf of bread, a cup of coffee. The mass stays singular after of. The container takes the number: two pieces of information, three cups of coffee.

This is the repair for every uncountable word on this page. You do not need a new word for the idea — you need the container the situation already has.

This is where Portuguese leads you into the error. Informações, conselhos e notícias são plurais normais em português, so the -s travels across on its own: informations, advices, news with a plural verb. English has no plural for the mass — the plural belongs to the container.

is the workhorse: one unit of something you cannot count. Not the piece of a machine.

a piece ofone unit of a mass — a piece of information is one item of it, a piece of advice is one suggestion, never uma peça.

Nothing, some, or the — what a mass takes instead of "a"

A massa não leva a. Ela leva nada (water), the (the water in the bottle) ou some (some water). Três escolhas, e quem decide é o ouvinte: ele sabe de qual água você fala?

Three choices, and the listener decides

A mass never takes a. It takes one of three things, and the choice is about how much the listener already knows. Nothing at all for the material in general: Coffee is expensive this year. Some for an amount nobody is measuring: We need coffee for the meeting. The for the specific mass both people mean: The coffee in the kitchen is cold.

These same three words stand in front of a plural countable noun — Reports are useful, We need reports, The reports from Monday are here. A singular countable noun cannot do this: it must carry a or the.

Pick the word each line needs

One choice per line: — for nothing, some for an unmeasured amount, the for the mass both speakers already know.

A small trap: some and the are also correct in front of plural countable nouns. The test is still the first question on the page — can I say two___s?

How much, how many, and the small amount

how much pergunta por massa (How much time?), how many por coisa contável (How many files?). E a quantidade pequena: a little para massa, a few para contável.

Two questions, four quantity words

The question picks the noun group: how much asks about a mass, how many asks about items. The quantity words follow the same split — much with a mass, many with items — with one habit to learn: in a positive sentence English prefers a lot of. We have a lot of time is normal speech; We have much time is grammatical and sounds like a written report.

A little is a small amount of a mass; a few is a small number of items. The a is the whole difference: a little time is some, enough to work with; little time is almost none, a problem.

Some goes in statements and in offers: We need some information, Do you want some coffee? Any goes in questions and negatives: Do we have any feedback?, We don't have any time.

is the phrase to keep in front of countable items, and it is safe in positive sentences where not many would sound negative.

a fewa small number — three or four, and enough to work with: a few issues, a few minutes. Without the a, few issues means almost none.

Four words that look countable and are not

News ends in -s and looks plural. It is one mass of information, so the verb is singular: the news is, never the news are. That single letter is the sentence that reveals where a speaker comes from.

The news is good.

Money and time are masses even though the student counts both all day. The count is not on the mass, it is on the unit: two dollars, three hours, five minutes.

Time has a second life as a countable noun: a time, two times — an occasion. So how much time counts hours and how many times counts occasions. The letters are the same; the noun is not.

Three more from the meeting room. Research is a mass like information: some research, a piece of research. In American business writing data behaves the same way — the data is clear. Staff names the group as one thing: the staff is ready.

Three decisions, and the sentence is yours

Três decisões antes de falar: a coisa se conta? então leva a e plural em -s. É massa? então não leva a e não tem plural. E qual quantidade: much, many, a little, a few.

What you do before you speak

Every sentence on this page was built with the same three decisions. One: can I say two ___s? That fixes the group. Two: does the listener already know which mass I mean — nothing, some, or the? Three: how much or how many, and do I need a container to count it?

What you can now produce without translating the pattern: I need some information about the new client. We don't have much time. There are a few issues. Two pieces of advice. The news is good. Each one is a sentence you will say this week, not an exercise.
