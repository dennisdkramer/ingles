The Sounds & the Key

What this lesson installs: the whole sound inventory of English — vowels, consonants, and the key that turns spelling into sound, so you never need a person to tell you how a word is said. You will hear the pairs that betray Brazilians (ship / sheep, three / tree, hat / hot, west / vest), fix the consonants Portuguese never trained (TH, R, H, NG, dark L, flap T), and read the key: syllables, the strong beat, the sleeping vowel ə, and the difference between the careful and the natural version of a word. Uma lei vale para tudo: uma sílaba forte por palavra, o resto relaxado — one strong syllable per word, everything else relaxed. This is a double lesson: ears first, then your own words, then the key that makes you independent.

2. The vowel map

Five vowel letters, and not one of them owns a single sound. Each letter says its name in some words and something else in others — the difference is usually a silent e at the end, or a second vowel beside it. Listen ▶ before the table: one letter, four sounds.

LetterSays its NAME in…Says something else in…
Acake · name · rain (a + silent e, or ai)cat · car · about
Ehe · see · meet (final e, or ee)pet · the
Itime · five · my (i + silent e, or y)sit · machine
Ogo · home · road (o + silent e, or oa)hot · who · love
Ucute · use (u + silent e)cut · put · rule
The sleeping vowel is the fourth sound of every letter: when a syllable is weak, the vowel collapses to ə — about, problem, support, computer. It has no letter of its own; it is what a vowel becomes when it is not the strong beat, and it is the most common sound in English.

C and G also decide by the letter after them — the same "it depends on what follows" logic that runs the whole lesson:

C
city · cinema · cycle → som de s
cat · cold · cut → som de k
antes de e, i, y = s · resto = k
G
general · giant · gym → som de dj
go · green · bag → som de g
antes de e, i, y = dj · resto = g

4. The key — syllables, the strong beat, the sleeping vowel

The key is the site's own pronunciation notation, and it is the only spelling system you should trust: no improvised "respellings" that work for one word and lie for the next. Four marks, and you can read any entry.

MarkWhat it tells youExample
·a syllable break — one beat eachta·bl = two beats
grey letterswritten but almost not said — the letter that disappearscən·si·dər
əthe sleeping vowel — the weak, relaxed vowel of an unstressed syllablea·bout · prob·ləm
CAPITALS (on this page)the one strong syllable; everything else is relaxedpho·TO·gra·phy
The key does not invent sounds: it shows you where the beats are, which letters vanish, and which vowel fell asleep. That is enough to say a word you have never seen — and it is the same information a native uses without knowing it.

Stress is the engine of the key. One syllable is strong, the rest collapse to ə — and in English the beat can be the only difference between two words. Listen ▶ to the same letters with the beat in two places:

Noun (beat first)Verb (beat second)
REcord (o registro)reCORD (gravar)
PREsent (o presente)preSENT (apresentar)
PROject (o projeto)proJECT (projetar)
A regra que você já tem da aula 2 continua valendo, agora dentro da chave: -tion, -sion, -ity, -ic empurram a força para a sílaba anterior (in-for-MA-tion, u-ni-VER-si-ty). O que muda aqui é que você agora sabe marcar isso — e ler a marca.

And at speed, the small words lose their vowel too. This is what the key sounds like in a real sentence:

WrittenSaid (with the sleeping vowel)
a cup of coffeeə cup ə coffee
the price of the productthə price ə thə product
to me, for youtə me, fər you
a, the, of, to, and, for are the six words that shrink most — they are never the strong beat, so their vowel sleeps. Falar essas palavras "cheias" é o segundo maior marcador de sotaque, depois do th.
