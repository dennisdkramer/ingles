The trade

What this lesson installs: the one sentence the whole skill lives in — if you can take delivery in March, we can hold the price — a condition attached to every concession, in the same breath, never a free gift. Around it sit five abilities. Negotiate interests, not positions: find out why the number matters before you argue about the number. Name your BATNA, because the walk-away is where the power is and a negotiator without one is negotiating from hope. Anchor with a reason — an anchor with no justification is just a wish. Concede in shrinking steps across dimensions, never straight down the price. And when it stalls, agree the process — who decides, and when — instead of re-arguing the content.

1. Read — the same first exchange, twice

Same supplier, same number, two different rooms. Read both, then answer the only question that matters: what exactly did the second question buy?

Room A
Supplier: "We can't go above 90."
You: "That's nowhere near where we need to be."
Supplier: "It is what it is."

Room B
Supplier: "We can't go above 90."
You: "What would you do with the extra ten?"
Supplier: "Honestly? It would fund the extra shift in March. Without it we can't take on the volume you're asking for."

A segunda pergunta não foi mais educada. Foi a única que produziu informação.

A. Diagnose the exchange

1."We can't go above 90" is…
2."It would fund the extra shift in March" is…
3.What did the second question buy?
4.Room A ends because…
5.The move in Room B is…
6."It is what it is" is…
Positions are the last thing the other side will tell you — and the first thing you will hear, because a position is easy to say and safe. We need 10% off is a position. Our budget is fixed and our board meets Friday is an interest. Only the second one can be traded with.

2. Position, interest, and the move between them

Every row below is something you will actually hear in a renewal meeting. The third column is the question that turns the first into the second — and it is always cheaper than a counter-offer.

What is saidWhat it hidesThe move that answers it
"We can't go above 90."a cost structure, or a margin someone has to defend"What would the extra ten pay for?"
"We need an answer today."their own deadline, probably internal"What happens to you if it slips to Thursday?"
"Everyone gets the same price."a policy, or a fear of setting a precedent"Who would have to approve an exception, and on what grounds?"
"The volume is what it is."a forecast they do not control"Is it the volume or the certainty of the volume that matters?"
"We'd rather not change the warranty."a risk they are carrying somewhere else"What would make the warranty conversation easy?"
"Let's leave it there for today."a decision they cannot make alone"Who else needs to see this, and when do they meet?"
The pattern in the third column: never argue inside the frame, ask what the frame is protecting. A counter-offer closes the topic; a question opens a dimension — and dimensions are where deals are actually made.

3. Your BATNA, in four lines

Your BATNA — best alternative to a negotiated agreement — is your only real power. If you do not know what you will do when this fails, every concession you make is anxiety made audible. Fill it in before the meeting; four lines, honest ones.

The lineYours
mine — what I actually do if we do not agree
theirs — what they probably do if we do not agree
the cost of no deal — to me, in money and in time
my first move — the anchor and the reason for it
Two tests. A BATNA you would not actually take is not a BATNA — it is a story you tell yourself before conceding. And the cost of no deal is a number: if you cannot put one on it, you will discover it in the meeting, at the worst possible moment.

Refuse the frame, do not argue inside it. Your competitor is cheaper invites you to defend your price; the frame-refusal is "cheaper for the same scope, or cheaper for a smaller one?" — which is answerable, and which moves the conversation onto scope, where you have room.

5. The shape of a concession

How you concede is information, whether you mean it or not. The other side reads the sequence, not the sentences.

The sequenceWhat it says to the other sideThe move that answers it
10 → 4 → 1shrinking steps: we are nearly at the floorhold — or ask for the last centimetre in another dimension
10 → 10 → 10equal steps: there is more where that came fromdo not stop asking; make the next step conditional
10 → 0, one big jump then silencethat was an opening position, not a limittreat the silence as a floor and test it once, cheaply
a concession with no conditionthis is free — ask againnever be the first to do this; it teaches the room that you are a donor
a concession with a conditioneverything here has a price, and the price is tradeablethe only shape that survives a second meeting
The three shapes on the left are not tricks and they are not lies — they are the natural traces of how hard somebody is pushing. You are being read whether or not you are performing, which is why the conditional is not politeness: it is the only way to make your concessions mean what you want them to mean.

6. Said — the conditional offer at speed

This is the sentence the whole lesson lives in, and it is worthless if it arrives late: if you can…, we can… — condition first, concession second, in the same breath. Eight situations. Say each one aloud before you write it.

The situationYour conditionYour concession
They want the price held, and you need volume.
They want delivery in March, and it costs you a shift.
They want 60-day payment terms.
They want the warranty extended to three years.
They want a named engineer on site.
They want out of the contract in twelve months.
They want a discount for a reference case study.
They want the audit findings early.
The two amateur errors, named: two concessions in one sentence with one condition ("we can hold the price and add the warranty if you commit to volume") and the condition that arrives after the concession ("we can do 78 — if you can give us three years"). The first gives away the second thing for free; the second is not a trade, it is a discount with a hope attached.

Select this line and record ⏺ all eight, one breath each, condition first. If a condition arrives after the concession, do the item again — the order is the skill, not the vocabulary.

7. The trade matrix

Price is the dimension both sides defend, so the deal is found in the others. This is the intellectual work of the lesson: buy in one row and sell in another. Fill the four crossed moves — each one gives something cheap to you and takes something valuable.

The dimensionWhat we wantWhat they wantThe crossed move
priceflat — 1.02 on last year+12%
volumeoption to flex down 20%a committed baseline
datedelivery in AprilMarch, because of their shift
payment60 days30 days, or a deposit
warrantytwo years as standardthree years
scopethe same service, less trainingtraining included, on site
The matrix only works if the two cheap rows are on your side: a longer term, a case study, a payment date you can afford, an April delivery — those cost you little and are worth a lot across the table. Trade what is cheap for you and expensive for them, and never trade two rows to buy one.

A. The package, and the test

8. Close — one summary, one e-mail

A deal is what was said back by both sides and then written down within a day. The closing summary is four lines; the e-mail is the same four lines in the register from lesson 45.

The closing lineYours
"So, to confirm what we've agreed…" — the package in one sentence
"What each side does, and by when" — two rows, names and dates
"What is still open" — the honest line, named
"What happens if it slips" — the date that triggers a review
The sentence that closes without closing the door: "If anything above is not as you understood it, tell me today and we'll fix it before I send it on." It gives the other side one last chance to disagree privately — which is exactly the face-saving move from lesson 46, applied to a commercial agreement.

A. Record ⏺ — the close, out loud

Select this line and record ⏺ the closing summary in sixty seconds: the package, the two commitments with dates, what is still open, and the trigger date. Then say the confirmation e-mail as if you were reading it into a phone — one register, no hedging.
