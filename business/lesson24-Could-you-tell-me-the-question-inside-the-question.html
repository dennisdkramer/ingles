Could you tell me: the question inside the question

Inside a question frame, the question word comes first and the verb follows

A direct question moves the mode in front of the subject: Where is the office? Put a frame in front of it — Could you tell me … — and the question inside stops moving: Could you tell me where the office is?

Two things happen at once, and they happen in one sentence. The frame is still a question, so it keeps its own inversion at the front. The question inside is no longer a question, so it takes the order of a statement: subject first, verb second.

É uma inversão só, e ela está na frente. Depois de where, when, what, volte à ordem normal: where the office is, nunca where is the office.

Do you know where she works?

Could you tell me when the report is ready?

Read the marks left to right: the mode opens the frame (Do, Could), and after the question word the subject comes first and the verb second — she works, the report is.

Twelve openers, and what each one does to the sentence

The frame is not decoration: each opener lowers the pressure a different way. Could you tell me and Do you know are the two that take a question inside. I was wondering takes if for a yes-or-no question, and no question mark at all. Just to check and quick question warn the other person that something is coming.

Three of them are worth a card, because their Portuguese is close and their grammar is not: , , .

How come significa por que, mas não inverte: how come the file is late? — sem is the file. É a exceção da lição.

how comewhy — and the clause after it keeps statement order: How come the file is late? Never how come is the file late.
if you don't minda polite wrapper for a request, put at the end or the front — Could I see the draft, if you don't mind?
let me knowtell me later, when you know — Let me know by Friday. The thing to be told is never named.
  • could you tell me — the standard frame for a wh-question.
  • do you know — the same frame, slightly more direct.
  • I was wondering — the softest, and it takes if.
  • can I ask — asks permission and the question at once.
  • just to check — the frame for confirming something you half know.
  • quick question — warns that a question is coming.
  • one more thing — adds a question to the end of a list.
  • which one — asks a choice between named things.
  • when exactly — asks for the precise moment, not the day.
