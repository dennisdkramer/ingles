The little verb that carries the tense: don't and doesn't

Every sentence has a slot between who and what happens

The slot sits empty until the sentence needs help

An English sentence is who → a slot → the main verb → the rest. In the affirmative simple present the slot is empty, and the main verb carries everything: I work, she sends.

The moment the sentence has to say no, the slot fills with a small verb — do or does — plus not. That small verb takes the person and the tense, and the main verb leaves them behind: She doesn't send the invoice.

Portuguese says no with one word, não, and the verb keeps its ending: ela não envia. English gives the no its own verb and takes the ending off the main one. That is the whole difference, and it is the sentence a Brazilian most often gets half right.

Two empty slots, two filled ones. Read the third and fourth rows against the second: the verb after the slot has no ending.

She doesn't send the invoice.

I don't have the file yet.

The mode mark lands on does and do, and the verb mark lands on a bare send and have. Nothing carries the -s except the little verb.

That is the shape to look at twice: doesn't send, never doesn't sends. The -s of the third person has moved from the main verb onto the small one, and it lives there now.

One -s per clause. Two of them is the signature error of a Portuguese speaker, and it is easy to hear because the little verb is the one the ear is listening for.

Twelve words, and the two that carry the no

The two words this lesson is built on

don't — não, with I, you, we, they: We don't use that system.

doesn't — não, with he, she, it: She doesn't send the invoice.

Both are two words spoken as one: do not → don't, does not → doesn't. In writing, the apostrophe stands where the o of not was.

Choose by the subject, exactly as you choose the -s: the same three persons that take -s take doesn't.

Ten verbs that stand after them with no ending

have(hav) — ter · I don't have the file yet.

need(need) — precisar · They don't need help.

want(want) — querer · I don't want two options.

like(like) — gostar de · He doesn't like the new system.

work(work) — trabalhar · She doesn't work on Friday.

Five more, and do itself

live(live) — morar · They don't live here.

eat(eat) — comer · He doesn't eat breakfast.

drink(drink) — beber · I don't drink coffee at night.

help(help) — ajudar · He doesn't help with the reports.

do(do) — fazer · I do the report every Monday.

Do is the one word on this page with two jobs. As the little verb in the slot it means nothing at all — it is there to carry the no. As a main verb it means fazer, and then it can be negated like any other: I don't do the report on Friday.

That sentence has do twice, and both are correct. The first one is the slot; the second is the work.

Fill the slot, and the main verb loses its ending

The -s moves to the little verb

The affirmative has the person on the main verb: she sends. The negative cannot have it there, because the little verb is now carrying the person: she doesn't send.

So the rule has two halves, and both must be done at once:

  • Fill the slot with does or do + not.
  • Take the ending off the main verb. It goes back to the form in the dictionary: send, have, use, answer.

Do half of it and the sentence is wrong in a way every ear notices: she doesn't sends carries the -s twice, and she don't sends carries it in the wrong place and twice. One clause, one -s.

The negative sits on the same line the affirmative did: it is the same fact, said the other way. Nothing about the time changes — only the slot.

Read the five back with the verb stressed: doesn't drink, don't need, don't live. The stress falls on the main verb, which is why it has to be a clean, single word — the little verb in front already said who and when.

And keep the third person straight: she, he, it take doesn't; I, you, we, they take don't. The next section makes that choice mechanical.

don't or doesn't: the subject decides, and only the subject

The same three persons, the same letter

The rule is the one from lesson 4, read from the other side. The persons that put -s on the verb are exactly the persons that take doesn't; everyone else takes don't.

Two habits to drop

Habit one: putting the -s on both verbs — doesn't sends. The -s is already in does; the main verb is base.

Habit two: using don't for every person, because não is one word in Portuguese. English has two, and the choice is made by the subject, not by the verb: he don't is wrong even when the verb feels singular.

A name or a thing takes the same decision as he, she or it: Ana doesn't answer on Sunday. · The system doesn't work on Monday.

do and have are also real verbs, and be never takes do

When do means fazer and have means ter

The little verb and the main verb can be the same word, and the sentence stays correct: I do the report every Monday. · I don't do the report on Friday. The first do in the second sentence is the slot; the second is the work.

Have is the same story. In I have the file it is a main verb, so its negative fills the slot: I don't have the file yet. Yet is ainda — the word that turns a no into a not-yet.

In this office the negative of have is don't have. The short haven't belongs to another variety of English and is not what a colleague in the United States will say to you.

be is the one verb that says no by itself

Be never takes do. It fills its own slot with not: I'm not at the office. · She isn't here. · They aren't ready.

So every verb in this lesson takes don't or doesn't, and be takes neither. That is the one exception, and it is worth saying out loud once: she don't be at the office is not a sentence; she isn't at the office is.

The test is quick — if the affirmative uses am, is or are, the negative uses the same verb plus not. If the affirmative uses any other verb, the negative needs the slot.

One word is wrong

Write each sentence again, correctly.

2. He don't answer on Sunday.

3. We not use that system.

4. I no have the file yet.

5. She don't be at the office.

Each of the five is a sentence a Brazilian says with the right words in the wrong machine. The repairs:

  • the -s belongs to does, so send is base;
  • he needs doesn't, not don't;
  • not cannot do the work alone — it needs its verb;
  • no is not the negative of a verb;
  • be never takes do.

Saying no at work: four refusals, one machine

Complete the exchange

Ana is asking Bruno about a file. Write the missing little verb in each turn.

  1. Ana1.I have the file yet. The client sent it on Friday.
  2. Bruno2.That system is old. We use it now.
  3. Ana3.And Lia? She answer on Sunday.
  4. Bruno4.Right — she work on the weekend.

Four negatives, two subjects: I and we take don't, she takes doesn't, and every main verb after them is clean — have, use, answer, work.

This is how a professional says no without a fight: the negative stays on the fact, and yet keeps the door open. I don't have the file yet is a no that also gives the client another week; I don't have the file is the same fact with the door shut.
