Verb + preposition: the preposition each verb takes

The preposition is part of the verb, not a choice

A preposição é parte do verbo, não uma escolha: look for = procurar, look after = cuidar, look at = olhar. Trocar a preposição troca o verbo.

One verb, three different verbs

English does not let you choose a preposition by meaning. The verb and the preposition are one word in the speaker's head, and the pair is what carries the meaning. look for is not look plus a direction — it is a different verb from look at, and a third one from look after.

That is why a wrong preposition is not a small mistake. Depend of is not a version of depend on; it is a word English does not have. There is no rule to reason your way to here — there is a list, learned pair by pair, and this page is that list.

1. We are ___ the numbers before the meeting.

2. I am ___ the invoice — it is not in the folder.

3. She ___ the two new analysts.

4. Please ___ the recording tonight.

Five pairs that survive for years

The twelve a working week actually uses

As doze que uma semana de trabalho usa de verdade, cada uma com o que faz. Aprende-se o par inteiro — look for, look at, look after — nunca o verbo sozinho.

The list, and what each pair does

Six of the twelve are one verb with one preposition and nothing else. The other six carry a particle that works with the verb the same way — and the next lesson takes that family apart.

Read the list as twelve words, not as a verb plus a decoration. Deal with is one entry; deal alone is not the word you use.

Where the object goes, and what a question does to the pair

O par fica junto, e o objeto vem depois dele: look for the file, nunca look the file for. Na pergunta, o par continua colado no fim: What are you looking for?

The pair stays together

The pair takes its object after it: look after the team, deal with the ticket, wait for the client. The object never sits between the verb and its preposition — English has no look the invoice for, because the two words are one word.

A pronoun keeps its object form after the preposition: wait for him, listen to them, talk about us. Wait for he is the same mistake as for he anywhere else.

In a question the object moves to the front and the preposition stays behind: What are you looking for? Who are you waiting for? That is the normal spoken form, and it is what a meeting uses.

The other form exists — For what are you looking?, For whom are you waiting? — and it is formal: a contract, a speech, a very careful letter. In speech it sounds like a costume. Learn to hear it; do not use it in the room.
