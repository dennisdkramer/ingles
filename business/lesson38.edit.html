The Disagreement Ladder

What this lesson installs: you can disagree on purpose — at the rung the stakes deserve, with the person intact — and you can hear when a colleague disagrees badly. The law is that disagreement is a dial, not a switch: six rungs from I completely agree to I don't think that holds, each one stating exactly how much of the other person's claim survives. The second law: hedge the belief, never the facts — soft on the verb (I'm not convinced), sharp on the content (the timeline is three weeks short). One mapping to fix on the way in: Portuguese discordo em parte is not I disagree partially; it is I agree with most of that, but / I see your point, but — and até certo ponto is up to a point, an idiom, never until a certain point. By the end of the page you run the same four moves — place, concede, evidence, offer — in a design review.

1. The same line twice

A design review. One person, one true thing, said twice — once as an attack and once as a contribution. Listen ▶ to both, and watch what the room does. Nothing is defined yet.

A. Version one

"That's never going to work — people don't read emails."

The room: a sigh. Nobody answers. The slide stays up for another two minutes, and the decision slips past the point where it could still be changed.

What they heard: you are the problem.

B. Version two

"I see your point about reach. The thing I'd worry about is whether people read it."

The room: an answer. Two people talk at once about open rates, the decision is deferred by a week, and the same true thing enters the record.

What they heard: the claim is open to discussion.

A. What changed?

1.Both versions say the same thing about…
2.Version one attacks…
3.The words "I see your point about reach" do what?
4.The room reacts to the person, not the argument. The lesson from that is…

2. Six rungs, not two settings

Most people own two settings: completely agree and open war. The professional skill is the space between them — and each rung says exactly how much of the other person's claim survives. Listen ▶ down the ladder, from the softest to the hardest.

RungThe phraseWhat it commits you toThe stakes that earn it
1I completely agree. the whole claim survives, and your name is on ityou have read the detail and it is right
2I agree with most of that. the direction survives; one part is opena good proposal with one flaw you can name
3I see your point, but… the point is recorded; the conclusion does not followthe cost of being wrong is a week, not a quarter
4I'm not sure that's the whole picture. the claim is incomplete; you owe the missing partyou have a fact they do not have
5I'm not convinced. the burden is now on them, softlythe decision is expensive and reversible only in theory
6I don't think that holds. the claim fails on its own terms — and you will say howthe room is about to commit to something that cannot work
Vocabulary of the ladder: rung (degrau) · to concede (reconhecer, ceder terreno) · a concession (a parte que você reconhece) · to push back (resistir, discordar sem brigar) · hedge (suavizar a certeza) · to qualify (acrescentar uma ressalva) · to soften (amolecer o tom) · stakes (o que está em jogo) · the burden of proof (o ônus da prova) · to hold (aqui: se sustentar, ser válido) · up to a point (até certo ponto) · what would change your mind (o que te faria mudar de ideia).
The rung is chosen by the stakes, not by your mood. A rung too high loses the room and gets you nothing; a rung too low loses the point, and the meeting decides something you disagree with while you say I see your point. You cannot fight every rung — deciding which one is worth fighting is the skill.

A. Place each disagreement on the ladder

1."The logo should be blue. Blue." (you know the brand rules: it is blue)
2."We should cut the training budget to pay for the redesign." (you have a cost figure they do not)
3."Six weeks is enough." (your estimate is nine, and the client's contract is at stake)
4."Let's call the release 'Sunrise'." (you mildly prefer something else)
5."The whole model works because users will pay monthly." (that premise is what the business cannot do)
6."We should launch in April." (April is right; the week is wrong)

4. Concede first — and never with but

The word but cancels everything in front of it: I take your point about cost, but the timeline is impossible is heard as one sentence, and it is the second half. Two sentences instead, and the concession is real because it is not being withdrawn in the same breath.

A. Take the but out

1. I take your point about cost, but the timeline is impossible. →

duas frases, sem but
2. That's true, but we don't have the people. →

a concessão sobrevive
3. Fair enough, but the client won't pay for it. →

concede, depois vira
4. I agree in principle, but not with this date. →

o que fica, o que cai

B. Hedge the belief, not the facts

1. The timeline is impossible. → I ____ the timeline is workable.

suave na crença
2. Your numbers are wrong. → I ____ those numbers add up.

duro no fato
3. This will fail. → I ____ this gets us there by June.

certeza, não conteúdo
4. You haven't tested it. → I ____ it's been tested with users.

pergunta como hedge
The test for a hedge: remove the hedge and the sentence should still be worth saying. I'm not sure the timeline is workable → the timeline is not workable. If the hedge is on the facts instead — it's maybe possibly a bit of an issue — nothing survives the removal, and the room files you under unprepared.

5. Evidence is the neutral ground

What's that based on? moves the fight from people to data in five words, and it is the only question on this page that nobody can take personally. Offer your own evidence the same way and it looks generous instead of defensive. Listen ▶ to the four questions and two offers.

MoveWhat it sounds likeWhy it works
ask, flatly"What's that based on?" it asks for a source, not a surrender
ask for the number"Where does the eleven per cent come from?" a number with no source is a mood with a decimal point
ask for the comparison"Compared to what, though?" it makes the baseline part of the claim
ask for the risk"What's the worst case if we're wrong?" it turns the disagreement into a shared calculation
offer your own"I'll send you the Q3 sheet — then you'll see what worries me." you go first, so the request is not an accusation
offer a condition"If we had the churn number I'd know how worried to be." it names exactly what would settle it — and it is not an opinion

A. Ask for it, or offer it?

1."What's the pilot data look like so far?"
2."I'll put the cohort sheet in the channel after this."
3."Compared to last quarter, or to the target?"
4."I can show you the two clients who asked for it."

6. Attack the idea, protect the person

Every line below is true. Only some of them can be said in the room. The check is the third-person test: would I say this if their boss were listening — and would I say it to their face exactly as I say it behind their back? Two yeses, say it. Anything else, rewrite it.

A. Rewrite it so the person survives

1. "You clearly haven't thought this through." →

o problema é o plano, não a pessoa
2. "Marketing always does this." →

tire o "sempre" e o grupo
3. "That's a terrible idea." →

diga o que não funciona e por quê
4. "Whoever wrote this didn't read the brief." →

o documento, não o autor
Why it matters commercially: the person you flatten today is the person whose sign-off you need in six weeks. Disagreement that costs you the relationship is a bad trade even when you are right — especially when you are right, because then you have proved your point and lost the vote.

7. What would change your mind?

A position with no possible counter-evidence is not a position, it is a mood — and rooms learn to ignore it. Say the condition out loud and your disagreement becomes credible instead of stubborn. Ask it of yourself first.

A. Name the condition

1. I'd drop my objection if ____

um fato verificável
2. I'd support the earlier date if ____

um número ou um teste
3. What would ____ for you?

devolva a pergunta
4. So we agree on the test: if ____, we go.

feche com um critério
Two lines of your own: write one I'd drop my objection if… and one What would change your mind? about a real decision at your work, then say both aloud . A disagreement with a condition attached is a proposal; a disagreement without one is a complaint.
