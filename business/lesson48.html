Leading in English

What this lesson installs: the language of the person who has to make other people's work possible. A brief is five parts or it is a wish — outcome, why, when, the limits, and who decides what — and when one is missing the questions come back later as mistakes. Delegation without authority is a task with a hostage: hand over the decision as explicitly as the work. Feedback lives in situation–behaviour–impact, with no adjectives about the person anywhere, because you are careless is an identity and identities get defended instead of changed. Coaching is questions, mentoring is advice, managing is the decision — and confusing the three is why so many one-to-ones are status reports. Change is announced in four lines, and the fourth one is the credibility: what I do not know yet, and when I will tell you.

1. Heard — the brief with a hole in it

Twenty-five seconds of a handover. Listen ▶, then count what was missing — and notice what the silence afterwards is doing.

Manager: "Right — the supplier thing. Just get it done, you know what I mean. Make it good. Any questions?"
[ silence — two full seconds ]

O silêncio não é concordância. É a pessoa a tentar adivinhar o que acabou de aceitar.

A. What was missing?

1.The most expensive thing missing was…
2."Make it good" supplies…
3."Any questions?" produces silence because…
4."You know what I mean" is…
5.The cost of this brief arrives…
6.The leader's most expensive sentence is…
Every missing part comes back as a mistake. Not because people are careless, but because a human being handed a hole fills it with their own reasonable assumption — and their reasonable assumption is not yours. The five parts exist to make the assumption impossible.

3. Delegation and the four questions

A. Delegation or a hostage?

Four handovers. Write delegation or hostage, then the missing line.

1. "You own the supplier decision — run it, tell me when it's signed."
2. "Do the analysis and check with me before any call."
3. "Make the call, but cc me on everything."
4. "You decide up to 1.08; above that it comes to me."

B. Four coaching questions

A team member says: "I don't think I can deliver the audit by Friday." You may ask, not advise. Write the four questions, in order.

1. What do you think ?
2. What have you ?
3. What would look like?
4. What do you need ?
Depois da quarta pergunta: silêncio. É a parte difícil.
Coaching is questions, mentoring is advice, managing is the decision. All three are legitimate — the failure is using one while pretending it is another. A manager who asks four questions and then makes the decision anyway has taught the team that the questions are theatre; a coach who starts advising has stopped coaching, and should say so out loud: "can I put my manager hat on for a second?"

4. Feedback: situation, behaviour, impact

The three columns are the technique. The person never appears as an adjective — no careless, no unprofessional, no attitude — because an identity gets defended and a behaviour can be changed.

SituationBehaviour (observable)Impact (on the work, or on you)
"In Tuesday's client call…""…you interrupted her twice while she was describing the delay…""…and she stopped asking questions after that."
"In yesterday's review…""…the numbers in slide four were from March, not April…""…so we made a decision on stale data and will have to revisit it."
"On Thursday, with the supplier…""…you agreed the price without checking the limit I gave you…""…which puts me above my own authority on this contract."
Notice that every impact is a consequence in the world, not a verdict on the person: she stopped asking questions, we decided on stale data, it puts me above my authority. Nothing in the right-hand column can be argued with, which is exactly why it can be heard — and it is followed by a question, not a conclusion: "what would help?"

A. Six rewrites — the label is forbidden

The label (never say this)Your situation–behaviour–impact

"Your attitude is a problem."
"You're not a team player."
"You're careless with detail."
"You lack ownership."
"You're too slow."
The test before you speak: could a camera see it? If the camera could not record the behaviour, it is an interpretation, and the person will argue with the interpretation rather than change the behaviour — because that is what a person does with a verdict.

5. The conversation nobody wants

Four steps, and the order is the content:
1 · fact plus intent. "You've been on this account for two years and I want you to keep it."
2 · the gap. "What I expected was a weekly update; what I observed in the last six weeks was no update, and two client e-mails asking whether we were still engaged."
3 · the pause. Long enough to be uncomfortable. Say nothing. The other person needs the silence to think in — and if you fill it, you will fill it with an apology.
4 · one observable next behaviour, with a date. "From Monday, one line to the client every Friday by 16:00. We review it on the twenty-first."

6. Announcing change — four lines

Six weeks from now a supplier is replaced, two people report to you from Monday, and nobody knows what happens to their own work. Four lines, and the fourth is the one that buys credibility.

The lineYours
1 · what changes
2 · what does not change
3 · what I do not know yet
4 · when I will tell you
The fourth line is the credibility, not the courtesy. Unspoken uncertainty becomes a rumour; uncertainty with a date becomes a plan. And line two — what does not change — is the one people are actually listening for, because change is only frightening where it is unbounded.

7. The vision, in one sentence

A vision is one sentence because it has to travel without you — repeated in a corridor, by somebody who was not in the room. Three parts: what we are doing, for whom, and what is different when it is done.

1."We will become the leading provider of integrated solutions in our sector." — why does it fail?
2."We close audits in half the time, so mid-sized clients stop fearing them." — why does it work?
3."We deliver excellence through people and process." — this is…
4.A strategy that needs slides is…

B. Write yours

One sentence:
Se não sobrevive a ser repetida por outra pessoa no corredor, não é uma visão.

8. Said — the coaching turn

Four one-line problems from a team member. You may ask; you may not advise; and you must survive three turns without giving a solution. This is the hardest block on the page, because your instinct will arrive in sentence two.

#What they sayYour turn is a question, not a solution
1"The client keeps moving the date and I look stupid."what do you think is happening?
2"I don't have time for the audit and the supplier."what have you tried?
3"I'm not sure I'm the right person for this account."what would good look like?
4"Everyone else seems to manage it and I don't."what do you need from me?
After the fourth question, say nothing. The silence is where the person thinks, and filling it is the difference between coaching and a conversation about your own ideas. If you must speak, ask "what else?" — the only sentence that keeps you out of the way.

A. Record ⏺ — three turns, no advice

Select this line and record ⏺ item 2 above: three questions in a row, then hold the silence. If you give a solution, start again — the recording is the only place you will notice how fast advice arrives.

Why this is the last block and not the first: every other skill here can be prepared alone. This one cannot — it is a decision to be quiet, taken in front of somebody who is waiting for you to talk. The page can only make you practise it; the practice happens in the corridor.
