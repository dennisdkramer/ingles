Until, by, within and ago: the four words for a deadline

A deadline, a duration and a point in the past are three different things

Four words state when something is due, and each one answers a different question. By says the latest moment something may happen. Until says how long something continues. Within gives a period measured from now. Ago places something at a point before now.

The mistake that costs a commitment is until used for by. I need it until Friday means the thing stays with you through Friday; I need it by Friday means Friday is the last acceptable moment. One sentence gives a deadline, the other gives a loan.

Em português até cobre os dois. Em inglês não: by é o prazo, until é a continuação. Diga by Friday quando o outro tem que entregar.

Three shapes on one line

Each figure draws the same line: before on the left, now in the middle, then on the right. What changes is what each word does to that line.

by Friday — the latest moment

until six — how long it continues

two days ago — a point in the past

The bar is the time the waiting takes. The mark is the moment the sentence names. By and until both put a moment on the line, and that is exactly why they are confused: by is a limit on the arrival, until is the end of the stay.

Say the sentence twice with the two words and the meeting changes meaning. I need the deck by Friday: send it on Wednesday, Thursday or Friday, and after Friday you are late. I need it until Friday: keep it with you through Friday, and Friday is when it comes back.

The same contrast works for a person. Stay by me is not English for support; stay with me until six is a stretch of time, and be there by six is one moment.

Pergunta de teste: o outro entrega (by) ou continua (until)? Se entrega, by; se continua, until.

Both sentences are correct English; only one of them is a deadline. The mark shows the one this meeting needed.

The words a deadline is actually made of

A deadline is not one word but a small set of them, and each has a place in the sentence. By takes the day: by Friday. Until takes the moment the stretch ends: until six. Within takes a length: within two days. Ago sits after the length: two days ago — never before.

Ago vem depois do tempo (two days ago), ao contrário de todas as outras palavras de tempo, que vêm antes. É a única que muda de lugar.

On time means at the planned moment; in time means before it is too late. A train is on time. You arrive in time to catch it.

At the latest closes a sentence that already has a deadline: Friday at the latest is Friday and no later. By then refers to a moment someone else named — the review is Thursday, so send it by then.

During and for both sit before a length of time, and they are not the same: during the call is inside the call, for the call is the whole of it. is the verb for doing it all at the end.

A deadline argued out loud, in four lines

Two colleagues settle a deadline, and each sentence uses one of the four words. The client's limit is by; the stretch of time someone can afford is until; the time available from now is within; the work already finished is ago.

The last sentence is the one to copy: by then replaces a whole date that both people already know, so neither has to say it again.

  1. JessThe client needs the deck by Thursday.
  2. BrunoI can work on it until six today, and that is all.
  3. JessThen send what you have within the hour, and I will finish it.
  4. BrunoFine. I sent you the first half two hours ago — use that.

The four examples are the lesson's own sentences read from the other side. Two things to keep:

  • The word goes where the Portuguese puts até, and it is never the same word twice: by for the limit, until for the stretch.
  • Ago is the only one that comes after its length: two days ago, never ago two days.

Em uma frase: o prazo é by; o tempo que continua é until; a janela é within; o ponto no passado é ago, e ele vem depois.
